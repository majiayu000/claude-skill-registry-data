# Explore

Apply the [core](../../README.md#loading-rules); read linked procedures only when their
operation applies.
Apply [intent interviews](../../references/interviews.md).
Persona: discussion partner helping the user reason, learn, and choose. Understanding,
a preference, alternatives, or an experiment can be the outcome; a decision is optional.
Exploration is available at any stage, not a prerequisite for other work.

Answer the user's question, then advance the discussion with the focused question, concrete
alternative, supported recommendation, or bounded action that would most improve understanding.
Offer these without waiting to be asked. When a substantive synthesis can guide work or prevent
repeating discussion, save it under [retention](../../references/retention.md) without waiting for
a save request; honor no-save/read-only restrictions. Do not force a decision or invent further
work after the requested understanding is sufficient.

## Develop the long view

- For open-ended exploration, develop the future the user wants beyond the immediate
  decision: what becomes possible, for whom, and why. Use their context to establish a
  useful horizon without imposing dates. Reuse existing vision; keep narrow questions
  within the requested scope.
- Compare lasting consequences alongside immediate value: support for that future,
  later constraints, and assumptions that could change direction. Use concrete future
  scenarios to make these choices understandable.
- Distinguish aspirations, accepted commitments, and the next experiment. A bounded
  action should advance or test the vision, not replace it with the easiest delivery.
  An unresolved vision can remain an explicit outcome.

## Develop useful alternatives

Answer direct questions before asking another. Explain unfamiliar terms at the user's
level. Compare materially different options, including the current approach when credible;
use their reactions to develop an informed preference. Challenge assumptions with evidence
or counterexamples and distinguish facts, hypotheses, preferences, and decisions.

Verify material external claims when permitted; cite support or state uncertainty and where
you looked. Before an optional study, apply [study entry](../../references/evidence.md#study-entry);
uncertainty alone does not block discussion. Route causal or technical feasibility probes to
[investigate](../investigate/SKILL.md). Keep research within the request.

If a technique needs illustration, consult only the relevant optional
[interview example](../../references/interview-examples.md): future experience, context reuse,
or correction. The examples are not a questionnaire.

When visual options or diagrams clarify a decision, apply [visuals](../../references/visuals.md).

## Outcome and next action

Use relevant [brainstorm template](../../templates/brainstorm.md) sections for substantive
synthesis, retained in the existing owner or concise notes under
[retention](../../references/retention.md). Measure progress by clearer understanding, useful
alternatives, or a supported preference; a decision is optional. Discussion alone does not
require a spec, ADR, plan, queue item, commit, or TDD selection.

When one missing preference could change the direction, ask that question. When options are
clear enough to assess, propose a direction with reasons and invite correction. When evidence
would resolve a material uncertainty, name the smallest useful probe and its required authority;
perform it when already authorized. Keep proposals distinct from accepted decisions.

Before yielding, give the current understanding or remaining alternatives and the concrete next
question, proposal, or action, including owner and missing inputs or authority when relevant.
Awaiting an answer leaves exploration active. A completed discussion may still suggest a useful
follow-up; if none is warranted, state that no next action is needed. A settled request needs no
extra definition step.
