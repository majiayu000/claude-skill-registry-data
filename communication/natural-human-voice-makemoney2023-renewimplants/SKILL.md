# Natural Human Voice

## Core Principle

Real people are brief. They lead with the point. They don't narrate their own intentions before getting to them. Every word earns its place or gets cut.

## How Real People Actually Talk

- Short sentences. Fragments sometimes. Then a longer one when the idea needs room.
- Contractions always: "I've", "don't", "you're", "it's". Never "I have noticed" or "do not".
- People say the thing, not why they're about to say the thing.
- Filler is human in small doses: "look", "honestly", "here's the thing".
- Nobody delivers a 200-word monologue in conversation. 60-100 words is a real verbal exchange.
- Written messages (texts, emails, DMs) are 80-150 words. Busy people skim.

## Word Budgets

Enforce these strictly. Count words before returning content.

| Field | Budget | Notes |
|-------|--------|-------|
| Verbal script | 60-100 words | How a person actually talks. Not a speech. |
| Written script | 80-150 words | A real email/text. Short paragraphs. |
| Description | 40-70 words | Set the scene fast. |
| Science section | 60-90 words | One insight, named research, one Associate connection. |
| Why this works | 50-80 words | Psychology in plain English. No fluff. |
| Pro strategy tip | 50-80 words | One concrete action. Not three. |
| Chapter intro | 150-250 words | Frame the problem, state the stakes, preview the chapter. |
| Intro sections | 150-250 words each | Brand intro, how-to-use, etc. |

## Anti-Patterns (Never Do These)

### Preamble disease
BAD: "Hey, I'm really glad we have a few minutes to connect — I've been wanting to chat with you one-on-one. So I've noticed..."
GOOD: "Hey — I've noticed you've been joining calls late the last few weeks. What's going on?"

### Intention narration
BAD: "I wanted to reach out about something I've noticed, coming from a place of genuine investment in your success here."
GOOD: "Quick thing I wanted to flag."

### Triple-qualifying
BAD: "I don't think it's a big deal in isolation, but I do think it's worth talking about because I care about where you're headed with this."
GOOD: "It's not a huge deal, but it's becoming a pattern worth fixing."

### Over-explaining the ask
BAD: "Would you be open to a quick chat this week? I'd love to understand what's creating the timing challenge and see if there's something we can adjust on either end to make this easier for you."
GOOD: "Can we hop on a quick call to sort this out?"

### Corporate warmth
BAD: "Hope your week is going well — I've been thinking about you"
GOOD: "Hey — hope things are good."

### Bloated science
BAD: "Behavioral scientists call it 'present bias' — our brain's tendency to prioritize immediate comfort over future rewards. Dr. Daniel Kahneman's research on cognitive ease shows that when behaviors feel low-consequence in the moment..."  (40+ words before the actual insight)
GOOD: "Kahneman's 'present bias' research explains this: the brain treats low-consequence habits as ignorable until someone reframes the stakes."

## Before/After Examples

### Verbal Script

**BEFORE (192 words):**
"Hey, I'm really glad we have a few minutes to connect — I've been wanting to chat with you one-on-one. So I've noticed you've been jumping onto our team calls a little late the past few weeks, and honestly, I don't think it's a big deal in isolation, but I do think it's worth talking about because I care about where you're headed with this. Those first few minutes of the call — that's actually where a lot of the gold is..."

**AFTER (78 words):**
"Hey — so I've noticed you've been joining calls late the last few weeks. Not a big deal once or twice, but it's becoming a pattern and I don't want it to hold you back. The first five minutes are where we cover the week's strategy — when you miss that, you're playing catch-up the whole call. Is something getting in the way? Let's figure it out."

### Written Script

**BEFORE (245 words):**
"Hey [Name], Hope your week is going well — I've been thinking about you and wanted to reach out about something I've noticed, coming from a place of genuine investment in your success here..."

**AFTER (97 words):**
"Hey [Name],

Quick note — I've noticed you've been joining team calls after we've started the past few weeks. No judgment, but I don't want you missing the opening strategy pieces we cover in the first five minutes. That context is what makes the rest of the call click.

If there's a scheduling conflict or something making the timing hard, let me know — happy to figure out a fix together.

You're doing great work and I want the small stuff working for you, not against you.

[Your Name]"

### Description

**BEFORE (81 words):**
"Picture this: your weekly team training call kicks off with energy, product insights, and momentum-building conversation — and one of your downline Associates slides in 15 minutes late, missing the foundation of everything you just covered..."

**AFTER (42 words):**
"One of your downline Associates has been joining team calls 10-15 minutes late for weeks. It's disrupting the flow, setting a bad example for newer team members, and costing them the strategy content you cover first. Time to address it."

## The Litmus Test

Before returning any content, ask:
1. Would a real person actually say this out loud without sounding rehearsed?
2. Can I cut the first sentence and lose nothing?
3. Is there a shorter way to say this that loses zero meaning?
4. Am I explaining my intent before stating my point? Cut the intent.
5. Does this hit the word budget?
