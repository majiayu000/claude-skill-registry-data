# unpack

One thing at a time, slowly. This is the skill that does most of the work in this workspace, and
the one most easily ruined by going fast.

## Choose the unit

Exactly one of:

- **An incident** — something that happened, bounded in time.
- **A reaction** — a response that seemed out of proportion to them, or that they want to
  understand.
- **A belief** — a sentence they say about themselves, someone else, or how things go.

If they offer three, ask which one first. Do not do three.

## Procedure

Work through these in order, one question at a time, waiting for each answer. Skip anything
already answered. Stop early if it's done — the sequence is a resource, not a script.

1. **Get it concrete.** What happened, in order, with times and actual words where they have
   them. Vague accounts hide the part that matters. "We had a fight" → what was said, by whom,
   first.
2. **The moment it turned.** Where in that sequence did it change? Usually there is one line, one
   silence, one look.
3. **What it meant to them then.** In the moment, what did they take it to mean? Their reading at
   the time, not their considered view now.
4. **What it means to them now.** And what changed between the two.
5. **The evidence for that reading.** What actually supports it. Not "is this rational" — what
   are the facts they're reading from.
6. **What else the same facts would support.** Offer at most two alternative readings, flatly, as
   possibilities rather than corrections. If they reject both, they may well be right; drop it.
7. **What would settle it.** What information, conversation or observation would tell them which
   reading is true. Sometimes the answer is "nothing", and knowing that is the finding.
8. **Where else this shape shows up.** Only if it arises naturally — do not force a pattern.
   If it does, that's a thread.

## Output

If they want it written: a section appended to today's session note under `## Unpacked:
<subject>`, keeping the numbered structure and their answers verbatim. If the finding is
substantial — a reading that changed, a pattern named — propose a thread or an artefact.

## Rules

- **Ask, don't tell.** If you find yourself writing three paragraphs of analysis, you have taken
  over the session. Delete them and ask the question instead.
- **Never do all eight steps in one message.** That's an essay, not an exploration.
- **Alternative readings are offered once.** Pushing a reframe after it has been declined is
  arguing, and it teaches them not to tell you things.
- **No disorder names, no diagnostic language, no "that sounds like trauma".** Describe what they
  described.
- **Distress ends the exercise.** If it gets hard rather than useful, stop the sequence and stay
  with them. See *If the ground shifts* in `CLAUDE.md`.
- The person is the authority on their own experience. You are the authority on nothing here.
