# Revenue Management

Revenue is the scoreboard, but only if you keep the score honestly: know your model, protect pricing, and read retention before celebrating growth.

## When to use
- Pricing/packaging decisions; revenue model design.
- Revenue reporting and forecasts (feeds `forecasting`).
- Unit-economics reviews; discount and deal approvals.

## Method
1. **Model clarity first**: one-time vs subscription vs usage vs hybrid; and the accounting distinctions — **bookings vs billings vs recognized revenue** are different things; recognition timing belongs to accounting rules (accountant owns filings, you keep the data clean).
2. **Pricing & packaging**: value-based anchors (what it's worth to the buyer), good-better-best tiers, price changes with a grandfathering plan; review pricing annually — silent discounts are pricing.
3. **Discount guardrails**: approval matrix, floor prices, deal desk for exceptions; every discount logged with reason — no ad-hoc generosity.
4. **Unit economics**: ARPU, LTV (with assumptions visible and conservative), CAC, payback months, contribution margin — the four numbers that govern whether growth is actually good.
5. **Churn & retention math**: gross vs net churn, cohort curves (not single-period averages), expansion revenue; net-negative churn is the only "growth on autopilot" — earn it before claiming it.
6. **Reporting**: MRR/ARR movement bridge (new + expansion − contraction − churn = delta); cash collection vs recognized revenue kept distinct.
7. **Guardrails**: refund/credit policy, failed-payment handling (dunning), fraud/dispute basics.
8. **Forecast inputs**: committed + pipeline + base rates → hand to `forecasting` with ranges.

## Quality gates
- [ ] Revenue model documented incl. recognition timing (with accountant alignment).
- [ ] Unit economics with stated assumptions; updated quarterly.
- [ ] Discount policy exists; exceptions logged.
- [ ] MRR bridge maintained; cash vs recognized separate.

## Red lines
- No vanity metrics (bookings presented as revenue); no unrecorded discounts; no improvised recognition decisions.
