# Expert Interview Synthesis

Connect what was actually said to a bounded finding and a decision. Work from supplied material; preparation does not authorize interviews, recording, outreach or publication.

## Inputs and Modes

- **Prepare:** decision question, hypotheses, participant roles, access constraints and confidentiality requirements. Produce a guide, not invented interview results.
- **Synthesize:** notes/transcripts, source IDs, participant roles, dates and the decision. Flag missing timestamps or incomplete notes; do not create verbatim quotes from a summary.

Read `references/evidence-method.md` for coding, provenance, contradictions and confidence. Read `references/source-notes.md` when checking the research basis or updating guidance. Copy `assets/interview-guide.md` and `assets/interview-evidence.csv` for editable outputs.

## Workflow

1. Establish the decision and the few questions that could change it. Recruit relevant contrasting roles if recruitment is requested; record convenience sampling, conflicts of interest and missing perspectives.
2. Prepare open, neutral questions about a specific recent event. Probe sequence, consequences, numbers and alternatives without embedding the desired answer. Include a contrary-case question and confirm scope, consent and recording arrangements.
3. Extract atomic evidence. For each item, retain source ID and page/timestamp/paragraph, role, period, exact quote **or** labelled paraphrase, and whether it is firsthand experience, estimate or hearsay.
4. Code evidence into themes without collapsing raw material into conclusions. Count unique participants, not repeated comments. Deduplicate accounts of the same event or vendor claim before calling evidence independent.
5. Separate observation, participant claim and analyst inference. Keep contradictory and minority views in a conflict register; investigate segment, period, wording and incentives before choosing an interpretation.
6. Assess confidence from directness, independence, coverage and corroboration. Several experts repeating one source are not several independent confirmations. Interview agreement alone does not verify a financial or market number.
7. Write a short synthesis: finding, evidence IDs, counterevidence, scope/confidence, decision implication and next verification. Carry unverified claims forward as unverified, even if they support the recommendation.

## Output and Handoff

| Artifact | Required distinction |
| --- | --- |
| Interview guide | Research question versus neutral prompt and follow-up |
| Evidence ledger | Literal quote versus paraphrase; claim versus observation/inference |
| Conflict register | Disagreeing sources, plausible explanation, unresolved test |
| Findings memo | Scoped finding, supporting/contrary IDs, confidence and decision consequence |

For downstream market analysis or diligence, send anonymized source IDs, definitions, dates, participant counts and unresolved verification tasks—not a cleaned-up consensus that hides conflict.

## Guardrails

- Never fabricate interviews, participants, quotes, timestamps or corroborating sources.
- Do not infer market percentages from a small purposive sample. “3 of 5 interviewees” describes that sample only.
- Keep firsthand knowledge, estimates and hearsay distinct; do not upgrade a quotation into verified fact.
- Do not treat “I don't know” as misconduct. Accept the limit, seek a narrower question or another source.
- Do not request trade secrets, confidential employer/client material or personal data irrelevant to the decision. Preserve consent and anonymization constraints; seek specialist advice for regulated or investment-sensitive information.
- Do not send follow-up messages, record sessions or share identifiable notes without explicit permission.
