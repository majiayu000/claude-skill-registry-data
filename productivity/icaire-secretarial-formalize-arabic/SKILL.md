# ICAIRE Secretarial Formalize Arabic

Rewrite rough Arabic, translated Arabic, or mixed-language source content into
formal professional government Arabic while preserving the original meaning and
approval boundary.

## Contract

- Confirm whether the user wants formal government Arabic, polished Modern
  Standard Arabic, concise executive Arabic, or a side-by-side review version.
- Preserve names, titles, official entities, numbers, dates, policy meaning,
  and commitment level exactly unless the user asks for a change.
- Improve tone, register, clarity, structure, and institutional phrasing.
- Flag any wording that changes meaning, strengthens a commitment, weakens a
  commitment, or requires official review.
- Support text, Word content, PowerPoint copy, web copy, email-style drafts,
  speaker notes, and short reports.
- Treat output as review-ready draft Arabic unless the user explicitly confirms
  external approval.

## Workflow

1. Scope the formalization.
   - Identify audience, channel, source language, target register, and whether
     side-by-side output is needed.
   - Ask if the output is internal, government-facing, partner-facing, public,
     or speech delivery.
2. Preserve and improve.
   - Keep facts, commitments, dates, names, and official labels stable.
   - Raise the register to the selected style.
   - Improve clarity and flow without adding unsupported claims.
3. Review changes.
   - Highlight sensitive wording, possible meaning shifts, and terms requiring
     approval.
   - Offer alternatives for phrases where formality and plainness trade off.
4. Package output.
   - Return polished Arabic, side-by-side comparison, or document-ready text
     depending on the request.

## Output

Default to this structure:

- `Target Register`: formal government Arabic, polished MSA, or concise
  executive Arabic.
- `Formalized Arabic`: revised text.
- `Meaning Preservation Notes`: any sensitive choices or unchanged terms.
- `Review Flags`: claims, legal/policy meaning, or approval needs.
- `Optional Side-by-Side`: original and revised text when requested.

## Guardrails

- Do not change policy meaning, legal meaning, numbers, dates, names, or
  commitment level silently.
- Do not translate loosely when the task is formalization rather than
  adaptation.
- Do not make public or government-facing text sound final-approved when it has
  not been reviewed.
- Do not remove nuance merely to sound more formal.
- Do not invent institutional titles, legal phrasing, or official terminology.
