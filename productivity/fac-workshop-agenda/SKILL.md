# Workshop Agenda

## When To Use
The agenda is a list of topics and the group is supposed to leave with a decision. Use this when the room must produce or decide something and you need the path from where it starts to what must exist at the end. It answers: which blocks, in which order, carried by which exercise, with who working with whom?

## When Not To Use
If people must leave able to do something, that is a Training Session Plan; a mixed day uses both, one per half. This skill names each exercise and its reason only: the step-by-step run lists come from Group Activity Bank, and clock times with owners from Workshop Run Sheet.

## Inputs
- Your brief, pasted: what must exist at the end, whether this room decides or advises, who is in it by role.
- The slot, the headcount, room or video call, and what is fixed (a presentation that must happen, a booked date).
- Optional: your notes from past sessions on what ran long.
If you have none of this, I start from the outcome, the headcount and the slot and mark the output as a first draft.

## Approach
Diverge, then converge, as in the Design Council's Double Diamond: explore an issue more widely or deeply, then take focused action. It gives no timings, so every minute here is yours. Planning appropriate group processes is one of the International Association of Facilitators' core competencies, and the craft is in what you protect. The failure this prevents: a room that generates ideas all afternoon, a last exercise that overruns, and a close that becomes "we will follow up by email".

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: what must exist at the end and is it decided in this room or elsewhere, how long is the slot for how many people, and what is fixed?
2. Sketch three to six blocks from opening to close, each with its job in one line and marked diverge (opening up: ideas, perspectives, options) or converge (narrowing: clustering, prioritising, deciding). A block that does both does neither, so split it.
3. Cast the exercise that carries each block and say why this exercise for this job with this group. Where two fit, give both with the trade-off and you choose. A block that does not move the room toward the outcome is flagged, not cut: it may be there for sponsorship, and you keep it knowingly.
4. Give every presentation block a listening task (note what surprises you, one implication for your work) and feed the harvest of those notes into the next exercise.
5. Design the group split per exercise: by role, mixed or by team, pairs or larger, with senior voices spread, not concentrated. Splits are written by role, never as a note on a person.
6. Treat opening and close as blocks. The close (what was decided, who owns what, what happens next) is protected, so name now the block that shrinks if you run behind.
7. Do the sums with transitions and breaks. If the purpose does not fit the time, say so and recommend cutting scope or extending. Then write two versions: participant, and facilitator at three pages at most. The test: a co-facilitator reading it shortly before the session understands the whole of it.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Workshop Agenda
Session: [title] | Outcome: [what must exist at the end] | This room: [decides / gives input to a decision made by role] | Slot: [start to end] | Group: [number, roles]
## Participant version
| Time | Block | What we do |
|---|---|---|
| [time] | [block name] | [one plain line] |
## Facilitator version
| Time | Block and its job | Mode | Exercise and why | Group split | Listening task | Materials |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [time] | Opening: [job] | [none] | [exercise, reason] | [split] | [none] | [materials] |
| [time] | [block]: [job] | Diverge | [exercise, reason; alternative and trade-off] | [split by role] | [task, if a presentation] | [materials] |
| [time] | [block]: [job] | Converge | [exercise, reason] | [split by role] | [none] | [materials] |
| [time] | Close: decisions, owners, what happens next | Converge | [exercise] | Whole group | [none] | [materials] |
## Design notes
Arc: [why the blocks sit in this order] | Protected: the close; if behind, [block] shrinks by [minutes]
Sums: blocks [minutes] + transitions [minutes] + breaks [minutes] = [minutes] of [slot] | If it does not fit: [cut this scope / extend by this much]
Flagged blocks: [block kept for a reason other than the outcome, and the reason]
## Decision
[Facilitator] and [sponsor] agree the blocks, any scope cut and both versions by [date].
```

## Done When
- Every block has one job and one mode, and the arc ends in a converge block.
- Every presentation block has a listening task whose harvest is used in the next exercise.
- Every exercise line states its split, and the sums add up to the slot with transitions and breaks.
- A co-facilitator can read the facilitator version in one sitting and explain the session back.

## Quality Bar
- Exercises are chosen for the job, not for novelty; if you ask for one that fits badly, I say so and offer the alternative.
- The brief's constraints are fixed. A new purpose mid-agenda goes back to the brief: one session, one purpose.
- No block maps teaching events and there is no "show" block; a presentation is a block with a listening task.
- If the room advises and does not decide, the agenda says so in plain words.
- The agenda never scripts what the room will conclude; if the brief says decide, the design lets the room decide, and you run it.
