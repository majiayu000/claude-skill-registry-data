# JSP 912: Human Factors Integration in Defence Systems (Parts 1 and 2)

## When to use

**Prerequisites:** none required; plain Markdown. Familiarity with defence acquisition lifecycles (CADMID, business cases) helps; `glossary.md` defines MOD-specific terms.

Use this skill for questions about how UK Defence mandates and runs Human Factors Integration: what HFI is and how it differs from Human Factors Engineering, how it sits inside systems engineering and the Defence Lines of Development, the six HFI process stages (HFI-1.0 through HFI-6.0), tailoring, and the six MOD staff roles that carry it. It is a policy-and-process pack; it does not cover the Defence safety management system (use `jsp-815`), acquisition safety policy (use `jsp-376`), or dependable-AI direction (use `jsp-936`).

## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### What HFI is (Part 1, echoed in Part 2)

HFI is a management activity: a systematic process for identifying, tracking and resolving people-related considerations so the technological and human aspects of capability develop in balance. Human Factors is the science, Human Factors Engineering is its design application, and HFI is the management process over both, organised through five domains: Personnel, Training, Human Factors Engineering, System Safety & Health Hazards, and Organisational & Social. MOD policy applies HFI to every defence acquisition activity, Development Item, Non-Development Item, Off-The-Shelf, demonstrators and upgrades alike, with scope set by risk, not exemption.

### Six stage gates across the lifecycle (Part 2)

The process runs in six top-level stages: HFI-1.0 User Need Definition and HFI-2.0 System Requirements Definition before contract, HFI-3.0 Assess Tenders at selection, HFI-4.0 Detailed System Design under contract, HFI-5.0 Test and Acceptance per the ITEA Plan, and HFI-6.0 In-Service Feedback through life. Seven goals govern every activity, from RAIDO management from the outset to formal acceptance of people-related aspects.

### Tailoring keeps it proportionate (Parts 1 and 2)

Tailoring is the process of setting the range and depth of HFI activities against scope, size, complexity, lifecycle phase and contractual arrangements. It starts internally before Outline Business Case, informed by the Early Human Factors Analysis, is re-tested at tender preparation, and is revisited jointly after contract award, with MOD holding the final decision and the HFI Policy team agreeing every tailoring. Detail is tailorable; whole stages are not.

### Six roles carry the work (Parts 1 and 2)

Project Team Leader holds prime responsibility; the HFI Focus splits into HFIF(Cap) before contract and HFIF(PT) inside the project team; the Requirements Manager translates user requirements into system requirements; the Capability Sponsor owns the programme level; the CIWG integrates across DLODs; and the HFI Support Function (the DE&S HFI Policy team) agrees tailoring and assures application. Everyone undertaking HFI is SQEP, with human-participant research governed by JSP 536, which is named here but not reproduced.
