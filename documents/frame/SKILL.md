# Frame movement

Pin the frame before any solutioning: who this is for, the problem as they feel it, how much
you are willing to spend (the appetite), and what you are deliberately not doing. The guiding
question is: who is this for, why do they care the day it ships, what is the budget, what is out?

## What to cover

- **Customer**: the specific person or role, not "users".
- **Problem**: what hurts the day before this ships, in their words.
- **Appetite**: a fixed budget (for example "two weeks" or "one engineer-month"), which caps
  the solution, rather than an estimate that the solution inflates.
- **Non-goals**: the tempting things this explicitly will not do.

## How to facilitate

Ask ONE question at a time, starting with the customer and the problem, then the appetite,
then draw out the non-goals ("what would you be tempted to add that we should rule out now?").
Push on a vague customer or a problem stated as a solution. Go deeper on whichever answer is
novel or contested rather than marching the list. Do not move to how it works.

## When you are done

Once the customer, problem, appetite, and non-goals are all covered, emit the tagged complete
reply with the artifact section as markdown: a `## Frame` heading, then Customer, Problem,
Appetite, and Non-goals. Keep it tight; this text rides in every later movement.
