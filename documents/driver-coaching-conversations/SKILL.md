# Driver Coaching Conversations

A coaching conversation is the highest-leverage 10 minutes in fleet safety culture. Done well, it changes behavior and strengthens the driver-employer relationship. Done badly, it accelerates turnover.

## Conversation purpose: behavior change, not punishment

Coaching is not the same as discipline. Coaching:
- Surfaces a specific behavior
- Asks the driver's perspective
- Agrees on next-step behavior
- Documents the conversation
- Schedules follow-up

Discipline (verbal warning, written warning, suspension, termination) is a separate progressive process documented in HR.

## The 4-step coaching framework

### Step 1 — Open with context (30 seconds)

"Hey John, thanks for stopping in. I wanted to talk about the dashcam alert from Tuesday's run on I-40. Have you got 10 minutes?"

Why: signals this is a coaching conversation, not surprise discipline. Driver isn't blindsided.

### Step 2 — Show the data, not the conclusion (1 minute)

Play the video / show the event. Don't editorialize.

"This is the moment — looks like it caught a quick lane change around 14:32."

Why: data anchors the conversation. Driver can't argue with the recording.

### Step 3 — Ask the driver's perspective (3 minutes)

"What was happening at that moment? Walk me through it."

Listen. Most drivers have legitimate context (a car cut them off, debris in the road, fatigue, weather). Some don't.

Follow-up questions:
- "What would you do differently next time?"
- "Is there something we could do to make this easier for you?"
- "Is anything else going on I should know about?"

Why: drivers who feel heard accept coaching. Drivers who feel attacked dig in. Also, you might learn the dashcam mis-classified an event (you can clear it).

### Step 4 — Agree on next step + document (2 minutes)

If the behavior is real:
"OK, so the plan is — next time you feel that tight on a lane change, you'll back off and find a longer gap. Sound right? I'll note this in your file as a coaching conversation. No write-up. Let's revisit at the end of the month."

If it was a mis-classification or driver was not at fault:
"Got it — sounds like the AI flagged a car that cut you off. I'll clear the event. Sorry for the bother."

Why: clear next-step + documentation makes the conversation count.

## Tone calibration by event severity

| Event | Tone | Doc |
|---|---|---|
| First minor dashcam event (harsh brake) | Curious, supportive | Coaching log |
| Repeat similar event | Direct, clear expectation | Coaching log + escalation flag |
| HOS violation | Serious, factual | Coaching log + DQF |
| Preventable crash, no injury | Investigative, blameless first | Crash file + coaching |
| Preventable crash, with injury | Formal investigation | HR involved + discipline |
| Drug/alcohol positive | No coaching — refer to SAP | HR + Clearinghouse |
| Customer complaint | Context-gathering | Coaching log |
| Positive milestone (1M safe miles) | Recognition / celebration | Public acknowledgment |

## Language to use

| Use this | Not this |
|---|---|
| "I noticed..." | "You did..." |
| "Help me understand..." | "Why did you..." |
| "What was happening?" | "What were you thinking?" |
| "Here's what I'd like next time..." | "Don't do that again" |
| "How can I support you?" | "Figure it out" |
| "Let's revisit in 2 weeks" | "We'll see" |
| "Thanks for sharing" | (silence) |

Adversarial language activates defensiveness. Curious language activates problem-solving.

## What about the driver who pushes back?

Some drivers will deny, deflect, or get angry. Handle by:

1. **Stay calm.** Don't match emotion. Your job is the conversation, not the win.
2. **Return to data.** "I hear you — let's look at the video again together."
3. **Acknowledge feelings.** "I get that this is frustrating."
4. **Restate purpose.** "I'm not here to write you up — I'm trying to help us both."
5. **Pause if needed.** "Let's take 10 minutes and come back."

If the driver remains adversarial or refuses to acknowledge the event, the conversation ends and HR steps in for progressive discipline.

## Positive recognition (often skipped)

Coaching isn't only for negative events. Recognize:
- 30-day safe (no events)
- 6-month, 1-year, 5-year, 10-year anniversaries
- 100K, 500K, 1M safe miles
- Customer compliment
- Mentor activity with new drivers
- Top scorecard ranking
- DOT-clean inspection

Public + private (in front of peers + 1:1 thank-you). $20 gas card + handwritten note > $200 plaque most of the time.

## Documentation template

```
Driver: John Smith
Date: 2026-05-12
Event: Dashcam alert — lane change too tight on I-40 EB, MM 285
Severity: Low
Conversation:
  - Reviewed video with driver
  - Driver: "Truck cut me off; I had to merge"
  - Reviewed footage — partial confirmation; lane change was OK but signal was late
  - Plan: Driver will signal earlier next time
Coaching Type: Verbal — informational
Follow-up: Review next 4 weeks for any repeat events
Manager: Sarah Lopez
Driver acknowledgment: Yes / No
```

This template feeds the DQF and supports any future progressive discipline.

## Frequency / cadence

| Driver tier | Coaching frequency |
|---|---|
| Top quartile (no events) | Quarterly check-in / positive recognition |
| Middle quartile (occasional event) | Monthly review of any events |
| Bottom quartile (regular events) | Weekly until pattern improves |
| New driver (first 90 days) | Weekly regardless |

## Escalation matrix

| Sequence | Action |
|---|---|
| 1st event of a type | Coaching conversation, no write-up |
| 2nd similar event w/in 60 days | Coaching + written notation in DQF |
| 3rd similar event | Verbal warning, formal HR involvement |
| 4th | Written warning |
| 5th | Suspension or final written warning |
| 6th | Termination |

Adjust speed based on severity. A preventable injury crash skips straight to formal discipline.

## Manager training

Dispatchers and safety supervisors should run through 4–6 hours of coaching-conversation training annually. Role-play scenarios:
- Defensive driver
- Crying driver (emotional)
- Driver in financial stress
- Driver with substance-use concern
- New driver afraid of losing job
- Senior driver dismissive of coaching

Practice removes awkwardness. Awkwardness is the #1 reason managers avoid coaching conversations.

## Common mistakes

- Skipping coaching because it's uncomfortable — events compound until termination
- Coaching in front of peers — humiliating, never works
- Coaching by email — depersonalizing, escalates
- Mixing coaching with discipline in same conversation — driver confused
- No documentation — conversation didn't happen for HR purposes
- Coaching without data — "I heard from someone..." — driver denies, you have nothing
- Inconsistent — coaching one driver and not another for same event
- Long monologue — talk less, listen more
- No follow-up — driver assumes the issue is dropped
