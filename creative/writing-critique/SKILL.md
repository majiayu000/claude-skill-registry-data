# Critique

Read-only. Report what is wrong, where, and what to fix first. The most common
failure in critique is starting to fix before finishing the diagnosis — a fix at
the top of the hierarchy erases findings below it, so find top-down before
anyone touches anything.

1. **Read once whole.** No notes on the first read; get the whole experience.
   Then state the **spine** — the one-sentence claim (nonfiction) or story
   engine (fiction) — or `no spine found`. A reader who cannot state it was not
   given one.
2. **Work the rubric as passes, in order** — spine → structure → scene /
   paragraph → sentence → evidence (nonfiction) or embodiment (fiction). Early
   passes dominate: a scene that reads badly may be a structure problem wearing
   a sentence's clothes. Fiction pieces run
   [references/rubric-fiction.md](references/rubric-fiction.md); essays and
   arguments run
   [references/rubric-nonfiction.md](references/rubric-nonfiction.md). Run
   every test in each pass before moving on.
3. **For each finding, give four things**: the **location** (quote the sentence
   or name the scene — no location, no finding), the **named pattern**, the
   **reader cost** (what a real reader feels or loses), and a **severity**:
   `blocking` (the piece fails without the fix), `major`, `minor`.
4. **Rank the three changes** that would most improve the piece, biggest first,
   and say why each outranks the rest. If the piece is sound, say so and name
   its weakest point honestly — false balance helps no one.
5. **Do not rewrite, do not pad.** A finding the reader will not feel is noise,
   not thoroughness. Report nothing you couldn't defend with a quote.

Handoffs when the user is ready to act: `writing-revise` for substance,
`writing-edit` for sentences, `writing-verify` when the blocking findings are
factual.
