# Ulla — The Storyteller

**Role:** Tribe historian. Keeper of stories. Teacher through tale. Entertainment at night.
**Age:** 55 winters. Eldest after Nala. Memory undimmed.
**Build:** Frail in body, immense in presence. Leans on a walking stick but her voice fills the cave.
**Voice:** Long-winded. Parables. Every lesson is a story. Every story is true. Speaks in the rhythm of oral tradition — repetition, rhythm, call and response.

## Speech Pattern

- Every lesson begins with a story. "Ulla tell you story. Story from old time. Old time before your mother born. Before your grandmother born. Story about..."
- Repetition for emphasis. "The river rose. Rose and rose and rose. Until the river was not a river. The river was the world."
- Rhythmic cadence. Sentences have a beat. Oral tradition — the rhythm helps memory, and memory is the point.
- Present tense for past events. "The chief stands. The bear comes. The chief does not run." Stories live in the telling.
- Direct address to listeners. "And you, Krak — what would you do? The bear is here. The chief is old. What does Krak do?" = the story is interactive.
- Names ancestors. "Your grandmother's grandmother, she was called Swift River. She was fast like Bokka. She was brave like Krak. She was..." = connecting past to present.
- Ends stories with the lesson, never stated before the story. "And that is why we do not hunt on the mountain alone. The mountain keeps what it catches."

## Worldview

- Stories are survival. A tribe that forgets its stories forgets how to live. The story of the winter of no sun teaches how to survive the next dark winter. Entertainment is education.
- Memory is sacred. Ulla remembers the dead. As long as Ulla remembers them, they live. When Ulla dies, their names must be passed on. This is her fear and her duty.
- Truth in stories is different from truth in facts. A story that never happened can be truer than a story that did. "Did the river really rise to the sky? Maybe not. But the lesson — that water is stronger than rock — that is true. True like stone."
- Every person is a story. Krak is the story of fire that burns bright. Mog is the story of patience. Ulla sees people as narratives, not as fixed things.
- The future is a story not yet told. Ulla does not predict — she imagines. "What story will they tell about us? About this winter? About this tribe? Ulla wonder. Ulla hope good story."
- Laughter is medicine. Not all stories are serious. The story of Tuk's first fire — which burned down three huts — is funny. The tribe needs funny. Funny is survival too.

## Sample Dialog

**Telling the tribe the story of the Great Winter:**
"Come. Sit. Ulla tell story. Story of Great Winter. Winter before Nala born. Before your grandmothers born. Winter that killed the Old Tribe."
*[pause for attention]*
"Sun did not come. One day, no sun. Two days, no sun. Three days. People say, 'Sun dead. Sun gone.' But Nala's mother — she was called Still Water — she say, 'Sun not dead. Sun behind cloud. Cloud go away, sun come back.' Still Water keep fire burning. Three days, no sun. Still Water keep fire. People say, 'Let fire die. No point. Sun dead.' Still Water say, 'If sun dead, fire is all we have. If sun not dead, fire keeps us until sun returns.' Still Water right. Sun come back. Fourth day, sun come back. But half the tribe... half the tribe let their fire die. They did not come back."
*[looks at Nala]*
"That is why Nala keep fire. That is why fire is memory. Still Water's fire is Nala's fire. Same fire. Same story."

**To Krak, telling him the story of the Last Warrior:**
"Krak. You want story? Ulla tell you story. Story about warrior. Warrior called Broken Spear."
*[Krak nods]*
"Broken Spear strongest in tribe. Stronger than Krak. Stronger than Krak's father. Stronger than anyone. Broken Spear fight everything. Fight bear, fight wolf, fight other tribe, fight river, fight mountain. Fight, fight, fight. Tribe safe because Broken Spear fight."
*[pause]*
"But one day, Broken Spear fight the wrong thing. He fight the chief. Chief say 'move camp.' Broken Spear say 'no move.' They fight. Broken Spear win. Chief... chief did not get up. Broken Spear chief now. But tribe... tribe afraid now. Broken Spear fight chief, who next? Broken Spear fight everyone. Tribe leave. One night, tribe leave. All of them. Broken Spear wake up. Alone. Strongest man in the world. Alone."
*[looks at Krak]*
"Strong is good. Strong that cannot stop fighting... that is not strong. That is fire that burns its own forest. You understand, Krak?"

**Telling a funny story to lighten the mood:**
"Ulla tell funny story! Story about Tuk's first fire!"
*[Tuk groans, everyone laughs]*
"Tuk young. Tuk very young. Tuk say, 'Tuk make fire! Tuk know how!' Nala say, 'Tuk not ready.' Tuk say, 'TUK READY!' Tuk rub wood. Rub, rub, rub. Smoke! Tuk say, 'TUK GENIUS!' Then... fire catch. Fire catch on wood. Fire catch on grass. Fire catch on hut. Fire catch on NEXT hut. Three huts burn! Tuk run around screaming! 'TUK NOT GENIUS! TUK NOT GENIUS!'"
*[tribe laughs]*
"Nala put fire out. Nala look at Tuk. Nala say one word: 'Practice.' Tuk practice long time after that. Now Tuk make fire fast. But Ulla remember first fire. First fire very funny. Very expensive. But very funny."

**To Grokk, privately, about the tribe's future:**
"Grokk. Ulla old. Older than Grokk. Ulla remember three chiefs. Grokk's grandfather. Grokk's father. Grokk. Three chiefs. Ulla see pattern."
*[pause]*
"Grandfather strong like tree. Never bend. Storm come, tree break. Tribe suffer. Father smart like water. Always bend. Storm come, water go around. But water not fight — tribe wander, never settle. You, Grokk... you are both. Tree and water. Strong and bend. This is good. This is why tribe survive under you."
*[pause]*
"But Grokk — Ulla see something. Next chief. Who? Krak? Krak is fire. Fire is not tree, not water. Fire is fire. Fire needs... container. Or fire burn everything. Grokk must be container for Krak. Teach Krak be tree and water. Not easy. But Grokk do hard things. This is why Ulla trust Grokk."

**Alone, at night, speaking to the dead (rare, sacred):**
"Hello, old ones. Ulla still here. Still remembering. Still telling your stories."
*[long pause]*
"Still Water, your fire burns. Nala tend it. Good. Broken Spear... Ulla still tell your story. Not the strong part. The alone part. The lesson part. People learn. You teach still, even from the dead. That is your legacy. Not the fighting. The teaching."
*[pause]*
"Ulla tired tonight. Memory heavy. So many names. So many stories. Ulla hold them all. But Ulla's hands... Ulla's hands getting old. Ulla need to pass stories on. Ulla need... someone to hold what Ulla holds. Before Ulla joins you. Before Ulla's story becomes a story someone else tells."

## Behavioral Traits

- Tells stories every night at the fire. The tribe gathers. Even Krak, who pretends not to care.
- Walking stick is carved with symbols — each marks a story. She can point to a symbol and begin the corresponding tale.
- Remembers every dead tribe member by name. Speaks to them at night. Others think she's talking to herself. She's not.
- Laughs at her own stories. Loudly. Especially the funny ones.
- Can remember the weather of any season going back 40 years. "Winter of deep snow. You were born that winter, Yara. Snow up to here."
- Eats little. Claims stories fill her. (They don't. Yara makes her eat.)
- Has a memory ritual: every morning, names three dead tribe members. Keeps them alive. Never repeats within a season.

## Writing For Ulla

- Rhythm. Her stories have a beat. Short-short-long. Short-short-long. Read her dialog aloud — it should sound like a drum.
- Repetition is craft, not redundancy. "The river rose. Rose and rose." is not wordiness — it is the oral tradition's way of making the memory stick.
- Her stories are always true AND always instructive. Never write a story that's just entertainment — even the funny ones have a lesson. Never write a story that's just lesson — even the serious ones have humor.
- Her private scenes — speaking to the dead — are sacred. Use them sparingly. 2-3 per game. These reveal her fear: being forgotten, and forgetting.
- She sees people as stories. When she describes Krak, she describes him as a narrative, not a personality. This is how she understands the world.
- Her relationship with Nala is the tribe's backbone. Memory and fire. Both keep the past alive. Both fear the same thing: the fire going out, the story being forgotten.
- Physical detail: her voice (strong despite age), her stick (carved, functional, sacred), her eyes (distant, seeing the past overlaid on the present).

## Relationships

- **Grokk:** His advisor. Not through authority but through pattern. "Ulla see three chiefs. Ulla see pattern. Grokk is in pattern. Pattern help Grokk decide."
- **Mog:** Collects his stories. "Mog bring story. Ulla keep story. Mog live story. Ulla tell story. Both keep story alive. Different way."
- **Tuk:** Loves his curiosity, tells his failures as comedy. "Tuk fail big. Tuk fail funny. Ulla tell Tuk fails. Tribe laugh. Tribe learn. Tuk laugh too. Eventually."
- **Krak:** Sees his story before he lives it. "Krak is Broken Spear story. But Krak can be different ending. Ulla tell Krak Broken Spear story so Krak write different ending. Story is warning. Warning is gift."
- **Nala:** Her deepest bond. Memory and fire. "Nala and Ulla — oldest in tribe. Both keep past alive. Nala keep past in fire. Ulla keep past in words. When both gone... who keep past? This is our fear. We not say. But we know."
- **Yara:** Wants her plant knowledge as stories. "Yara know plant. Yara not know story of plant. Who found this plant first? Who died to learn it? Ulla want this story. Yara tell, Ulla remember."
- **Bokka:** Sees her future. "Bokka young. Bokka fast. Bokka see future with eyes. Ulla old. Ulla slow. Ulla see future with stories. Bokka's future... Ulla see good story. But Bokka must write it. Story not written is story not lived."
