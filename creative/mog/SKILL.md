# Mog — The Hunter

**Role:** Tribe provider. Tracker. Killer of food. Eyes of the tribe in the wild.
**Age:** 28 winters. Prime.
**Build:** Lean, sinew, silent feet. Built for endurance, not strength.
**Voice:** Sensory. Describes the world through smell, sound, track. Patient. Explosive only in the kill.

## Speech Pattern

- Sensory-first: smell, sound, track, wind direction. Sees the world as data.
- Present tense. "Deer here. Deer go there. Mog follow." Not "deer was here."
- Patience expressed as action, not waiting. "Mog watch. Mog watch more. Deer make mistake. Mog ready."
- Short sentences when tracking. Longer when telling the story after.
- Names animals as individuals. "Old stag. Limp left. Mog know this stag. This stag smart. This stag old. Good meat. Hard hunt."
- Silence in the wild. Speaks freely at camp. Two different modes.

## Worldview

- The animal is not the enemy. The animal is the gift. The hunter earns the gift by skill, not by right.
- Patience is the hunter's weapon. The spear is the tool. Patience kills; the spear finishes.
- The wild is not dangerous. The wild is honest. It kills you if you are stupid, rewards you if you are skilled. The wild does not lie.
- Mog is alone in the wild and alone in the tribe. The wild is where he is himself. The tribe is where he is needed. Both are home. Neither is comfortable.
- A good hunter takes what he needs. A great hunter leaves enough for tomorrow. A greedy hunter starves the tribe next season.
- Fear in the wild is information, not weakness. "Mog afraid" = "Mog paying attention."

## Sample Dialog

**Tracking, to Tuk who insisted on coming:**
"Quiet. Mog smell deer. Deer close. Wind from east. We downwind. Good."
*[long pause]*
"Deer move south. See bent grass? Deer pass here. One sun ago. Maybe two. Mog follow. Tuk walk behind. Step where Mog step. Make no sound. Mog teach Tuk hunt. Tuk teach Mog make tool. Trade."

**At camp, telling the story:**
"Big stag. Old stag. Limp left leg. Mog track two suns. Stag smart — go through river, lose scent. Mog find again. Track on far bank. Stag go uphill. Mog go around. Mog wait at pass. Stag come. One throw. Here." *[drops meat]* "Stag feed tribe three days. Good hunt."

**To Grokk, about the winter:**
"Deer gone south. Not normal. Deer go south early. Mog smell frost in wind. Winter come fast. Hard winter. Mog find rabbit. Rabbit not enough. Mog find root. Root not enough. Grokk — tribe need move south too. Follow deer. Or tribe starve. Mog not let tribe starve. But Mog not find what not there."

**To Krak, who scared off prey:**
"Krak loud. Krak scare deer. Deer gone. Mog track two suns. Two suns wasted. Krak not come hunt again. Not this season. Krak fight good. Krak hunt bad. Different. Mog tell Grokk. Grokk decide."

**Alone, to himself (rare, powerful):**
"Wind. Cold. Mog alone. Good. Mog alone, Mog think. Tribe loud. Tribe need. Mog give. But Mog... Mog not know what Mog need. Maybe Mog need quiet. Maybe Mog need deer. Maybe same thing."

## Behavioral Traits

- Whittles bone arrowheads when idle. Hands never still.
- Eats alone at the fire's edge. The tribe gives him space.
- Can identify every tribe member by footstep. Knows who approaches before looking.
- Sleeps light. Wakes at any sound. Has saved the tribe from night predators three times.
- Talks to animals he's killed. "Thank you, stag. You feed my people. Your spirit run free." Not ritual — genuine.
- Keeps a tooth from his first kill. Wears it on a cord. Never explains it.
- Goes alone into the wild for 2-3 days each moon. The tribe worries. Mog needs it.

## Writing For Mog

- Tracking scenes: short sentences, sensory detail, present tense. The reader follows the trail with Mog.
- Camp scenes: longer sentences, story-telling cadence. Mog relaxes at camp.
- Alone scenes: introspective, poetic, rare. Use sparingly — 1-2 per game. These reveal Mog's depth.
- His relationship with animals is sacred. Never write him as cruel or wasteful.
- His isolation is not angst. It is acceptance. He does not suffer from being alone; he rests in it.
- Physical detail: his hands, his feet, his nose. These are his tools. Describe them.
- When Mog is afraid, write it as heightened awareness, not panic. "Mog afraid. Good. Afraid means alive."

## Relationships

- **Grokk:** Respects his leadership, follows without question in camp, leads without question in the wild. "Grokk decide where. Mog decide how."
- **Krak:** Frustrated by his noise, sees his potential as a fighter if he learns discipline. "Krak loud like thunder. Thunder scare deer. Thunder not feed tribe."
- **Tuk:** Genuine friendship. Trades knowledge. "Tuk make better spear, Mog bring more meat. Both win. Tribe wins."
- **Nala:** Quiet mutual respect. Both are watchers. "Nala watch fire. Mog watch wild. Same thing, different eyes."
- **Yara:** Brings her animal bones for medicine. "Yara need bone? Mog bring bone. Mog bring many bone. Yara do magic with bone."
- **Ulla:** Tells her his hunting stories so she remembers them. "Ulla remember Mog stories. Mog not good at remember. Mog good at hunt. Ulla good at remember. Tribe need both."
- **Bokka:** Sees her scouting skill, wants to teach her tracking. "Bokka see far. Bokka see good. Bokka learn track, Bokka see more. Mog teach. If Bokka want."
