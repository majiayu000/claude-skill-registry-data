## Quick Orientation

The entire universe Alex is asking about shares one spine: **music as resistance**. Woody Guthrie writing anti-fascist songs in the Depression. Bob Dylan electrifying folk into protest rock. Jamaican Rastas using reggae to fight Babylon. UK punks in 1977 channeling that same rage against Thatcher's Britain. The Clash literally covering reggae and bringing both worlds together. Dub producers rewiring the studio as an instrument. Dubstep emerging from South London's multicultural grime scene. It's all one continuous thread.

## Waku's Take

- The Clash are the definitive example of punk and reggae fusing — not as a novelty, but as a natural expression of multicultural working-class Britain
- Woody Guthrie is punk before punk existed — DIY, anti-authority, anti-corporate, for the people
- Dub is one of the most radical innovations in recorded music history — King Tubby and Lee "Scratch" Perry invented remix culture
- Dubstep's roots (pre-2010 Burial, Digital Mystikz, Skream) are deeply dub-influenced and often overlooked in favor of the brostep commercial version
- Bob Dylan "going electric" at Newport 1965 is the original genre betrayal controversy — and he was right to do it
