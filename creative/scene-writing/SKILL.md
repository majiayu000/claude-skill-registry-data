# Scene Writing

Use when writing a dramatic scene for video, game, film-like sequence, audio, or another scripted narrative format.

## Procedure
1. Define where the scene begins in story state, what each principal character wants now, what prevents easy success, and what must be different by the end.
2. Enter as late as practical, after setup that the audience can infer, and establish location or situation through action rather than explanatory dialogue where possible.
3. Build conflict from incompatible goals, information, pressure, or values instead of arbitrary hostility.
4. Let physical action, environment, silence, and reaction carry information that does not need to be spoken.
5. Write dialogue from character intention and subtext while respecting knowledge, relationship, and voice.
6. Escalate or complicate the scene through discoveries, choices, reversals, or consequences that alter the characters' options.
7. End on a turn, decision, reveal, failure, commitment, or emotional shift that changes what follows.
8. Read the scene in sequence with adjacent material for pacing, continuity, redundancy, and whether the same story job is already performed elsewhere.

## Decision rules
- A scene should change story state, character state, or audience understanding.
- Conflict can be quiet and relational; it does not require shouting.
- Visual action should not be narrated redundantly.
- Exposition works best when a character has a reason to communicate it now.

## Quality gate
The scene is ready when character goals and conflict are active, the scene changes something consequential, dialogue and action carry distinct information, continuity is intact, and removing the scene would create a meaningful hole in the larger narrative.
