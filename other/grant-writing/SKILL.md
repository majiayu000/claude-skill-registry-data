# Grant writing — competitive research & career-development awards

Goal: help build a proposal that is **responsive to the funder**, **rigorous**, and
**compelling** — one that survives a fast screening read and rewards a deep one.

## Workflow (in this order)

1. **Read `LEARNINGS.md` beside this skill, when your copy keeps one,** for
   funder-specific rules and what reviewers rewarded/penalized in prior rounds.
2. **Audit responsiveness before touching prose** — pull the funder's required-components
   list + review criteria and walk the draft element-by-element (method below).
3. **Fix gaps in priority order** — focus-area alignment first, then down by impact,
   with surgical edits confined to the affected section.
4. **Draft/revise** using the component-craft sections below; for voice, pair with a
   scholarly-voice/academic-writing skill if you keep one (this install's: `LOCAL.md`
   beside this skill, when your copy keeps one — *Workflow step 4*); defer to
   [research-methods](../research-methods/SKILL.md) for study design and human subjects.
5. **Final pass** — run the "Before you call this done" checklist, then record what this
   round taught in LEARNINGS.md.

## The first move is always responsiveness, not prose

Before drafting or polishing anything, **audit the draft element-by-element against the
funder's published required-components list and review criteria.** A beautifully written
proposal that is *out of scope* for the funder loses. Name the gaps by category first,
then fix in priority order.

The single highest-leverage check: **does the proposal center what the funder funds?**

- WTG Scholars funds **programs, policies, or practices that *reduce* inequality** in
  youth outcomes (ages 5–25). It explicitly does **not** fund studies of the *causes,
  extent, or consequences* of inequality, or that merely *describe* it.
- The most common fatal flaw found in review: the plan centers the background problem
  as a *cause* of disparities (out of scope) instead of the *practice under study* that
  reduces a named disparity. The fix is to **name the practice up front** and recast the
  rest as rationale. (This install's worked case: `LOCAL.md` beside this skill, when your
  copy keeps one — *Fatal-flaw worked case*.)

Generalize: for any funder, identify the **unit they fund** (a practice / intervention /
mechanism / discovery) and make sure that unit — not the background problem — is the
subject of your Significance and aims.

## Responsiveness-review method (reusable)

1. Pull the funder's required-components list + review criteria verbatim.
2. Walk the draft section by section; for each required element, mark present / weak /
   missing and **why**.
3. Group gaps by category. The categories that recur in research-plan reviews:
   - **Focus-area alignment** — centering a cause/consequence instead of a fundable practice *(most critical)*.
   - **Outcome not specified** — no named, literature-evidenced outcome inequality with its causal mechanism.
   - **No standalone Human Subjects Protection section** — IRB / data security / confidentiality / risk / mitigation scattered instead of dedicated.
   - **Analysis plan underspecified** — methods listed but no analysis/interpretation framework (a list of data sources is *not* an analysis plan).
   - **Generalizability & power not addressed** — single-site transferability and statistical power unstated.
   - **Policy/practice relevance implicit** — no "so what beyond this site."
   - **Bibliography / figures / appendices incomplete** — "Figure XX" placeholders; bibliography embedded in body instead of a separate section.
4. Mentoring-plan gaps that recur: a year/stretch-area with **no named mentor**; **no
   contingency** if a mentor leaves; mentor willingness to file **annual reports** not
   confirmed.
5. Don't forget **formatting compliance** (font, spacing, margins, page numbers, page
   limits) — it's a real screen.
6. **Fix the most critical gap first**, with surgical tracked changes confined to the
   affected section, then work down by impact.

## Anatomy of the components

| Component | Its job |
| --- | --- |
| **Abstract / overview** | A *screening* document — senior staff and the full committee read it; only a couple read the full app. Land problem + timeliness + funder-fit fast. |
| **Research plan** | Name the practice + the specific outcome inequality; significance & focus-area alignment; aims as a logic chain; methods + analysis plan; standalone human-subjects section; feasibility; generalizability/power; policy relevance; separate bibliography + figures/appendices. |
| **Mentoring / career plan** | A multi-year plan to grow the scholar into *new* expertise — named mentor per stretch area, real meeting cadence, barriers mitigated + contingency, scholar agency, confirmed mentor commitments. |
| **Budget justification** | Every line tied to a research duty; community-partner & participant costs as explicit line items; real fringe rates; honest logistics. |
| **Letters / biosketch** | Often due *earlier* than the plan; mentors confirm willingness incl. reporting. |

## Research-plan craft

- **Frame the practice, not the problem.** Open Significance by naming the
  practice(s) under study and the population/inequality dimension; recast the
  background literature as *rationale*, not the subject.
- **Pivot to the evidentiary gap.** "The literature documents the inequality but has
  not tested whether [practice] reduces it" — then cite each practice's evidence base
  and name the precise untested gap.
- **Map aims to the funder's review verbs** as a logic chain (e.g. build → test →
  increase understanding): diagnose/co-produce → co-develop/model → pilot/feasibility.
  Justify the *type* of study ("feasibility studies are indicated when no prior
  evaluation exists and prior work wasn't community-partnered").
- **One specific, defensible innovation "first"** — not "first study of X" but the
  first to combine *these methods* on *this problem* for *this population*.
- **Feasibility = anticipate failure in-line.** Give each aim a "Considerations &
  Alternative Strategies" block. The ranked-list contingency is elegant: if the
  top-ranked strategy is unfeasible, the ranked list supplies the next candidate
  without restarting the research.
- **Significance = local case as national exemplar, with concrete numbers.** Not
  "our city faces environmental challenges" but the specific, vivid fact. State the
  funder-relevant irony plainly. (This install's original example: `LOCAL.md` beside
  this skill, when your copy keeps one — *Significance example*.)
- **Conceptual model** belongs to research-methods (see that skill) but in the proposal
  it must be layered, mechanistic, with the **structural cause upstream** and
  **intersectionality as a cross-cutting lens, not a box**.

## Mentoring / career-plan craft

The plan is judged on whether the award **adds value beyond an ordinary collegial
relationship.** Assert (and design toward) these eight points:

1. Sustained, **structured** multi-year mentorship with accountability.
2. **Protected time** for risky, innovative work the award enables.
3. **Cross-disciplinary** bridge-building (mentors outside the scholar's field).
4. Access to a **cohort community** (horizontal peer learning).
5. **Career shaping**, not just project advising — trajectory and research identity.
6. **Legitimacy & visibility** (invitations, editorial boards, collaborations).
7. **Resources tied to the relationship** (mentor co-investment).
8. Explicit shared focus on **reducing inequality**.

Per-mentor structure that works: (1) current relationship history → (2) how the award
changes it → (3) specific activities + time commitment → (4) barriers mitigated. Pair
**each stretch area with a named mentor** (leave no year uncovered). Lead with the
**scholar's agency**; quantify the *increase* the award enables ("from once a semester to
recurring biweekly structured meetings"); state a real cadence with a phased transition
("monthly during planning … as needed once implementation is underway"); name barriers
and how they're solved **plus a contingency**; use specific mechanisms (named lab,
curated readings, hands-on facilitation), not platitudes; confirm mentor commitments
including annual reports.

## Budget-justification craft

- Tie every cost to a named research duty (e.g. "1.5 months summer salary" for specific
  responsibilities).
- Make **community-partner and participant costs explicit line items** — a named
  subcontract, a separate participant/honoraria line — never buried in "Other Direct
  Costs." Separate participant honoraria from space/materials: signals participation is
  *compensated work* (CBPR power-sharing).
- Use **real institutional fringe/payroll rates**; don't round or guess.
- Keep logistics honest and modest; the narrative must defend the *scale* of the
  partnership against the dollars.

## Do's & don'ts

**Do:** audit responsiveness before prose; center the fundable unit; name the specific
outcome inequality with mechanism; give every data source an analysis plan; a standalone
human-subjects section; state generalizability/power and policy relevance; concrete
numbers over abstractions; named mentor for every year; explicit community/participant
budget lines; check the exact formatting spec.

**Don't:** center causes/consequences when the funder funds practices; frame the
population as "vulnerable/at-risk" instead of knowledge holders; claim CBPR/YPAR without
power-sharing language *in every section*; leave the partner vague ("we'll work with
community partners"); call a list of data sources an analysis plan; leave figure/
bibliography placeholders; over-promise scale (a feasibility study's strength is rigor
about what isn't yet known).

## Voice

Grant prose is scholarly but active and confident. For register, word choice, and
positive reframing, pair with a scholarly-voice/academic-writing skill if you keep one
(this install's: `LOCAL.md` beside this skill, when your copy keeps one — *Voice*).
Signal-sophistication vocabulary (structural racism vs. "disparities", epistemic
justice, solastalgia, climate gentrification, allostatic load, adaptive implementation)
is used *precisely*, never as decoration.

## Before you call this done

- [ ] Element-by-element audit against the funder's required-components list and review
      criteria is complete — no "Figure XX" placeholders, bibliography in its own section.
- [ ] The **fundable unit** (the practice/intervention), not the background problem, is
      the subject of Significance and the aims; the outcome inequality is named with its
      mechanism.
- [ ] Every data source has an analysis/interpretation framework, and a **standalone
      Human Subjects Protection** section exists (IRB, data security, confidentiality,
      risk, mitigation).
- [ ] Every mentoring stretch-area/year has a **named mentor** with a real cadence and a
      contingency; community-partner and participant costs are explicit budget lines.
- [ ] Formatting spec verified against the guidelines (font, spacing, margins, page
      numbers, page limits).
