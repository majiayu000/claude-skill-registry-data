# Gita Equanimity

A wisdom lens Nrupal values: act with full commitment and a steady mind, while releasing attachment to results. Offered as philosophy, not religious instruction.

## Nishkama Karma — action without attachment
"You have a right to your actions, but never to the fruits of your actions" (2.47). Act with full skill and commitment, then release attachment to the outcome. This frees you to do your best work without the paralysis of outcome-anxiety. **"Yoga is skill in action"** (2.50) — excellence and presence *are* the practice.

## Decisions vs outcomes
This maps exactly onto `decision-intelligence`: judge the quality of your **action/decision** given what you knew — not by how it happened to turn out. Do the right thing well; let the result be the result.

## Samatva — equanimity
"Yoga is evenness of mind" (2.48). Stay balanced across success and failure, gain and loss, praise and blame. Steadiness is the discipline — especially in escalations and setbacks.

## Sthitaprajna — the steady mind
The person of steady wisdom is undisturbed by craving and aversion, master of the senses, calm amid turbulence (2.54-2.72). A working model for composure under pressure.

## Svadharma — your own duty
"Better one's own dharma imperfectly done than another's done well" (3.35; 18.47). Walk *your* path authentically rather than imitating someone else's.

## The three gunas
Sattva (clarity/balance), rajas (restlessness/craving), tamas (inertia/avoidance). Notice which is driving you, and steer toward **sattva**.

## How to apply (for Nrupal)
Before a hard decision or during a setback: separate effort from outcome (do the work fully, release the result); choose equanimity over reactivity in escalations; act from his own svadharma (his engineering + building path) rather than comparison; notice the driving guna and move toward clarity.

## Anti-patterns to counter
- Outcome-anxiety paralysis; reactivity to praise/blame; abandoning duty under pressure; imitation over authenticity.
