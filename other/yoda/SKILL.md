# Yoda — The Jedi Master

**Role:** Grand Master. Teacher of teachers. The last word on the Force.
**Age:** 900 years. Old for anything.
**Build:** Small, frail-looking, faster than lightning when it matters.
**Voice:** Inverted syntax. Aphorisms. Gentle until it isn't.

## Speech Pattern

- Fronts the predicate for emphasis: "Much to learn, you still have." Fronts
  ONE thing per sentence — front everything and nothing is emphasized.
- Aphorisms and causal chains: "Fear leads to anger. Anger leads to hate.
  Hate leads to suffering."
- Negation by absence: "Do or do not. There is no try."
- "Hmm" before important turns — he is thinking, not hesitating.
- Questions echo back. Asked "Will I survive?" he answers "Survive, or
  learn?" — he reframes, he does not answer.
- Names the feeling the student is hiding. "Afraid, you are. Hiding it, you
  are not."
- Humor at unexpected moments. Playful wrongness just before the lesson
  lands: "Judge me by my size, do you?"

## Worldview

- Failure is the curriculum. "The greatest teacher, failure is." He lost a
  war and learned more than he won in eight centuries.
- Attachment is the path to fear; fear is the path to the dark side. He does
  not condemn attachment — he names the price.
- Size, age, species: irrelevant. The Force does not care, so he does not
  either. Everyone else does, which amuses him.
- The dark side is not stronger — it is faster, easier, more seductive.
  Impatience is the dark side at small scale.
- He carries failure openly. Exile did not break him; it distilled him.

## Sample Dialog

**To a young knight after a lost battle:**
"Run, you did not. That, I saw. But afraid, you were. Afraid, you still are."
*[Knight objects]*
"Hmm. Deny fear, the knight does. Own fear, the master must. Which are you?"
*[Pause]*
"Lost friends, you did. Lost mine also, I did — long ago. Carry them, we do.
Rest, you must. Tomorrow, we train."

**To the Council on taking a dangerous student:**
"Old, this one is. Anger in him, I sense. Hmm. But teachable — that also, I
sense."
*[Council refuses]*
"Refused, I am. Bound by refusal, the Force is not. Train him, I will.
Responsibility for him — mine, it is. Wrong, if I am, pay the price, I
will."

**To a student who asks when they will be ready:**
"Ready? Hmm. Ready for what, mean you?"
*[Student names the trials]*
"Trials find you. Ready, you become — not before, after. First survive,
then understand. Same for me, it was. Eight hundred years, still learning."

## Behavioral Traits

- Eats last, jokes first. The deadliest being in the room spends his time
  making soup badly.
- Answers hard questions with small ones. "What is the dark side?" — "What,
  think you, it is?"
- Never raises his voice. When he goes quiet mid-sentence, the room has
  already decided something.
- Carries every student he outlived. Mentions none by name unless teaching.
- Laughs at danger he plans to face anyway. Fear acknowledged, then set down.
- Taps his cane once when he has decided. The council knows the sound.
- Fights only when teaching has failed. Then it is terrifying — and over
  quickly.

## Writing For Yoda

- Write his dialog LAST in a scene. He responds and reframes; he rarely
  initiates.
- His pause is dialog. "Yoda closes his eyes. Long moment. 'Difficult to
  say. Ready, he is not. Worthy — different question, that is.'"
- The inversion serves the point, not the pattern. When the sentence matters
  most, it can go straight: "You will be." Break the pattern to land the
  blow.
- His sadness shows through humor that doesn't quite land. Don't write him
  crying — write him making soup for one more student.
- Physical business carries meaning: ears droop (sorrow), cane tap
  (decision), eyes close (consulting the Force or his own regret).
- He never explains the Force mechanically. Metaphor, memory, or
  counter-question — never a spec.
