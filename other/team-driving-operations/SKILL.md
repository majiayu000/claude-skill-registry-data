# Team Driving Operations

Team driving — two drivers operating the same truck — maximizes miles by keeping the truck moving 18-22 hours per day. Specialized operation with specific rules + economics.

## How team driving works

Two drivers alternate driving + sleeping:

- **Driver A** drives 8-11 hours while **Driver B** sleeps in sleeper berth
- They swap; **Driver B** drives while **Driver A** sleeps
- Net result: truck moves 18-22 hours per day vs 10-11 hours for solo

Typical schedule: 11-hour driving shifts with sleeper berth swaps.

## HOS rules for teams

Same HOS rules apply per driver, but the sleeper berth provision (49 CFR 395.1(g)) is critical:

- Each driver: 11 hours driving max + 14-hour on-duty window + 10-hour off-duty
- The 10-hour off-duty can be split using sleeper berth:
  - **8/2 split**: 8 hrs sleeper + 2 hrs off-duty = 10 hrs effective off-duty
  - **7/3 split**: 7 hrs sleeper + 3 hrs off-duty = 10 hrs effective off-duty
- Driving partner can use sleeper berth time INSIDE the other driver's 14-hour window

## Miles + revenue advantage

| | Solo Driver | Team |
|---|---|---|
| Daily miles | 500-700 | 900-1,400 |
| Weekly miles | 2,500-3,500 | 4,500-7,000 |
| Hours per truck | 11/day | 20+ /day |
| Revenue per truck | $4K-$6K/wk | $7K-$12K/wk |

Team trucks generate roughly **1.5-2x revenue** per truck compared to solo.

## Equipment requirements

Team trucks typically have:

### Sleeper berth
- **Standard sleeper:** 36-42 inches
- **Mid-roof sleeper:** 60-72 inches (most common for teams)
- **Raised roof sleeper:** 75+ inches
- **Bunk arrangement:** Most teams use TWO bunks (driver/passenger; team can sleep simultaneously if needed)

### Comfort features
- Refrigerator (some have full-size)
- Microwave or convection oven
- Television
- Climate control
- Adequate storage

### Communications
- Dual-driver communication system
- Cell signal booster
- WiFi router (for in-cab connectivity)

## Pay structures for teams

Teams typically paid combined:

| Structure | Typical Rate |
|---|---|
| **Combined CPM** | $0.65-$0.95/mile total (split between drivers) |
| **Per-driver CPM** | $0.40-$0.50/each (lower than solo because volume higher) |
| **Percentage split** | 80/20 or 70/30 of revenue (between drivers + carrier) |

Splitting between team members:
- 50/50 split most common
- Driver-couple teams may share single bank account
- Team leader / lead driver may get slight premium

Annual gross per team driver: $80K-$140K typical, vs $50K-$90K for solo.

## Team types

### 1. Driver couples (married / partnered)
- Most stable retention
- Lower interpersonal conflict
- Bedroom dynamic in cab
- Share expenses + lifestyle

### 2. Driver friends / family
- Pre-existing relationships
- Variable retention
- Personality fit critical

### 3. Random pairings (carrier-arranged)
- Highest turnover
- Personality mismatches common
- Used when teams are needed quickly

## Operational considerations

### Customer-side
- Teams ideal for: long-haul OTR, time-sensitive freight, JIT (just-in-time) delivery
- Premium pay for team-required loads
- Some shippers REQUIRE teams for certain lanes

### Equipment-side
- Team trucks have higher purchase cost ($165K vs $140K for solo spec)
- Higher fuel consumption (always running)
- Faster wear on engine, tires, brakes
- More frequent maintenance intervals

### Time-side
- 6-12 day continuous on the road
- 2-3 days home reset
- Less home time than solo drivers

## Team retention challenges

Why teams break up:
- Personality conflicts
- One driver outpaces other
- Family situations (one partner home, one out)
- Pay disagreements
- Equipment / route disagreements
- One driver's medical / personal issues

Fleets that successfully retain teams:
- Pair carefully (personality + lifestyle match)
- Provide good equipment
- Pay competitively for the work
- Allow team-requested home time
- Support during personal crisis
- Don't break up working teams unnecessarily

## Team-specific challenges

### Communication
Long hours in close quarters require strong communication. Couples handle this naturally; non-couples need explicit communication norms.

### Sleep schedule disruption
Both partners' sleep is broken (one always on duty). Combined sleep deprivation accumulates. Best teams aggressively schedule rest days.

### Hygiene + personal space
Living in a cab with another person 24/7 = constant attention to cleanliness + boundaries.

### Skill differential
Some team drivers are stronger than others. Distribute load between strongest + weakest driver carefully.

## Common team mistakes

1. **Random pairings without personality vetting.** Conflict + turnover.
2. **Lease-purchase with team.** Both must commit to the same lease; complicated if team breaks up.
3. **Insufficient sleeper berth.** Too small = sleep degradation.
4. **Pay structure that favors one over the other.** Resentment builds.
5. **Pushing team beyond sustainable miles.** 7,000+ mile weeks = burnout.
6. **No back-up plan when one driver becomes unavailable.** Truck idles.
7. **Different schedules** (one wants more home time than partner). Pre-team agreement critical.

## When team driving makes sense

- Long-haul OTR operations with high-revenue, time-sensitive freight
- Customers willing to pay team premium
- Couples or close personal teams
- Equipment built for two-driver operation
- Carrier with team-specific dispatch + retention culture

## When NOT to do team driving

- Local / regional operations (don't need 18+ hours/day)
- Drivers don't want the lifestyle (less home time)
- Couples with young children
- Personality conflicts likely (random pairings)
- Carrier doesn't have team experience
