# Log Hauling Operations

Log hauling is a specialty operation primarily in forested regions (Pacific Northwest, Southeast, Northeast, Canada). It has specific cargo securement rules + state-level permit overlays.

## Federal log securement (49 CFR 393.116-118)

Logs are governed by special cargo securement rules:

### Bunks + stakes

Logs typically loaded between **bunks** (cross supports on the trailer) + secured with **stakes** (vertical posts on each side):

- Bunks must be securely attached to vehicle
- Stakes must extend at least 6 inches above the topmost log
- Stakes must be made of suitable strength (steel typically)
- Bunk + stake combination must withstand acceleration: 0.8g forward, 0.5g lateral, 0.5g rearward, 0.2g downward

### Tiedowns

- One tiedown over each "course" of logs
- Tiedowns must be tightened to manufacturer-specified working load limit
- Logs on a single-stack load require additional tiedowns

### Length-based rules

- Logs ≤ 10 feet long: 1 stake on each side
- Logs > 10 feet long: stakes positioned every 8-10 feet

## State logging permits

Major logging states have specific permit programs:

| State | Permit |
|---|---|
| Oregon | Forest Products Hauler Permit, Weight-Mile Tax (separate from IFTA) |
| Washington | Log Hauler Permit |
| Idaho | Log Hauler Permit |
| Montana | Special haul permit for log trucks |
| British Columbia, Canada | NSC-equivalent + provincial log haul permits |
| Maine | Maine DOT permits |
| Georgia | GA DOT |
| Mississippi | Forestry haul permit |

These typically allow operation at higher axle weights than standard (logs often over federal 80K lb limits with permit).

## Short-haul exemption for logging

Per 49 CFR 395.1(e)(1), short-haul drivers operating within 150 air miles are exempt from full HOS + ELD requirements. Logging operations typically qualify:

- Within forest area + mill area = often within 150 air miles
- Bobtail home + load from forest = within 150 air miles
- Therefore: many logging drivers run on short-haul (paper logs only)

But: cross-state logging operations exceed 150 miles → full HOS + ELD applies.

## Driver requirements

- Standard CDL (Class A for tractor-trailer)
- DOT medical certificate
- Some states require **logging-specific** training (Oregon SAIF, Washington L&I, etc.)
- Some states require log loading certification (especially for self-loading trucks)

## Load configurations

- **Tractor + log trailer** — most common; 4-6 logs in a load
- **Self-loader (Stinger Steer)** — truck with onboard hydraulic loader; often used in remote areas
- **Pole logger / Pole truck** — for hauling very long timber poles
- **Mountain Logger / Steep Slope** — heavy-duty trucks for mountainous terrain

## Common log hauling mistakes

1. **Insufficient stake length** — stakes don't extend 6" above top log. Roadside OOS.
2. **Inadequate tiedowns** — only 1 binder when 2+ required.
3. **Operating over permitted weight** without state log permit.
4. **No bunk-and-stake gauges** to verify load weight.
5. **Drivers underestimating short-haul radius** + falling into full HOS rules.
6. **Logging operations crossing state lines** without each state's permit.
7. **Equipment failure on steep slopes** — rare but catastrophic.

## Tare weight vs gross weight in logging

Log trucks typically operate at very high weights:

- Empty tractor + log trailer: ~22,000-30,000 lb
- Loaded weight: 80,000-105,000 lb (with permit)
- Cargo weight: 50,000-75,000 lb of logs

Operators benefit from accurate cargo weighing to avoid overweight citations.

## Insurance for log hauling

Logging is higher-risk:
- Rolling logs hazard (cargo shift causing rollover)
- Steep slope operations
- Remote area operations (slower emergency response)
- Heavy load + uneven terrain

Insurance minimums often:
- $1M-$5M commercial auto liability
- $750K-$5M cargo coverage
- Specialized log hauling endorsement on policy

## Hours of service flexibility

Logging operations often benefit from:
- **150-mile short-haul exemption** (no ELD required)
- **Agricultural exemption** during state-declared forestry seasons (some states)
- **State-specific log hauling exemptions** (Oregon, Washington have specific carve-outs)
