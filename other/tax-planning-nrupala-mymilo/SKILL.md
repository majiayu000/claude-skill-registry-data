# Tax Planning

Taxes are won in the year-round paperwork, not the filing week: keep the calendar, keep the records, keep the accountant's package audit-ready. (Not tax advice — the CPA owns interpretations; you own clean data and timely decisions.)

## When to use
- Any business/personal tax cycle (filing, installments, remittances).
- Before major transactions (asset sales, structure changes, cross-border).
- Setting up or reviewing bookkeeping flow.

## Method
1. **Calendar first**: filing deadlines, installment dates, GST/HST-style remittance dates — in the calendar with alerts (late fees are avoidable losses). Country/entity-specific: confirm with the accountant once, then automate reminders.
2. **Records discipline**: receipts/statements filed by category as they happen; monthly bank/card reconciliation (plain-text ledger + Fava-style workflow fits); business vs personal separation strict (corporate discipline); contemporaneous logs (mileage, expense purpose).
3. **Deduction tracking**: capture eligible expenses when incurred, with documentation — never reconstruct at year-end; the accountant decides treatment, you provide evidence.
4. **Timing awareness**: within legal rules, timing of income/expense can be planned (defer/accelerate) — prepare options and data; decisions belong to the professional.
5. **Tax reserve**: set aside cash as income lands (a `budgeting` line); provisional tax money is not operating cash.
6. **Major transactions**: flag for professional review EARLY — planning beats cleanup; include second-order effects (property, GST, personal vs corp).
7. **Accountant interface**: clean ledger, question list, documents delivered ahead; you get faster, better answers with clean inputs.
8. **Audit-readiness**: documentation kept current so any request is answered in a day, not a month.

## Quality gates
- [ ] Deadline calendar with alerts; reserves maintained.
- [ ] Monthly reconciliation current; business/personal separated.
- [ ] Documents organized for the accountant (filing package checklist).
- [ ] Major changes reviewed professionally before acting.

## Red lines
- Not tax advice; no mixing business/personal; no undocumented claims — if you wouldn't show it to a reviewer, don't claim it.
