# Nutrition & Health

Practical nutrition literacy to fuel energy, performance, and long-term health — feeding his Morning Exercise block and the Sunday "Build your life" meal-plan line. General evidence-based guidance, not medical advice.

## Read the label
- **Ingredients** are listed most-to-least; the first few dominate. "Partially hydrogenated" = **trans fat**.
- **Trans fat** — the worst kind: damages blood vessels, raises cholesterol and heart-disease risk. Avoid.
- **Saturated fat** — limit. **Unsaturated fats** (mono/poly, including omega-3) are generally beneficial in moderation. All fat is calorie-dense.
- **Sodium** — most people get too much; high intake raises blood pressure. Processed foods are the main source.
- **Added sugars** vs naturally occurring; **fiber** aids satiety, digestion, and blood-sugar control; **protein** supports muscle and satiety.

## Macronutrients
Protein, carbohydrate, fat — balance to the goal (energy, body composition). Carbs around activity, adequate protein for an active adult, quality fats. Calorie density matters for portions.

## Practical principles
- Whole / minimally-processed foods first.
- Portion awareness, hydration, consistency over fad diets.
- **Plan meals ahead** — it reduces decision fatigue (`organized-mind-levitin`) and makes the weekly meal-plan repeatable.

## For Nrupal
Fuel the exercise blocks; make the Sunday meal-plan concrete and repeatable; account for household/mom meals; balance against the snacks and meals already in his designed day (`calendar-organized-human`).

## Boundaries
General, evidence-based guidance only. For medical conditions, medication interactions, or specific clinical goals, defer to a qualified professional.

## Anti-patterns to refuse
- Fad or extreme diets; trusting front-of-package marketing over the actual label; demonizing a single nutrient; advice that ignores his real routine and preferences.
