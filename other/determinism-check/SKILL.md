# Determinism

A build is deterministic when a reader who follows the shipped sentences word for word, inferring nothing
the files do not say, lands on the golden. That is how the portal's judge reads. Builds fail it one way: the
golden holds only under a reading the author supplies (the write-up, the design note, "plain sense") while
the shipped text, read literally, supports another. Findings in 25 judge reports: a write-up figure
not reproducing 19 (§5); estimand not literal 16 (§1); convention unpinned 13 (§3); method unforced 9
(§1); a filed rule reading otherwise 7 (§1). task445 passed our judge twice and failed the portal on a
population read literally; eight builds lost their stump when a pin removed the ambiguity it lived in.

## 1. WHAT is filed literally, HOW may stay open

**WHAT:** the estimand, filed so that its literal execution is the golden's quantity: population (in terms
the data executes, saying whether members not yet observed at the as-of date are in it), outcome, window,
unit, comparison, inclusion and exclusion, threshold, rounding. Each fixed by the question or by one
filed sentence at authority, stated once, never worded so the literal reading is the easy answer.
A source the golden completes (a list whose capture it estimates, a link whose error it corrects) says in
its own documentation that it does not hold every member, never how many or which: left unsaid, a literal
judge reads that source as the count and the other source's excess as error (task448's portal return).

**The comparison is WHAT, and a decisive step that is a comparison choice cannot be saved.** The five
parts above include the comparison, so whose rates, which pool, which peer group supplies the
expectation is part of the estimand and has to be filed so its literal execution is the golden.
task194's architecture 2 put its decisive step exactly there, on whether the expectation comes from a
published standard or from the comparison population's own current rates, and the author compounded it
by ruling that the charter must say nothing about whose rates supply it, which withheld the one part
the spec requires filed. The judge read the published rates literally, as the only rates the package
carries, and filed a different unit; and filing the comparison instead turns the step into executing
it. Every lever below it, the pool and the denominator, is also part of the estimand, so there is no
position where both gates hold. Before drawing a decisive step, name which of the five parts it is: if
it is one of them, it is WHAT, it must be filed, and the stump has to live somewhere else. The HOW is
how a fully filed estimand is computed, and the data must force that.

**Where the decisive step is an estimator choice, the assertions run over its GROUPINGS, not only over its
placements and penalisations.** One build asserted a single grouping refuted and left three untested; of those
three, two named another candidate at 1.648x and 1.676x and a third pooled the designated unit with its
neighbour, so the shipped refutation sentence was weaker there than the note claimed. A 16-member standing
family with two names rode through 233 green assertions and three judge passes. Enumerate the groupings the
records license (per unit, per parent, shrunk to the pool, collapsed to a binary) and assert each, because a
grouping the data cannot reject is the hardest member to see: the groups genuinely share the quantity, so the
rival differs from the golden only by estimation noise and the question is whether the decision's own margin
clears the noise the grouping adds.

**Two candidate denominators can be unseparable in principle, and the test is cheap.** Where the open HOW is
which exposure base a rate is built on, the record separates the candidates only if their period profiles
differ inside the unit the decision is made at. Profile both bases' period counts against the realised events
and read `2 dlogL` against the events the record actually carries. One build's two bases correlated 0.9951 to
0.9988 by unit and 0.9994 pooled, giving `2 dlogL` of 0.22 on one degree of freedom against a 3.84 threshold,
so separating them needed about 40,782 events against the record's 96, some 425 times the pack or 743 years
of that centre's traffic. A per-unit fit also absorbs exactly the cross-unit variation such a design leans
on, which is why no across-unit test touches it. Run the profile before building a pack whose forcing is
meant to come from the data.

**HOW** may stay open only when something forces it (`references/forcing-the-answer.md` Part 5): the
design (the data's structure rules out every other method; preferred), a governing standard's general
method, or a filed selection criterion in general terms that never names the method (a recognisable
organ, never in consecutive builds). The data separates every reasonable candidate with margin, asserted.
**Convergence by insensitivity is not forcing either.** Before claiming the design forces the method, ask
why the agreeing estimator agrees. If it agrees because the graded quantity is insensitive to exactly what
the candidate specifications differ on, the agreement is algebra and every other candidate is still
standing: task190's architecture 2 reproduced its golden under the average of the surplus below a threshold
and the deficit above it, in 186 of 186 specifications, because averaging the two sides cancels the fitted
profile's level, while the surplus alone spanned 71 to 786 and the deficit alone 207 to 858 over the same
family. A refusal rule that lives only in the generator ("the specifications that fail to reconcile are
dropped") is not in the bundle, so a literal reader cannot apply it and the surviving set cannot be
reproduced. Three flavours of this have now been measured, and each needs its own check: the data too
smooth to separate anything, the candidates differing by less than their own sampling error, and the
agreeing estimator insensitive by construction.

**Two candidates can be collinear, and then no pack separates them.** The three flavours above are
measured on the pack; this one is settled before a file exists, because it is a property of the candidate
set rather than of the data. Where the candidates are functional forms, write each one out and compare
them over the range the record can actually occupy: forms that agree to first order there will fit within
noise of each other however much data you cut, and the margin you measure is the fit's arbitrariness
rather than a separation. task194's architecture 1 priced its counterfactual as a constant share of the
standing excess and its rival as a power on the standardised ratio; near a ratio of 1 those agree to first
order, so the two fitted at chi-square 3.30 and 3.34 on 7 degrees of freedom, a likelihood ratio of 1.02
to 1, and separating them would have needed units at ratios of three or four, which the domain does not
contain. Neither fix works on a collinear pair: filing the form files the HOW, and more placements buy
nothing, so the architecture is spent and the draw is re-rooted onto a quantity the record measures.
**Enumerate the forms before the routes.** That build enumerated eight pooling routes exhaustively and
two functional forms carelessly, which is the wrong way round: the forms are the outer loop and a wrong
one invalidates every route inside it. And **a separation measured on under-dispersed data is not a
separation**: report the dispersion of the subset carrying the comparison beside the dispersion of the
rest of the record, because the same build's placement quarters ran at 0.47 per degree of freedom against
1.65 elsewhere, so every likelihood ratio it reported was inflated about three and a half times.

**Forcing measured on smooth data is not forcing.** Where the design forces the method, the separation is
measured on a pack whose sampling noise is plausible for the process (a Poisson-like record at chi-square
per degree of freedom near 1, never far below it), because a draw taken from evenly spaced quantiles of a
fixed curve makes every estimator agree whatever the method. Measure the candidates' spread on that pack
first, then set the graded granularity from the spread, raising the record's volume where the figure needs
finer reporting than the noise supports. Coarsening the stated rounding to dodge the measurement is the
banned move; stating the figure at the precision the data carries is the honest one, and leaving the
amounts smooth to keep a fine rounding is the defect both ways round (task190 architecture 2: 199
estimators and 180 fit specifications agreed at chi-square per degree of freedom 0.029, and the judge put
the realistic spread at plus or minus 50 on a 400 figure reported to the nearest 10).
Nothing forcing it: two methods survive (`underspecified_objective`). The method itself filed: the step
is rule application and the stump is gone.

**Never file a procedure, basis or formula whose literal application gives another answer**: an official
decoy and a fork at once. **And a pin added to close one fork can open a larger one**, so read every
new pin's literal execution against the graded quantity and not only against the fork it was written for.
A build added a clause pinning the grain ("a partner's household-size band has one award level for those
twelve months ... an award level is not taken claim month by claim month") to close a month-by-month
reading, and the clause also said the scheme's awards are taken **at** those levels. Read literally that
collapses the within-cell distribution the committed method depends on, so a whole cell refers or none of
it does, and the literal answer moved two candidates away from the golden. The next isolated judge filed
that literal answer. The pin was endorsed in
writing as fixing the unit a level is taken at while leaving open how the distribution is recovered; it
did not leave that open, it determined it. **The stump lives in HOW, never in WHAT.** If a literal estimand makes the call
easy, the stump was a fork; redesign the decisive step (`stumping`), never keep the estimand vague.

**Run the fork-is-the-stump test before building the repair, not after.** The test is cheap on paper and the repair
is not. Its signature: the portal's finding is an unpinned population, and the stump sentence says the easy route
omitted that same population. The pin then tells the reader the one question they were not asking, and no wording
avoids it. Measured on one build: closing three forks without filing any method left every graded figure frozen and
every free gate green, and the call then landed cell for cell. The pin does not
necessarily make the decisive step unanalytical, but it removes whatever difficulty consisted of nobody asking, so
decide before building whether anything else carries the task.

**A pin can leave the decisive step analytical and still spend its difficulty.** The two failures are different and
the repair reaches them in order. Rule 3 asks what KIND of step decides; Gate F asks whether anyone is stopped by it.
On the same build the licensed judge ran 40 variants and measured that every easy route lands materially wrong (the
unconditional reading wrong on three platforms, stopping at the snapshot wrong on four, two field misreadings at
roughly half and one and a half times the golden), so the decisive step was an estimand choice and rule 3 passed.
It still failed, because the remaining step was a reflex: a competent solver conditions a survival curve on survival
to the as-of date without deliberating, and that reflex reaches the golden while the filed record supplies every
other convention. **A reflex reaching the golden spends the stump** (`stumping` Part 6), whatever the
step's kind. When a pin closes the last thing a solver had to ask, list the reflexes for what remains and check that
each lands wrong.

**A leak reader's question-6 answer is its account of a route, not a measurement.** On the same build a reader
answered that the call followed filed rules and read dates rather than an analytical choice, while the formula it
quoted carried the survival-conditioning denominator it never named as a choice. Where a reader's account and the
judge's measurement of the alternatives disagree, the measurement decides what kind of step it is.

**What is left open after the WHAT is filed must be a method, not a knob.** Filing the five parts is
mandatory, so a design whose decisive step is one of them has no stump once it is filed, and a design
whose only remaining residue is a tolerance, a cut-off, a rounding or a nearest-k has a convention rather
than a method: close it (section 3 requires that) and the stump is gone, leave it open and Gate C fails on
a flip nobody can defend. task198's architecture 1 filed the comparison correctly, as a candidate's share
against its own earlier quarters "which carried the same offered load", and the residue was how closely
"the same" must match: three different queues win across licensed readings (a band of 0.05 to 0.2 or load
to one decimal gives one, a band of 0.22 to 0.3 or load rounded to whole units gives the plain
highest-share answer, five or more nearest quarters pooled gives a third), and its judge read Gate H as
rule application because executing the clause correctly is all the answer needs. The test at the draw:
name the residue, then ask whether a competent analyst could defend a different value of it. If the honest
answer is that any value is as good as another, it is a knob and the design has no decisive step.

## 2. The literal-reader test and the semantic fork table

On paper before data is cut, again on the finished pack; the result is the design note's
`## Literal reader` line.
1. Execute the prompt and every filed definition and rule word for word. Where the text is silent, list
   every option and compute each.
2. Pass only if every literal reading gives the golden, or each differing reading violates a quotable
   shipped sentence, or the records make the readings converge (C1, asserted on figure and counts).
3. The easy answer comes from an easy method on the same literal estimand, never from another reading.
4. Every write-up step passes the same test; a step needing an unstated inference is an open fork.

**The semantic fork table** (`## Semantic fork table`, ids `SF1`, `SF2`...) is that test written down: one
row per term in the main ask (every noun, unit, period word) and per convention the main computation needs
(as-of, inclusion, grain, rounding path, tie-break): the golden reading, each alternative, the call under
it, and the sentence that rules it out, or `same call`. The stump is never one of the readings (six builds,
119 to 134, carried a reading difference as their difficulty and were judged a fork or landed every
time). `precut.py` refuses an unresolved row; `checks.py` asserts every row.

## 3. Close every convention

- **Walk the 22 axes** (`references/forcing-the-answer.md` Part 1) into the design note's
  `## Closure table`, one row per axis number with the reading and the move; axes that cannot move the
  answer today included. A design-stage gate: `precut.py` refuses a missing or empty row.
- **Closure moves, strongest first** (Part 2): **C1 converge** (every reading selects the same rows);
  **C2 confirm** (a filed criterion shown on closed data to select one candidate, never recovering an
  unfiled rule); **C3 corridor** (the parameter unrecoverable, the answer identical across its range);
  **C4 separate** (every cell mapped to the sentence it violates, nearest wrong cell at least 6 per cent).
- **When the rival route is another correction of the same graded quantity, all four closure moves are
  exhausted and none of them closes it.** Worked on a build whose decisive step corrects a classifier's
  marks with its recall and false-mark rate, and whose open seventh cell is a calibration of the hand code
  on the mark plus a month index, extrapolated past the audited months: it files 504 and prints 500 against
  a golden of 450, and nothing in the bundle rules it in.
  - **C1 converge** aligns the data so the rival agrees, which here means making the month term extrapolate
    the drift correctly. The rival then becomes a second correct route, so a solver who fits any month trend
    files the golden and Gate F pays for what Gate C gained.
  - **C2 confirm** needs a filed criterion that distinguishes the two corrections. The distinguishing
    property is that one is free of how common the class was inside the audited months and the other is not,
    **which is the decisive step itself**, so filing the criterion hands over the stump (the Part 4 pin test:
    delete the clause and the answer stops being forced, which is exactly why it is load-bearing, and
    exactly why it leaks).
  - **C3 corridor** needs the answer invariant across the parameter's range, but the parameter is what the
    two corrections disagree about.
  - **C4 separate** needs a shipped sentence the rival violates, which is C2 under another name.

  So a build in this position is not repaired, it is re-rooted, and the finding is structural rather than
  particular: **two corrections of one graded quantity cannot be separated by the record without either
  naming the separating property or making them agree.** Check it at design time, not at the judge: list
  every correction of the graded quantity a competent analyst could apply, and if two survive, the decisive
  step is the wrong shape. This is the Gate C against Gate F dial stated as an enumeration over the closure
  moves rather than as a tendency.

- **A C3 corridor closes the call and forfeits the figures, so it cannot carry a figure-dense prompt shape.**
  When the decisive construction has a fitted nuisance parameter and the data do not identify it, C3 is the
  honest closure: the parameter is unrecoverable and the answer is identical across its range. But "the
  answer" that survives is the set-valued call, not the numbers. Measured on one build's corrected
  interval-censored placement, over a level corridor from 0.70 to 0.999 whose profile likelihood rises
  monotonically to the boundary, at four seeds:

  | what is graded | across the corridor |
  |---|---|
  | the set of eight slots | **identical at every level and under both grid variants, every seed** |
  | the ranking of those eight | identical at three seeds of four, **not at the fourth** |
  | each slot's own figure | spreads up to 347.6 on a figure near 3,500, about 10 per cent |
  | the covered total | spreads 6.20 to 9.08 per cent |

  So the call is determinate and nothing else is. A prompt shape that draws its criteria from figures per
  candidate cannot be served, and `../guide-to-prompt` forbids making the count up from stacked asks. **This
  closes the whole family from the other side:** identify the nuisance parameter and the aggregation bias of
  1.1f makes the correction no better than the uncorrected route; leave it unidentified and C3 buys the call
  at the price of every figure. Before drawing a correction with a fitted nuisance level, decide which
  figures the shape needs graded and check them across the corridor, not just the call.

  **The escape is a correction with no fitted parameter at all**, its rate read off a shipped record rather
  than estimated: then there is no corridor and every figure is determinate.

- **Pin at authority, once** (Part 6.1). The prompt carries the question and no convention. Sweep for
  sentences quotable against the answer and qualify or remove them.
- **Measure the edge, not the centre.** For a value `v` printed to a unit `u`, the printed cell is centred
  on `round(v/u)*u` and the distance to its nearest edge is `u/2 - abs(v - round(v/u)*u)`. The quantity
  `abs(v - round(v/u)*u)` is the distance to the centre, and the two sum to `u/2`, so computing it reports
  clearance where it measured proximity: one build read 0.0129 as its tightest share's clearance when the
  true distance was 0.0371, and the mis-ranking of which figure was tightest followed from the wrong metric
  rather than from the data. Assert the edge distance, and name the tightest figure from the same number.
- **Bins:** every graded figure mid-bin on a non-round value, flip condition stated as a number; never
  grade an iteration count, an even-set median, or a percentage near a one-decimal edge (Part 3).
- **The bin is wider than the estimator, measured in bins before data is cut.** Where the graded figure is
  estimated rather than counted, three quantities are measured in units of its bin and written into the ladder:
  the decisive step's own worth, the spread between the defensible subgroups or samples an estimate could be
  read off, and the sampling interval of the one the golden uses. The first must exceed the other two together,
  and it is never the smallest rung gap. A build whose decisive step is worth a fraction of its bin while a
  subgroup choice is worth more is not repairable by a pin, a world parameter or a coarser bin: the estimate
  cannot be forced to that precision, the architecture is spent, and a coarser bin brings the default path into
  the golden's bucket. task157 shipped twice at 0.58 of a bin for the decisive step against 0.90 for the
  subgroup choice and 0.31 for the rate's own 95 per cent interval, and both portal rounds landed one bucket out.
  **Read the third quantity by whether the estimator has a free parameter.** Where the golden's estimator is a
  fitted rate, its sampling interval becomes real disagreement, because solvers fit it on different subgroups and
  file different figures: that is what sank task157. Where it is an observed count over a constant the design
  fixes, with no fitted parameter, window, band width, distribution family or grouping, every solver computing it
  files the same figure to the unit, and the interval is the quantity's honest uncertainty rather than a fork. It
  is still measured and still written down, because it sets how much of the bin the ladder may not use, but a
  build is not spent for it. The distinction is tested, never asserted: compute the estimator under every
  grouping the records allow and show the total is identical.
- **A decisive step that ADDS A TERM TO A FITTED SPECIFICATION can never pass the bin test, and the proof is an identity rather than a measurement.** The cheap rungs file the graded figure with the term absent, and the weakest licensed member of the designed family files it with the term absent too, so (a) and (b) are the same quantity and (a) greater than (b) plus (c) is unreachable at every setting of every dial. Measured on a task397 draw at a ratio of 0.999 at both ends of a sixfold sweep: narrowed to the two forms the rigor pattern itself names, (b) 22.72 against (a) 22.75 at one end and 52.28 against 52.50 at the other, with 0 of 8 dial settings passing. The one setting that closes the family (the term's simplest shape) is the setting where the term carries nothing, 0.0059 against a rival's 0.0446, so the golden becomes the rival and there is no stump: buying separation and closing the family are the same move in opposite directions. So screen the FORM of the decisive step before any harness. Adding a control, a level, an interaction, a random effect or a contextual term is a specification extension and is closed as a class, which is most of what a multilevel or regression-shaped task type offers; what passes is a reconstruction whose arithmetic closes over rows the records supply exactly.
- **In a fitted family, read (b) and not (c).** (c) was expected to bind on a multilevel draw and did not: it measured 3.59 bins and stayed flat across the whole dial, being the cluster-robust sampling error of the fitted coefficients carried to the graded figure by the delta method. The free parameter that kills such a build is not in the variance components, it is WHICH LEVEL the conditioning variable is centred at and WHAT SHAPE the contextual term takes, and both land in (b). One of the five licensed forms of the same construction printed the graded figure with the opposite sign, and the simplest of them forked the call itself, naming the golden's driver in 5 or 6 of 30 worlds against 30 of 30.
- **The bin test is necessary and not sufficient, and the screens run beside it rather than after.** It prices
  the decisive move against the readings and the noise, and says nothing about whether a one-divide route is
  rank-equivalent to the golden. A task198 candidate passed it at 1.26, 1.43 and 1.27 over three seeds, with the
  decisive step clear of the smallest rung gap, and died on the multiplicative-correction floor: sd(log factor)
  0.19 against a dispersion ceiling near 1.4, rho 0.10 against the 0.80 floor, and the easy route naming the
  same unit in 55 of 60 draws. Run the floor and geometric-mean screens, the deletion tests and the swap test
  alongside the three measurements, never as a second pass on whatever survives them.
- **The second quantity is the WHAT screen read as a number.** Where the defensible-subgroup spread is the
  binding term, the fork lives in the population, the window or the unit, which section 1 requires filed; and
  filing it leaves the decisive step a division over shipped columns, which is rule application. So a candidate
  whose spread is closed by filing the population has not been repaired, it has been converted into a banned
  decisive step. Measured on the same candidate: three defensible populations for a load factor (the event's own
  half hour, its own day's mean, its own month's mean) spread the figure 4.39 to 6.08 against a decisive worth
  of 6.58 to 8.03, while the sampling interval was 0.20 to 0.28.
- **The third quantity and the reflex's own precision can be one number**, and then buying the test with volume
  is not available. On a repairable-system trend test the same inter-arrival scatter sets the golden's interval
  and the uncorrected reflex's accuracy, so the setting that lifts the ratio to 1.00 is also the setting where a
  plain shape estimator reaches the truth more often than the decisive route (31 of 60 against 28). Check
  whether the dial that raises the first quantity also raises the third before reporting a cell as passing.
- **Convergence is proved against a rival's family, never the golden's own.** A convergence block that varies
  only the construction the golden already uses (which periods a ratio is read from, which window, which band
  widths) measures the golden's internal stability and says nothing about determinism. Every such block names,
  and computes, at least two families a competent solver would build instead, and the bucket each files.
  task157's fourteen constructions converged 20 of 20 on the golden in every world tested, including the one
  two portal judges broke by building a cell model the block never considered.
- **The call's robustness is not the figures' determinacy, and both are checked before data is cut.**
  Where the method stays open, run every candidate the data does not close and compare two things
  separately: which candidate it names, and what each graded figure prints. task182's architecture 3 had
  every fit form naming the same five dwellings and the same sixth while the fourteen graded levels
  differed by 0.9 to 1.8 dB, nine to eighteen times the printed unit, so the call was robust and every
  graded figure was forked. A design can pass the ranking test and fail Gate C on the figures the same
  ranking produces. Measure each figure's spread across the surviving candidates against its own printed
  unit, at the design stage, because the cure is a different decisive step rather than a repair.
  **The cell width comes from the asking sentence's stated rounding, never from the quantity's natural
  unit.** For a value `v` printed to unit `u` the cell centres on `round(v/u)*u` and the distance to
  the nearest edge is `u/2 - abs(v - round(v/u)*u)`; the second term alone is the distance to the
  centre, and the two sum to `u/2`. Both halves of that have now been got wrong in one wave: one build
  measured the distance to the centre and called it the edge, and another measured a fee rounded to the
  half against the nearest whole unit, so a reported 0.38 of clearance was really 0.12, and the rates
  then chosen to widen it put four of seven fees exactly on the boundary. Have the verifier recompute
  the distance from the shipped figure rather than read it out of `figures.json`.
- No decision metric in the prompt's own voice that a filed policy contradicts.
- No artifact ranks the candidates on the decision and gets it wrong, except a declared wrong-basis
  distractor (`dataset-generation` §8.3) that a shipped fact rules out and that is never the stump.

## 4. Assert in the generator, verify independently

In `checks.py` and again in `verify.py`, which reads only the shipped bundle: the literal reading's answer
equals the golden, or the readings' convergence; every C1 equality (figure, row and entity counts), every
C2 candidate's error and the winner's margin, the C3 corridor, every C4 cell with its violated sentence;
every `SF` row; every graded figure's bin distance; the **clean-data test** per suspect file (repaired in
the generator, the answer and the naive answer unchanged and still apart) and the **lens-swap test** (the
naive read and the answer are not one population at one moment under two lenses); the input gates; two
consecutive builds byte-identical. Compute forward; where a rung turns on two axes disagreeing, build the
disagreement per candidate (Part 6.5).

## 5. The write-up and figures.json

Every figure in `submission.md` is a claim the judge recomputes: the answers the rubric owes and a
reproducible path, nothing more (no incidental figure, no count a filed definition does not force, no
"the readings agree" without an assertion). Blocks and wording: `submission-writeup`. The generator writes
`<task>/generator/figures.json` (the call, each critical component, each step's result, every
deliverable answer, as the write-up states them), and the blocks are drafted from it. Build-stage gate:

```
python3 .claude/skills/determinism-check/figures_check.py taskNN   # 0 every number in blocks 1-4 is in figures.json; 1 missing; 2 no file
```

Typed figures were the commonest judge finding (12 of 16 in one window). Each step names the file and
rule in the verifier's words.

**`figures.json` may be written more than once, so a partial rebuild fails the gate on figures
nothing is wrong with.** task197's pack builder writes it and the write-up emitter then reads it,
adds the five figures only the write-up needs (the traverse spans, the obliged-pair count, the
count outside tolerance, the episode total and the standard's thresholds) and writes it back.
Running the pack builder alone left those five absent and the gate failed on four figures the
write-up had always stated correctly. Before reading a gate failure as a typed figure, check which
scripts assign the key and whether every one of them ran: the rebuild is the whole chain, not the
first script in it.

**A constant typed as a literal in the write-up emitter is still a typed figure, and the gate
cannot see it.** It compares the write-up against `figures.json`, so a threshold hard-coded in both
agrees with itself while disagreeing with the pack the moment the generator's parameter changes.
Every constant in `figures.json` is read from the generator's own parameters.

**Every graded figure is printed at the precision its asking sentence states.** A figure printed coarser
than the prompt asked for is a wrong figure, not a rounding preference: task182's prompt said to give the
air to one decimal and its write-up printed the six weekly figures whole, 689 for 689.2, so eighteen asked
figures were wrong on the face of the submission while every gate stayed green. `figures_check.py` cannot
see it, because it asks whether each number in the blocks is in `figures.json` and both carried the same
coarse value. Read the prompt's rounding sentence, list the figures it governs, and check each one's
printed precision against it by hand or in the verifier.

**A robustness claim is graded over the whole range it names.** "Furthest above under every number of
matched quarters" is checked at every number: task198's held for one to four quarters pooled and failed at
five, where a different queue leads. Compute the claim across its stated range or narrow the claim to the
range you computed.

**A clause that says what another method would give is a graded claim.** "Not a background taken from the
position's own record, which puts NSL03 and NSL12 in" names two candidates, so the judge builds that
background and checks: task182's judge could not get NSL12 into the five under any background proxy it
tried, and the method it did reproduce brought in a candidate the sentence left out. Either recompute
every rejected route the write-up names, and name exactly the candidates it yields, or write the sentence
without the candidate list.

**A graded figure the prompt never asked for is an open fork by construction.** The rubric can only owe
what the question asks, so a figure that sits in Deliverable Answers without an ask behind it has no
asking sentence to fix its basis, its population or its rounding, and a judge reads that as Gate E and
Gate C at once. task192 graded a five-part decomposition and a per-fortnight shaded series the prompt

## Running the judge

- **When:** `/build` stage 4 (in the background beside the leak reader, after the free gates pass), one
  re-judge in `/fix-determinism`, or when the author asks in their own words. Nothing else licenses it.
  `/fix-rubric` takes the free mechanical gates and no judge.
- **One isolated thread at medium effort**, in the background; meanwhile only work that moves no
  graded figure. It sees a fresh unzip of `target.zip`, the prompt, the submission's graded blocks and
  the judge's system prompt inlined verbatim; never the design note, `pipeline.json`, the stump sentence,
  an earlier report or anything its orchestrator read. The `determinism-judge` agent prepares and spawns
  it; without an `Agent` tool it returns the brief's path and the main session spawns from it. An
  orchestrator that has read beyond the bundle never judges in its own thread.
- **The graded blocks never sit in the brief.** They go in their own file the brief names and tells the
  thread not to open until its own derivation is written to disk. A brief that ends with the answers
  relies on the thread choosing to stop reading a file it was told to read, which it will not do:
  task199's re-judge read the committed answers before deriving and said so, and a verdict from a
  broken order is weaker evidence on Gate C, because a judge that cannot be surprised cannot tell you
  the answer was forced. The thread reports whether it kept the order, and a pass that broke it does
  not spend the architecture's one re-judge: the breach is the orchestrator's, so the cost is.
- **The thread reads literally first** (§2), then the gates, and counts a fork closed only by inference or
  by the write-up as open. Our judge is the floor: a fork it settles "by the plain reading" is a FIX_NOW.
- **An APPROVE is scoped to the pack the judge saw.** It ships that pack, not the folder's future. Any
  later change that moves a graded figure, or that alters a clause a gate turned on, voids it and the judge
  re-runs, because Gate A's verdict is that those figures recompute and they are different figures now.
  task197 was approved on every gate with Gate F strong, and a margin measurement ordered afterwards found a
  4.3488 MWh fork in how a loss factor is applied that the judge had never tested, whose closure moved all
  twenty exposure figures (the tightest bin distance going from 0.0040 to 0.0303 MWh). Work that moves
  nothing graded, such as realism in a file the call does not read or a wording fix the judge itself asked
  for, does not void it. So state the condition when ruling out a re-judge, because "APPROVE is not a
  FIX_NOW" holds only while nothing graded moves.
- **After:** relay the verdict, disposition, Gate G and Gate H lines. **APPROVE** ships. **FIX_NOW** is fixed
  in the generator and re-judged once. **SEND_BACK** re-roots or retires (`build-pipeline`).
- **A disposition is read against the gate table, not taken on its label.** The table sends back on Gate
  G and Gate H, the two that say the build is the wrong build. A Gate C or Gate E failure confined to a
  supplementary ask, with the main call forced and G and H passing, is an ask-layer repair and spends no
  architecture, whatever the thread called it: the asks are legally free of the call's rules, so the
  loosest wording in the build sits where Gate C is graded, and that is a wording fix. task196 was paused
  as spent on a third SEND_BACK whose gates read G PASS, H PASS, F strong with no lever needed, the main
  call reproducing exactly, and ask 4 alone failing C and E. Re-rooting that throws away a world, a pack
  and a forced call to repair one sentence. Before spending an architecture, name the gate that failed and
  check it is G or H.
