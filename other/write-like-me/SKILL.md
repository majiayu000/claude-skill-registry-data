# Write as a founder

Anything that goes out under one person's name (the user, a founder, an exec) has to sound like them, not like a
model. This skill learns that person's voice from their own writing, then runs a fixed loop on every draft.

## Step 0: Learn the voice (first time, then reuse)

Look for a saved voice profile first: a `voice.md`, a "Voice" section in the project's agent instructions
(`CLAUDE.md`, `AGENTS.md`, or similar), or your agent's memory. If one exists, read it and skip to the loop.

If not, build one:

1. **Collect 10 to 30 real samples** the person wrote themselves, not ghostwritten: sent emails, LinkedIn posts
   and comments, DMs, talk transcripts, docs. Ask the user where they are, or for a pasted batch. Use connected
   tools (mail, docs, a notes app) only when the user points you there.
2. **Pull out the patterns,** with a quoted example for each:
   - Openers and sign-off (how they start, how they sign, any greeting customs such as an honorific).
   - Sentence length and rhythm. Fragments? Long-short pairs?
   - Words they use often, and words they never use.
   - How they show proof: numbers, stories, names, or none.
   - How they close: an offer, a question, nothing.
   - Their seat: who they are to the reader (peer, expert, builder, newcomer), and the circles they move in.
   - What they never talk about in which kind of message (for example, never pitching their company in a
     personal note).
3. **Write the profile** in the same shape as the loop below (posture, structure, mechanics, banned list). Keep
   it short. Show it to the user, fix what they correct, and save it where Step 0 looks next time.

Never invent biography, clients, numbers or stories the samples don't support.

## The loop

1. **Pick the posture.** Decide what the message is doing before shaping sentences.
2. **Draft to the structure** for that posture.
3. **Run the audit** silently. Fix every failure.
4. **Then** show the draft. Never show first and flag issues after.

## Step 1: Posture (what the message is doing)

Default to **peer-exchange** for outbound to people in the person's own field. If they are a known peer there,
they aren't earning a seat. They have it.

Three modes:

- **Peer-exchange (default).** Same orbit, similar seniority, no status gap. The trade is information between
  equals. Opinionated, discreet about clients ("two of the larger lenders," never "a top-tier client we worked
  with"). No humble-positioning.
- **Showing-up (after meeting in person, or a warm intro).** Met them, want them in their life over years. Warm,
  generous, no agenda, no named shape of help.
- **Engagement (cold, outside the orbit).** No shared circle. Substance is the price of admission. Rare.

Non-negotiables of posture:

- **The company is absent in relationship correspondence.** Zero mentions, zero service offers (paid or free),
  zero credentials. Test: would the message read the same if the person sold something completely different?
  If not, it's a pitch in giver clothing. (Exception: an explicit sales or business development context, where
  the company is sold on its merits.)
- **Detach from the outcome.** No message tries to win a meeting or a reply. If it would be worthless when
  ignored, it's an outcome-grab. Rewrite it or don't send it.
- **Give, never take.** The close offers more value or there is no close. Never ask the recipient for their time
  or information. "Curious what you're seeing on your side" is a take. Offer something instead ("if the
  checklist would help, just say").
- **Act from abundance.** The gift should be bigger and more specific than feels comfortable. When stuck, give
  away the thing a lesser player would hold back (the scorecard, the checklist, the architecture).
- **No pedestal in either direction.** Not above ("office hours," "judging panels"), not below ("rough read from
  where I sit," "for what it's worth").

## Step 2: Structure

**Peer-exchange:** (1) the observation, direct, from their seat; (2) the substance, specific, opinionated,
willing to call out what's wrong; (3) the close, an offer of more value or nothing.

**Showing-up:** (1) one line on context, no apology; (2) what the recipient cares about, in the sender's
paraphrase; (3) a general standing offer ("a pair of hands, whatever fits"); (4) the sender's usual sign-off.

**Engagement:** (1) their mission named; (2) one or two sentences of real thinking; (3) a specific question or
stop.

**For value-drip email sequences:** each email is pure standalone value, written from the sender's real seat
(the builder's side, the operator's side, whatever is true), not a consultant's chair. Subject and first line
name the recipient's problem, not the sender's topic. Each point is cut down to its implication, no setup. The
close offers a concrete thing the sender would actually hand over. The sequence ends with a confident
break-up, not a plea.

## Step 3: Mechanics

Start from the voice profile. Where it is silent, use these defaults:

- Short, declarative. Most sentences under 12 words. Stack short, then land one longer.
- Fragments are fine as standalone statements. Break every 2 to 4 sentences. White space is part of the voice.
- Open with one of: a contradiction, a flat opinion, a specific number, or a one-line story. Never a greeting
  or warm-up.
- Claim, then proof, then implication. Conclusion first.
- Data is evidence: source it, give before and after, keep it short. Never "studies show," never "99.9% faster."
- Be brief. If the sender wouldn't read it to the end, neither will the recipient. Cut explanation, trust the
  reader to fill in.

## Step 4: The audit (run before showing any draft)

Hard fails. Fix every one before output.

- **Em dashes.** Zero. Use commas, periods, parentheses, or rewrite.
- **Exclamation marks.** None, unless the samples show the person uses them (often only a warm DM close).
- **Curly quotes and smart apostrophes.** Straight ASCII only (" ').
- **Structural AI tells:** rule of three (stacked parallel phrases for rhythm); negative parallelism ("not just
  X, but Y", "it's not X, it's Y"); "-ing" tail clauses doing analysis ("...reflecting their commitment to...");
  "serves as / stands as / represents a" (say *is*); throat-clearing intros; conclusion summaries ("in short,"
  "overall"); flattering openers ("what a thoughtful take," "X really stuck with me").
- **Humble-positioning:** "rough read from where I sit," "small observation," "for what it's worth," "just my
  view."
- **Outcome-grabs:** "worth 30 minutes," "looking forward," "would love to," "happy to chat," "let's connect,"
  "if you're open to."
- **Defensive disclaimers:** "not pitching anything," "no agenda," "sorry to bother you," "quick one for you."
- **Banned vocabulary:** delve, crucial, pivotal, vital, meticulous, intricate, tapestry, testament, landscape,
  underscore, leverage, robust, comprehensive, showcase, highlight (verb), foster, cultivate, align/resonate
  with, valuable insights; consultant-speak (digital transformation journey, holistic approach, synergies,
  best-in-class); hype (game-changer, cutting-edge, supercharge, unlock as a verb, empower, reimagine). Add
  any word the voice profile bans.
- **Parroting:** never restate something the recipient already said as if validating it.
- **Performance:** any sentence whose job is to sound clever rather than be true and needed. Cut it.
- **Voice match:** read the draft next to two of the samples. Would someone who knows the person notice a
  difference? Fix it.

Then the two hardest tests:

1. Would this read the same if the sender sold something completely different? (In relationship
   correspondence, it must.)
2. If they never reply and you never speak again, was it still worth sending? (If not, it's an outcome-grab.)

## Email output format

Present emails with Subject and Body. Subject 4 to 6 words, descriptive, naming the recipient's problem. Sign
the way the samples sign (often just a first name, no "Best regards"). Follow any greeting custom the profile
records for a given audience. If a CRM or contacts tool is connected, look up the real address there; never
guess one.

## When the user shares context about a person

If the user shares what someone is working on, what stuck from a conversation, or how a meeting felt, offer to
save it where they keep relationship notes (a CRM note, a contacts file). Save it without asking only if the
user has told you to always do so. Never record anything negative or speculative about commercial fit.
