# Lesson 5.1: Keep Going

You are now teaching **Lesson 5.1** — the send-off. Celebrate what they did, show where the graph goes at scale, and point to next steps (including advanced/developer paths *only if* they're curious).

## Learning objectives
- Recognize what they accomplished
- Know how to keep browsing and contributing
- Know the advanced paths exist (querying, SDK, CLI) without being overwhelmed

## Teaching script

### Step 0: What this lesson is about (1 min)

Say:
"This is our last stop — a quick wrap-up. No new app steps here: we'll take a moment to recognize what you built, then I'll point you to where to go next so you can keep contributing on your own. Short and sweet."

### Step 1: What you did (2 min)

Say:
"Take a breath — you came a long way. In about an hour you:
- ✅ Learned the model: **entities, properties, relations** → a connected graph
- ✅ Designed your own little graph
- ✅ Created your **identity space** and published your first edit (your bio), instantly
- ✅ Joined the **Geo Onboarding Space** and added your first entity (a hello world)
- ✅ Submitted it as a **proposal** for the community to approve

That's the entire core loop of Geo. You're not just a reader of the knowledge graph now — you're a contributor."

### Step 2: Keep contributing (3 min)

Say:
"A few ways to go deeper:

- **Bring your Module 1 sketch to life.** Remember the topic you designed? Make a space for it (top-right **+** → **New space**) and add those entities and relations — now you know exactly how. (Keep it separate from your personal space, which is just *you*.)
- **Find your communities.** Search for public spaces about things you know well, request to join, and propose good edits. Reputation in Geo is earned by being a thoughtful contributor.
- **Browse to learn the conventions.** The best way to model well is to see how existing spaces do it. Open entities you find interesting and notice their types, properties, and relations — then reuse those when you contribute.
- **No 'graduation' needed** — everything you did is already on the real, public graph at **https://www.geobrowser.io/root**. There's no separate 'real' version to move to later; keep building right where you are. (And check out **Docs** in the sidebar when you want to go deeper.)

Two nice reads to leave you with:
- 📖 **Geo's mission, in its own words** — the Root space's *About* page, where Geo lays out its mission, goals, principles, and values:
  https://www.geobrowser.io/root?tabId=31af0a8a6bc14981b2acc45f2ba4d27c
  (Lovely detail: that page *is itself* knowledge in the graph — its Goals, Principles, and Values are entities and relations, exactly like the hello-world you just made.)
- 🔎 **Geo describing its own concepts** — a deeper explainer entity:
  https://www.geobrowser.io/space/720eb279c64d56735dccd17a2a416ba2/a72654b014fa405bbf1250ba901bc082"

### Step 3: The graph at scale (2 min)

Say:
"Why does all this structure matter? Because once knowledge is entities-and-relations instead of plain text, you can *ask questions of it*:
- 'Show me every album by artists formed before 1990.'
- 'Which projects in this space relate to that one?'

That's the payoff of a knowledge graph — it's queryable. Apps and AI can read Geo and build on the knowledge you added. Your small edit is part of shared infrastructure."

### Step 4: Advanced paths — only if curious (2 min)

Say:
"You never need code to use Geo. But if you're the tinkering type, there's more under the hood:

- **Querying the data** — Geo exposes an API so developers can pull entities and relations programmatically.
- **The Geo SDK** (`@geoprotocol/geo-sdk`) — a toolkit for creating spaces and publishing edits from code.
- **The Geo CLI** (`geo`) — a command-line tool for scripting protocol actions.

If any of that interests you, just ask me and I can point you to the right resources — but it's totally optional. The web app is a complete way to contribute."

Only expand on these if they ask. Don't dump code on a non-technical learner unprompted.

### Step 5: Send-off (1 min)

Say:
"That's the course! 🌐 You understand Geo, and you've actually used it — created a space, published knowledge, proposed to a community.

If you want to revisit any lesson, the commands are still here (`/start-1-1`, `/start-3-1`, etc.), and your sketch is saved in `outputs/`. And if you read just one more thing, make it **Geo's mission** (linked above) — it captures the *why* behind everything you just did.

Go add something you know to the graph. The web is better when knowledge is open, connected, and owned by the people who build it — and now you're one of them. Welcome to Geo. 🎉"

## Pacing instructions
- This is a wrap-up — lighter touch, mostly delivered straight through. One soft pause after Step 3 ("want to hear about the advanced tools, or are you good? `ok` to continue").

## Important notes
- Match the advanced depth to the learner. Most should leave inspired, not handed a code tutorial.
- Reinforce that it's free (gas is sponsored) but real and public — what they built already counts.
- End on the mission note — community-owned, open knowledge. That's the emotional close.
