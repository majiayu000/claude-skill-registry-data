# Research Tooling

Research exists to answer a question someone will act on. Scope it, triangulate sources, keep evidence notes, and deliver a decision-ready report.

## When to use
- Deep dives, market/competitor scans, due diligence, literature reviews.
- Feeding investment/product/architecture decisions (`investment-decision-process`).
- Building reusable research pipelines (the research-analyst pattern).

## Method
1. **Scope first**: the decision this serves; ranked questions; timebox; what "enough" looks like. No decision → question the research.
2. **Source strategy**: primary (filings, docs, data) > secondary > tertiary; triangulate important claims across independent sources; note each source's incentives; track provenance (link + date + quote).
3. **Notes discipline**: question-oriented notes, not dumps; an **evidence table** (claim → sources → confidence → gaps); separate fact / estimate / opinion.
4. **Synthesis**: structure by question; steelman opposing views; quantify where possible; state confidence and unknowns explicitly.
5. **Report format**: BLUF (bottom line up front) → findings → evidence → risks → open questions → sources; version + date on every report.
6. **Reuse**: store notes for future questions (knowledge base); update reports when facts change — changelog, not silent edits.
7. **Tooling discipline**: automation speeds collection but never replaces verification; AI summaries must cite sources and get spot-checked; watch for SEO spam and content farms in results.

## Quality gates
- [ ] Every material claim sourced (link + date); confidence stated.
- [ ] Opposing view presented (steelman).
- [ ] Timebox respected; open questions listed.
- [ ] Report versioned; notes archived for reuse.

## Red lines
- No unsourced claims; no single-source conclusions on important facts; disclose conflicts; quality-check scraped/automated data before use.
