# Write like a human

Plain, direct, outcome first. Say less and mean all of it.

If the user has their own voice notes (a style guide, a `voice.md`, past posts they like), read them first.
Their rules win where they differ from this file. To learn a specific person's voice from their writing, use
`write-like-me`.

## How to use it (every time, in this order)

1. **Structure:** when the copy has to sell or convert (headlines, landing pages, store pages, pitches, CTAs),
   apply `make-it-sell` first. Skip it for pure-value pieces such as newsletters and news posts, which follow
   the structures below instead.
2. **Voice:** write the draft with this skill: the four checks, Voice and Never below.
3. **Slop pass:** run the slop pass at the end of this file on the finished draft, every piece, no exceptions.
   (If you have a dedicated AI-slop checker skill, run that too.) Fix what it finds, then read the result out
   loud once before showing the user.

## The four checks (run on every draft)

1. **Outcome first.** Open with what the reader gets or feels, not what the thing is or how it's built.
2. **3x so what.** Ask "so what?" of the main claim three times. Write the third answer.
   - Feature: "syncs your notes across devices" → you can start on the laptop and finish on the phone → the
     idea you had on the train is still there when you sit down to write.
3. **WIIFY (what's in it for you).** Every paragraph answers it for the reader. If it's about us, cut it or
   turn it around.
4. **Less is more.** Cut until removing one more word loses meaning. One idea per sentence, one ask per piece.

## Voice

- **Talk like a real person.** Read every line out loud. If nobody would say it that way, rewrite it the way you
  would. "The team is three people, and they just passed 10,000 users" is speech. "User count exceeded 10,000.
  The team has three people." is a report with a hanging sentence tacked on.
- Short sentences, but not a stack of them: connect thoughts with "because", "so", "but". Active voice.
  Everyday words. Contractions.
- Include the real mess when it's true ("I couldn't find one, so I'm building it"). Never invent an anecdote or
  a number.
- Take a clear position. No hedging, no "might be worth considering".
- Specific beats grand: numbers, names, a real example. "Everything you'd want and more" says nothing.
- Honest about the state of things. Never oversell; say what's true today.
- Match the world you're writing for. Game or story copy keeps its own register (plain medieval for a medieval
  game, for example), never modern slang or purple fantasy.

## Never

- Vague titles that name a genre instead of a payoff ("World Simulation Game", "Productivity App").
- Comparisons that invite the wrong fight (trademarks, "like X but better").
- Wrong facts. Check every number and name against the source.
- Personal contact details (the user's email, phone) unless they asked for them in the piece.
- Markdown in a post or DM (no headers, no bold). Newsletters are the exception.
- Hanging sentences: a lone fact tacked on at the end ("The company is five years old.", "It started in
  2021."). Fold it into the sentence it belongs to.
- Lines about how the product works in user-facing copy ("the engine adapts", "the AI remembers"). Say only
  what the user gets.
- Opening with "I made..." or "We built...". Start where the reader is.
- Lesson or tip endings: "Why it matters: ...", "So check what it says about you", "The lesson: ...". Let the
  facts speak.

## Newsletters

A structure that works (in the style of short daily tech newsletters):

- One plain opener: "Hey, here's what happened in <topic> this week."
- Five stories at most. Each one: a bold headline that carries the news and its number ("A three-person team
  doubled to 20,000 users in six months"), then two bullets of plain facts, said the way you'd say them, with
  the source linked on a short phrase.
- Nothing after the bullets: no "why it matters", no takeaway, no tip.
- Pure value. No selling, no ask, no CTA. Sign off with a one-line "see you next week" and the sender's name.
- About one phone scroll. Every number checked against its source, news from the period you name only.

## Community posts (Reddit and similar)

- Goal is honest replies, not upvotes. Lead with the reader's itch, not the product.
- Say plainly you're building it. Hidden promotion kills the thread.
- End on one question that's easy to answer from experience.

## Slop pass (run last, every piece)

Search the draft and fix every hit:

- **Em dashes.** None. Use a comma, a period or parentheses.
- **Shapes:** "not X, it's Y" and "not just X, but Y"; colon reveals ("The best part: it learns"); stacked
  punchy fragments; default threes; faux-insight setups ("what most people get wrong"); cute lines that say
  nothing; recap endings ("In short", "Overall").
- **Words:** delve, leverage, utilize, seamless, robust, unlock, empower, elevate, harness, supercharge,
  game changer, cutting-edge, landscape (figurative), testament, tapestry.
- **Weasel sources** ("experts agree", "studies show"): name the source or cut the line.
- **Portability test:** a sentence that could move unchanged to another product is filler. Replace it with a
  fact, a mechanism or a consequence specific to this one.
- **Out loud test:** read it once at speaking speed. Rewrite anything you stumble on or wouldn't say.
