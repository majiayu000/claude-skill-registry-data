# AIWF Startup Finance And Funding

## Core Rule

Keep operating math separate from financing eligibility and regulated offering decisions. Show inputs, formulas, timing, uncertainty, dilution or repayment implications, and the evidence still needed.

## Workflow

1. Establish entity, location, stage, revenue model, current cash, committed obligations, monthly inflows/outflows, founder goals, and timing.
2. Build base, downside, and upside cash cases. Use signed contracts separately from pipeline or hoped-for revenue.
3. Calculate gross margin and unit economics only when numerator, denominator, cohort, and period are defined.
4. Match capital type to use of funds, repayment capacity, collateral, traction, growth profile, control preferences, and deadline.
5. Build a diligence list: financial statements, cap table, ownership/IP, contracts, security/privacy evidence, forecasts, use of funds, and milestone plan.
6. Verify current program rules, offering limits, eligibility, deadlines, and filing duties from official sources at answer time.
7. Present options with cost, speed, control, risk, prerequisites, and next gate. Never imply approval or funding certainty.

Use `python scripts/calculate_runway.py --input <finance.json> --output <result.json>` for deterministic runway scenarios. Read `references/source-register.json` before current funding claims.

## Boundaries

- US-first; identify state, industry, and investor-type questions that require qualified legal, tax, or accounting review.
- Do not provide personalized securities, investment, tax, or legal advice, value a company as fact, or draft deceptive financial projections.
- Do not contact funders, submit applications, open accounts, accept terms, or publish an offering without explicit approval.
- Treat grants as competitive programs with eligibility and compliance duties, not free guaranteed money.
- Add `aiwf-data-privacy-protection` and relevant security skills for investor or enterprise diligence evidence.

## Output

Return assumptions, scenario table, capital options, diligence gaps, official-source checks, decision gates, and a concrete next-action list.
