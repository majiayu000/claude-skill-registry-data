# Commodity Gate

**The premise**: AI made the software a commodity. The moat is what only you can say.

If your copy could appear under a competitor's logo with no other change, it's commodity copy. Don't ship it.

## When to run

Before these reach the inbox, the page, or the queue:

- LinkedIn / X / Threads posts
- Email outreach (cold or warm)
- Homepage, pricing, comparison pages
- Ad copy
- Video scripts
- Lead magnets

Skip for internal-only work (agent prompts, engineering notes, SQL queries).

## The three checks

Pass all three or rewrite.

### 1. Opinion check

> Can a generic CRM, PR platform, or competitor say this without lying?

**Pass**: "Sending happens after explicit per-message human approval. No batch sends."

**Fail**: "AI-powered campaign management." (every tool says this)

### 2. Primitive check

> Does the copy use a brand-owned term, or introduce a new one?

Brand-owned primitives should be specific, named, repeatable. Generic terms (pipeline, dashboard, insights, automation) don't count.

### 3. Founder voice check (cafe test)

> Could the founder say this sentence aloud in conversation?

**Pass**: "Five years of spreadsheets. I thought I had a system."

**Fail**: "Many professionals struggle with disorganised contact data."

Check:
- Is it specific to a real moment, campaign, or customer? (or generic?)
- Is the subject "I / we" or "this product / users"?
- Would Chris on a quiet Tuesday write this sentence about his own work?

## Workflow

1. Read the draft
2. Score against the three checks: PASS / FAIL per check
3. If any FAIL, rewrite that section. Don't ship the original.
4. If all three PASS, ship.

## Output style

Terse. One line per check: PASS / FAIL + reason.

**Example**:

```
Email: cold outreach to Brighton cafe owner
- Opinion check:   PASS — no generic CRM could offer 15-minute AI workflow audit
- Primitive check: PASS — "15-minute audit" is brand-owned service
- Voice check:     PASS — first-person, specific to Brighton cafe workflow
Verdict: SHIP
```

## Anti-patterns this catches

- Popular LinkedIn carousel templates that "perform" but say nothing brand-specific
- Pricing copy that sounds like every SaaS pricing page
- Cold emails any founder could send
- AI-generated body copy with no names, dates, or numbers
