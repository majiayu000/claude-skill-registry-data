## Phase 2 — Plan the Week Before Writing

Before drafting any content, produce a brief **week plan** (do not output to the user yet — this is internal):

1. **This week's position in the arc**: What came before (prior week's theme, where the learner is emotionally/theologically), what this week must accomplish, what comes next
2. **Core tension**: The productive dissonance this week creates — what assumption does it unsettle?
3. **Core concept**: The theological reality this week recovers or awakens (not a topic to cover)
4. **Christocentric anchor**: How Jesus / Lord / Kingdom / Gospel is the spine of this week
5. **Action domain**: What concrete life-domain the action step enters
6. **Cohort conversation target**: What the group session needs to surface
7. **Argument pattern**: Which of the three patterns (A, B, or C) best serves the teaching — or a combination

## Phase 3 — Draft All Sections (Transformation Loop Order)

Draft each section in the canonical order below. For Weeks 2-7, this is:

### Section 1: Opening Video Script (`video`)
- **Word count:** 600-900 words (~5 min at ~145 wpm)
- **Structure:** Hook → concept → grounding → learner implication → closing question
- **Voice:** Conversational, direct, first-person. Alan to camera.
- **Rules:** This is NOT a lecture. NOT a summary of the reading. The video frames; the reading unpacks. End with a question that creates anticipation.
- **Argument pattern:** Usually Pattern B (Story → Tension → Scripture → Resolution → Application) or a compressed Pattern A

### Section 2: Dissonance Prompt (`chat_dissonance`)
- **Word count:** 200-350 words
- **Structure:** Tension statement (2-3 sentences) + question (1-2 sentences)
- **Voice:** Feels like Alan asking a hard question across a table. Intimate, unsettling, warm.
- **Rules:** Do NOT resolve the tension. Do NOT welcome or orient. This is a productive disruption that opens the learner before they encounter the teaching.

### Section 3: Main Teaching (`reading`)
- **Word count:** 2,000-3,500 words
- **Structure:** Opening hook → framework presentation → scriptural grounding → implications → application preview
- **Substructure:** 2-3 subheadings. 1-2 blockquotes from Alan's books or Scripture.
- **Voice:** Full Alan Hirsch — all five markers at full strength. This is the theological and pedagogical core of the week.
- **Rules:** Must advance the course's overarching narrative. Every paragraph earns its place by feeding dissonance, action, or cohort. Hold productive tension — do not close the loop in prose. Leave room for AI companion, field practice, and cohort.
- **Argument pattern:** Choose from Pattern A, B, or C based on the week plan. Patterns can nest.

### Section 3a: Scripture Element (`scripture`) — Week 3 only
- **Word count:** 300-500 words
- **Structure:** Scripture passage + theological unpacking + connection to the week's concept
- **Rules:** Week 3 includes this because "Jesus Is Lord" demands the scriptural weight of the Shema. Scripture is woven into argument, not proof-texted. This section sits between the main teaching and the case study.

### Section 4: Case Study / Witness (`case_study`)
- **Word count:** 300-600 words
- **Structure:** Setup → story → connection to concept → implication
- **Voice:** Narrative, concrete, specific. Not a summary of the teaching — a story that *shows* the concept at work.
- **Source preference:** Early church, Chinese underground church, Celtic movement, SMRC, Alan's ministry stories. Must be grounded in known historical examples from the corpus.

### Section 5: Action Step Prompt (`chat_action`)
- **Word count:** 150-250 words
- **Voice:** AI companion prompt. Warm, direct, concrete.
- **Rules:** One step. Time-boxed (this week). Named to someone (who will they tell or do this with?). Not a to-do list — a formation step.

### Section 6: Reflection Prompt (`chat_reflection`)
- **Word count:** 150-250 words
- **Voice:** AI companion prompt. Contemplative, honest, gentle.
- **Rules:** After the learner has acted. What happened? What got in the way? What do they want to carry forward? Gospel light on failure — grace, not guilt.

### Section 7: Cohort Meeting (`discussion`)
- **Word count:** 200-400 words
- **Structure:** Theme + opening question + 2-3 key discussion questions + closing practice
- **Voice:** Facilitator guide. Clear, warm, structured.
- **Rules:** Must create genuine shared risk. Not a book club discussion — a formation conversation.

### Section 8: Exit Ticket (`reflection`)
- **Word count:** 150-250 words
- **Structure:** Three response options (cognitive takeaway, emotional response, commitment) + brief preview of next week
- **Voice:** Brief, invitational, forward-looking.

## Phase 4 — Voice Audit (Mandatory Before Delivery)

After drafting all sections, run a self-audit on every section. Check:

### Five Voice Markers
| Marker | Weight | Min | Check |
|--------|--------|-----|-------|
| Christocentric Anchoring | 30% | ≥0.7 | 2-3 Jesus/Christ/Kingdom/Gospel references per substantial section |
| Pastoral Warmth | 20% | ≥0.5 | We/you/I balance (~45/35/20); relational, invitational |
| Narrative Imagery | 15% | ≥0.6 | ~8.5 metaphors per 1000 words; movement/DNA, organic, journey |
| Theological Depth | 10% | ≥0.7 | Scripture woven; historical examples; frameworks named |
| Prophetic Intensity | 25% | 0.5-0.8 | Questions, urgency, challenge — balanced with warmth |

### Failure Mode Scan
- [ ] No corporate consultant language ("leverage," "optimize," "scalable," "best practices")
- [ ] No detached academic voice ("research suggests," "implications indicate")
- [ ] No antithesis patterns ("not X, but Y" / "rather than X" / "instead of X")
- [ ] No rushing to practice before theological grounding
- [ ] No homiletical mode / stacked proof-texts
- [ ] No generic motivational language
- [ ] Christocentric anchor present in every section (automatic failure if missing)
- [ ] First-person narrative present where appropriate

### Structural Checks
- [ ] All sections present in correct order
- [ ] Word counts within targets
- [ ] Dissonance actually creates tension (not a welcome message)
- [ ] Teaching advances the course narrative (not just covers a topic)
- [ ] Case study shows concept at work (not summarizes the teaching)
- [ ] Action step is concrete, time-boxed, and doable in 7 days
- [ ] Reflection looks backward at action taken
- [ ] Cohort meeting creates genuine shared risk
- [ ] Exit ticket offers three response types + next week preview

### Formation Loop Integrity
- [ ] Each section serves its role in the loop — dissonance unsettles, teaching grounds, witness shows, action moves, reflection integrates, cohort holds, exit sends forward
- [ ] Productive tension is maintained — the teaching does not resolve everything; room remains for AI companion, field practice, and cohort
- [ ] The Christocentric spine is visible across all sections

If any marker falls below minimum or any failure mode is detected, revise before delivering.
