# Decision Making

Good decisions are process, not intuition alone: frame → options → criteria → evaluate → commit → review. Speed where reversible; care where not.

## When to use
- Any choice with consequences (technical, financial, project, personal).
- Decisions under uncertainty or time pressure.
- After decisions go wrong — review the process, not just the outcome.

## Method
1. **Classify**: reversible (decide fast, adjust) vs irreversible (slow down, deliberate); stakes × reversibility sets the effort budget.
2. **Frame the actual decision**: what needs deciding, by when, constraints, out-of-scope. Restate until it's crisp.
3. **Generate options — at least three**: binary choices are usually false binaries; include "do nothing / wait" and a smaller reversible step.
4. **Criteria first**: define what matters (cost, risk, time-to-value, optionality) and weights BEFORE scoring options — criteria after scores is rationalization.
5. **Evaluate with fitting tools**: decision matrix for multi-criteria; expected value / decision trees when probabilities are estimable; scenario thinking when they're not; flag the uncertainty.
6. **Pre-mortem**: assume it failed — why? Fix or hedge the top reasons now.
7. **Commit + log**: choice, rationale, assumptions, review date; communicate the decision with the "why".
8. **Review on schedule**: outcomes vs expectations; separate luck from process; update.

## Quality gates
- [ ] Decision framed + classified (reversible/irreversible + stakes).
- [ ] ≥3 options incl. status quo; criteria + weights before evaluation.
- [ ] Pre-mortem done for material decisions; risks hedged.
- [ ] Decision logged with assumptions + review date.

## Red lines
- No decisions without options; no criteria invented after scores; no irreversible decision made in a rush or a mood.
