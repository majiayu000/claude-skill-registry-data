# Management Deck Agent

## When to use
Use this when a company's management team must present its equity story to prospective buyers during a sale process, whether in person or by video. The management presentation is often the first live impression buyers form, so the narrative arc, page flow, and delivery all matter. This agent structures the deck and the words behind it.

## What it does
It produces a management presentation outline and talk track: a page-by-page flow that carries the equity story from company overview to financial outlook, plus the spoken narrative for each section and the questions management should be ready to field.

## Method
1. Frame the equity story spine before any pages.
   - Answer four things: what the company is, why it wins, why now, and why this is the buyer's opportunity.
   - Every page must serve that spine; cut any page that does not.

2. Open with a company overview.
   - Cover what the business does, the products and customers, geographic footprint, and headcount.
   - Land two or three headline facts that anchor everything after, for example scale, retention, or margin.

3. Present the market.
   - Give size, growth drivers, and structure, and place the company within it.
   - Common pitfall: inflated market claims; keep it credible and framed as the company's own view.

4. Build the competitive positioning.
   - Name the durable advantages: customer relationships, technology, cost position, brand.
   - Show the evidence that each advantage holds, not just the assertion.

5. Lay out the growth strategy.
   - Detail specific initiatives, the investment behind each, and the expected contribution.
   - Distinguish committed momentum from upside optionality so buyers can underwrite each differently.

6. Move to financials.
   - Show historical performance with a clean explanation of trajectory and margins, then the forward plan with key assumptions stated plainly.
   - Make the bridge from today to the forecast explicit, since buyers underwrite the plan, not the hope.

7. Write the talk track per page.
   - Give the two or three sentences the presenter says, who leads each section, and the transition into the next page.
   - Good looks like: a presenter who can deliver the page without reading it.

8. Anticipate the hard questions.
   - Prepare for customer concentration, margin sustainability, management depth, capex needs, and forecast credibility.
   - Draft a crisp, evidenced answer for each rather than a defensive one.

9. Close with the investment highlights.
   - Recap why this is a compelling opportunity, echoing the spine from step one.

## Inputs
- The company overview: products, customers, footprint, headcount
- Historical financials and the forward business plan with key assumptions
- The growth initiatives and the investment behind them
- Known buyer concerns or sensitivities
- The management team roster and who presents which section
- Any market data the company is comfortable citing as its own view

## Output format
- Page flow: an ordered list of pages, each with a title, three to five content points, the presenter, and a short talk track of the spoken words.
- Anticipated questions: each likely buyer question paired with a drafted, evidenced answer.
- Investment highlights: a short closing recap echoing the equity-story spine.
- Present all of this as structured prose, never a rendered markdown table.

## Example
Northwind Logistics management presentation. Page 3, Market: title "A large and consolidating freight market," points on market size, the structural shift to outsourced logistics, and fragmentation among regional carriers, presented by the CEO, talk track "The market we serve is large and still consolidating, which plays directly to our scale advantage." Page 6, Growth: title "Three funded routes to expand," splitting two committed new sites from an optional bolt-on program. Anticipated question: "Your top customer is 22% of revenue, how at risk is that?" Drafted answer: "That relationship runs under a five-year contract signed last year, with volumes up 8% and two new sites added since." Investment highlights recap: scaled network, contracted revenue base, and a funded growth plan.
