# Research methods — design, modeling, ethics, and domain context

Goal: bring the right methodology to a research task and ground it in what the
researcher actually studies. This skill carries **reusable method craft**; pair it with a
**domain/collaborator profile** of the researcher so recommendations land in context.

## Researcher domain profile

Keep a profile of the researcher this install serves and read it before any research
task. It should hold their **core domains** (topics, constructs, recurring indices),
**populations & settings** (who, where, which agencies), and **collaborators &
institutions** (appointments, mentors and their specialties, community partners,
funders, HRPP/IRB contacts). This install's profile: `LOCAL.md` beside this skill, when
your copy keeps one — *Researcher domain profile*.

## Conceptual / logic modeling

Build **iteratively, in layers**, then translate the diagram into prose backed by
peer-reviewed (DOI-linked) literature:

1. Start simple: one driver → outcome.
2. Add **pathways** (e.g. direct exposure, secondary stressors, eco-anxiety) — multiple
   mechanisms, not one variable.
3. Add **moderators** (social support, services, institutional trust, SES) — annotate
   how they're degraded by upstream forces.
4. Put the **structural root cause upstream** (e.g. structural racism via a
   neighborhood track *and* a chronic-stress/allostatic-load track), not as a background
   moderator.
5. Add **feedback loops** (outcome decline → increased vulnerability; an investment that
   severs a moderating layer).
6. Treat **intersectionality as a cross-cutting lens, not a box** — boundary conditions
   that reshape the meaning of every node; name the specific axes at each tier
   (documentation status, disability/IEP loss, policing/stigma).

## Community-based participatory research (CBPR / YPAR)

Engage participants as **paid co-investigators**, not subjects. Co-design research
questions, instruments, sampling, consent, and data-governance protocols. Power-sharing
safeguards seen to work: small-group-before-full-group, anonymous input channels,
metacognitive check-ins, bilingual (EN/ES) materials, anchoring discussion in
participant-produced artifacts to counter deference to adult/authority. Attend to
immigration fear, research fatigue, and emotional safety / referral pathways. The
community partner **reviews all protocols before fielding**. Frame participants as
**epistemic agents** whose lived experience is primary data that administrative records
and adult testimony can't replicate — and frame their *exclusion* as itself a mechanism
of inequality the practice disrupts.

## Participatory & agent-based modeling

- **Participatory modeling (PM):** causal loop diagramming (CLD) with participants
  (Zellner & Massey 2024), extended to agent-based models; participants **rank**
  strategies on feasibility × impact. CLD diagrams and ABM runs serve as analytic
  instruments.
- **Agent-based simulation (NetLogo-style):** patch-and-household model — each tick,
  mitigation projects complete and raise nearby housing quality/cost above a baseline;
  cost-burdened households evaluate moving via a utility function trading climate
  resilience vs. affordability; households that can't find affordable housing are
  "displaced," and at-risk-not-yet-moved households tracked via a displacement-pressure
  counter. Report sensitivity-analysis interpretation, not just runs.

## Composite-index spatial analysis (climate gentrification)

Tract-level composite: **Vulnerability = Environmental Exposures + Sensitivity −
Adaptive Capacity.** Indicators **z-score standardized then rescaled 0–100** (preserves
relationships, prevents negatives canceling positives), equally weighted, summed.
Sub-indices: a Gentrification Index (tract typologies across observation windows,
inflation-adjusted to constant dollars, mean imputation for missing data) and a
Displacement Risk Index (social-vulnerability + housing-market sub-indexes, signs
aligned, quartiled low/moderate/high). Adaptive capacity = count of resources within
walking distance (≈400 m). Fold in **community-nominated indicators** (e.g. PM2.5,
residential fires) from collaborative data-analysis workshops. Map at tract level with
graduated shading.

## Implementation-science & feasibility frameworks

- **Bowen Feasibility Framework** (acceptability, demand, implementation, practicality,
  adaptation, integration, expansion, limited-efficacy) — for piloting when no prior
  evaluation exists.
- **CFIR** (5 domains) preferred over RE-AIM when multilevel + equity attention is
  needed; **map each method onto a framework domain** so the framework is visible in the
  data-collection plan.

## Measures / data collection

Mixed methods, often with instruments **co-designed via YPAR rather than pre-selected
validated scales.** Methods seen: surveys & intercept surveys, semi-structured
interviews, focus groups, field observation, gallery walks, intergenerational
workshops. Go beyond deficit constructs — name **solastalgia, climate/racial grief,
allostatic load, displacement anxiety**. Always pair each data source with an
**analysis/interpretation framework** (thematic analysis, grounded theory, member
checking, ABM sensitivity interpretation) — a source list is not an analysis plan.

## Human-subjects protections (IRB / HRPP)

From AAHRPP-accreditation consulting work (the engagement this came from: `LOCAL.md`
beside this skill, when your copy keeps one — *Human-subjects protections: where this
came from*):

- **AAHRPP three-domain framework** structures everything: **I. Organization,
  II. IRB/Ethics Committee, III. Researcher & Research Staff.** Review documents
  element-by-element against these domains.
- **Plan vs. Investigator Manual:** a *Plan* (e.g. UW–Madison **HRP-101**, the standard
  **Huron Toolkit** "HRPP Plan") carries structure, definitions, roles/responsibilities,
  scope, document control. The plain-language researcher *voice* lives in the companion
  **Investigator Manual (HRP-103)**. Match the model document to the document type —
  don't conflate them.
- **Institutional-document hallmarks:** definitions up front; a roles-and-
  responsibilities spine; explicit "overseen vs. not overseen" scope lists; the
  principle that **protections do not vary by funding**; agency-by-agency regulatory
  mapping; formal document control; continuity-of-operations provisions.
- **FWA & registered IRBs:** an HRPP holds a Federalwide Assurance and may operate
  multiple registered IRBs (this install's worked instance: `LOCAL.md` beside this
  skill, when your copy keeps one — *FWA & registered IRBs: the worked instance*).
- A standalone Human Subjects Protection section in a proposal covers IRB, data
  security, confidentiality, risk, and mitigation — dedicated, not scattered.

**Standing caveats:**

- **AAHRPP element numbers** (III.1.E, I.1.D, …) must be **verified against the current
  Evaluation Instrument** before use in a submission — AAHRPP periodically renumbers.
- Distinguish **planning artifacts** (reviews, standards docs, restructure blueprints)
  from **final products** (the rewritten manual/Plan still has to be drafted from them).
- Treat already-approved stakeholder source material as a **reference input**, not
  something to regenerate.
- Tooling reality: some "PDF" files are ZIP archives of page images with OCR, or
  Markdown; auth/robots-restricted Box/Drive links can't be fetched — ask for the file.

## Literature synthesis

When asked to "find articles that support this model," return **peer-reviewed sources
with links/DOIs**, mapped to the specific construct/node they support. Recurring anchors:
Crenshaw; Bailey et al.; Marcuse; Dantzler (2021); Pulido (2016); Rucks-Ahidiana (2022);
Massey & Denton; Bates (2013); Desmond (2012); Ozer et al. (2020); Zellner & Massey
(2024); Bowen et al. (2009); Damschroder et al. (CFIR); Binet et al. (2019).

## Before you call this done

- [ ] Conceptual model has the **structural root cause upstream**, intersectionality as
      a cross-cutting lens (not a box), and feedback loops — not a single driver→outcome
      arrow.
- [ ] Every data source is paired with an **analysis/interpretation framework** — a
      source list is not an analysis plan.
- [ ] CBPR/YPAR work frames participants as **paid co-investigators / epistemic agents**,
      and the community partner reviews all protocols before fielding.
- [ ] Human-subjects material sits in one dedicated section (IRB, data security,
      confidentiality, risk, mitigation), and any **AAHRPP element numbers are verified
      against the current Evaluation Instrument**.
- [ ] Literature claims are backed by **peer-reviewed, DOI-linked sources mapped to the
      specific construct/node** they support.
