# The Copywriting Rulebook

Distilled from *The Copywriter's Handbook* (Robert W. Bly) and *Ogilvy on Advertising* (David Ogilvy). The source examples target professional-services websites; the principles are general. Apply them to any copy whose job is to sell: a landing page, a one-liner, an investor narrative, a cold email, a CTA.

**One rule above all: copy is salesmanship in print.** Its job is to move the reader to a decision, not to entertain or impress. If a sentence does not sell, cut it. Grade the copy on the response it earns, never on originality.

## Your facts, not the book's

The book examples (sample stats, testimonials, guarantees) are illustrations. Every claim in the copy must come
from the user's own product, data or customers. Look for them first: the product's docs and release notes, the
site, analytics, reviews, support threads, case studies. If proof is missing, ask the user for it or leave the
claim out. Never invent a number, a quote or a guarantee.

## Before writing (non-negotiable)

Research beats talent. Ogilvy studied Rolls-Royce for three weeks before the headline. Do the equivalent:

1. **Know the reader's world:** their job, their fears, their desires, the words they use for their own problem. Their language becomes your copy.
2. **Know the competition:** what everyone else claims. Your USP lives in the gap they leave open.
3. **Define ONE objective:** the single action this piece must produce. Every element serves it.
4. **List each feature → translate to a benefit** before drafting.

## Positioning

Make one specific, unique proposition the competition cannot or does not make. Template:

> We give [audience] [specific deliverable] so that [specific outcome]. Unlike [competitor approach], we [differentiator].

Every piece must reinforce the same brand personality. Inconsistent voice destroys the brand.

## Headlines (80% of the work)

5x more people read the headline than the body. Test every headline against **Bly's Four U's**:

- **Urgent:** a reason to act now (cost of inaction, time, scarcity).
- **Unique:** something no competitor is saying.
- **Ultra-specific:** precise numbers, not vague promises ("the electric clock at 60mph," not "it's quiet").
- **Useful:** a benefit the reader actually wants, not a feature.

**Eight types that work:** Direct (state the benefit) · Indirect (curiosity) · News · How-To · Question · Command · Reason-Why (numbered) · Testimonial.

**Ogilvy's commandments:** include the selling promise · appeal to self-interest · avoid negatives (say what you do) · no puns or cleverness · include the brand name · be specific.

## Body copy

**Bly's 4S:** Short (sentences + paragraphs) · Simple (everyday words, a 12-year-old understands it) · Specific (facts, not generalities) · Succinct (say it once, move on).

**Ogilvy's rules:** open in ≤11 words · subhead every 2-3 paragraphs · write to ONE person as "you" · make the truth fascinating with specifics, not superlatives · never be boring · use story (problem → journey → result) · long copy sells a complex product if no word is wasted.

**Benefits over features is the master rule.** "Nobody buys drill bits. They buy holes." For every feature, answer "so what does this get the reader?" and write that instead.

## Structure that converts

**AIDA:** Attention (stop the scanner) → Interest (expand the promise with facts) → Desire (paint life after) → Action (exactly what to do next).

**Bly's 5-step motivating sequence:** 1) lead with the biggest pain · 2) prove you understand it · 3) present the solution · 4) prove it works (evidence) · 5) ask for action, low-risk.

**Why people buy** (alternate positive motivators and pain-avoidance): make money · save time · be secure · be respected · be right · out of fear.

## Proof (no claim without evidence)

Ranked by power: **case studies** (before → intervention → after, with metrics) > **named, specific testimonials** > **data/statistics** > **credentials** > **guarantees / risk-reversal** > **client logos**.

**Specificity is the engine of credibility.** "63% more enquiries within 6 months across 47 firms" beats "great results." Every vague claim → replace with a number, a timeframe, or a name.

## Style craft

- "You"/"your" outnumber "we" by ≥2:1.
- 14-16 words per sentence average; vary the rhythm.
- Paragraphs 1-4 sentences. One-sentence paragraphs for punch.
- Fragments for emphasis. Start sentences with And/But/So for flow.
- No jargon, no buzzwords, no superlatives, no analogies Ogilvy would say get misread.
- Subheads are scannable mini-headlines (many readers read only those).
- End every section with a transition or a CTA. Never a dead end.

**Words that sell:** Free · New/Now/Introducing · How-to/Discover · You/Your · Proven/Guaranteed/Results · Save/Increase/Boost/Grow · Because/Here's why · Only/Exclusive.

## Calls to action

Specific ("Get your free ranking report," not "Contact us") · action verbs (Get/Book/Start/Claim) · low-risk (free, no obligation) · urgent · repeated (hero, mid-page, end).

## The cardinal sins (never)

Writing about yourself before the reader's problem · empty superlatives · clever over clear · burying the lead · weak CTAs ("learn more") · claims without proof · feature-dumping · inconsistent voice · walls of text.

## Review checklist

- **Strategy:** one objective? audience-specific? USP clear?
- **Headline:** promises a benefit? passes 4U? would a busy reader stop? no cleverness?
- **Body:** hooks in ≤11 words? every feature a benefit? second person? short sentences and paragraphs? no jargon? claims specific?
- **Proof:** named and specific? at least one proof point? risk-reversal?
- **CTA:** specific? repeated? action verb? low-risk? urgent?
