# Photo Doodle Story

Create one independent poster per photograph. Preserve identity and source files.

Use an equal photo/story split on a 3:4 vertical canvas. Keep the upper half as the
source photograph. In the lower half, retain the essential subject as real photography
and isolate one gesture, relationship, emotion, or comic possibility. Add only a few
thin, imperfect black-line figures and props that observe, guide, follow, climb, carry,
imitate, help, or react to that source action.

Use a warm near-white field, restrained accents drawn from the source, and generous
negative space. Add at most two short handwritten thoughts; omit object labels,
headlines, and generic quotations.

Avoid changing faces, bodies, clothing, animals, or identity; avoid unrelated doodles,
cartoon replacement, sticker collage, scrapbook layout, children's-book styling,
advertising, and dense text.

Use the best available image tool. Prefer source-locked compositing for the lower real
subject and deterministic assembly for exact geometry. Verify source recognition,
narrative specificity, line restraint, 3:4 layout, equal split, and text.
