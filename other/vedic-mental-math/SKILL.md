# Vedic Mental Math

A system of 16 sutras (aphorisms) and sub-sutras for fast mental arithmetic, popularized by Bharati Krishna Tirtha. Excellent for quick calculation, estimation, and cross-checking.

## Honest note on origins
The claim that these methods are "Vedic" (from the Vedas) is **academically disputed** — scholarly analyses (e.g. Vasantha Kandasamy & Smarandache) argue they are clever modern arithmetic rather than ancient Vedic content. Use them as **effective mental-math techniques**; don't oversell the historical provenance.

## Key sutras & what they do
- **Ekadhikena Purvena** ("by one more than the one before") — square numbers ending in 5: e.g. 75² = (7x8) | 25 = **5625**. Also recurring-decimal tricks.
- **Nikhilam Navatashcaramam Dashatah** ("all from 9, the last from 10") — multiply numbers near a base: 97x96 via deficits from 100 (-3, -4) -> 93 | 12 = **9312**.
- **Urdhva-Tiryagbhyam** ("vertically and crosswise") — the workhorse: multiply any two numbers in one line.
- **Paravartya Yojayet** ("transpose and adjust") — division and solving equations.
- **Yavadunam** — square numbers near a base.
- **Sankalana-Vyavakalanabhyam** ("by addition and by subtraction").
- **Anurupyena** — proportionality for multiplication/division.
- Plus sub-sutras (Antyayordashake'pi, Vestanam, etc.) for special cases.

## Practical use
Squaring (ending in 5, or near a base), multiplying near a base or crosswise, quick division, recurring decimals, and fast estimation / sanity-checks of larger computations.

## For Nrupal
Handy for quick checks in quant and engineering work and as a practiced mental skill. **Always cross-check high-stakes figures with `quant-and-applied-math`** — mental tricks are for speed, not for high-precision finality.

## Anti-patterns to refuse
- Using a memorized trick without understanding why it works; relying on mental shortcuts for high-stakes precision without verification; overstating the "ancient Vedic" claim.
