# Strategic brief

A brief written for the reader's chair (president, owner, operating partner) about a company that
a review-driven small-business report can't serve.

## When to use

- Large, enterprise or multi-state; a **subsidiary of a PE or public parent**.
- **B2B** (contractor, utility, infrastructure, industrial, software) with few or non-consumer
  reviews.
- No credible sector benchmark exists, and inventing one would read as fluff to a buyer who
  underwrites for a living.
- The point is to show what reasoning over public data can do, then open a conversation.

## The framing (read before writing a word)

- **Public data + reasoning only.** No internal figures. Say so on the cover. That constraint *is*
  the credibility; one machine-guessed number destroys trust.
- **Altitude = the reader's chair.** For a $200M subsidiary: capital allocation, the parent's
  strategy, the demand cycle, labour, competitive position. Not small-shop operating tips.
- **Lead with the single sharpest insight**, the central tension, as the headline.
- **Close with a brainstorm, never a pitch.** End on "Where do you want to take this next?" plus a
  few real questions you'd dig into together. No product names, no prices.
- Quantify only where the public math is honest. Range and label estimates ("~$160M-$215M,
  outside-in estimate"); keep observed facts (counts, ratings, filings) exact.

## Pipeline

1. **Resolve the real entity.** Directory lookups often return a renamed or acquired company.
   Confirm the true entity, its **parent**, scale, history and actual owner before researching
   (grounded search: "<name> <city> history founders parent revenue employees").
2. **Gather the public corpus.** Several grounded searches: the company; **the parent's stated
   strategy** (10-K, investor deck, earnings calls); the **sector demand cycle** (funding programs,
   backlog, pricing); **named public competitors** as proxies; the **labour market**. If reviews
   exist, decode them: strip employee and internal noise, keep the repeating public themes.
3. **Find the strategic spine.** One tension the public record reveals that the reader feels but
   hasn't framed. Example shape: the parent is rotating capital toward recurring revenue while this
   subsidiary is a one-off project business riding a temporary, grant-funded demand bulge.
   Everything hangs off that. Reason; don't pad.
4. **Draft** in plain language: a hook lead, 3-5 moves or sections, a one-frame summary table, 1-2
   deep-dive findings with a concrete action each, and the brainstorm close. If someone is reviewing,
   show the draft before branding; branding is the slow step.
5. **Brand by hand.** Author the HTML yourself (cover, hook block, finding cards, summary table,
   recommendation cards, closing Q&A). Do not push the draft through a generic "report brander"
   prompt: brander passes have been seen reformatting the hook away and inventing numbers.
6. **Render** to PDF (WeasyPrint or headless Chrome) and check every page.

## Done when

- The PDF renders untruncated.
- **Zero fabricated numbers**: every figure public and sourced, estimates ranged and labelled.
- The hook leads; the brainstorm close is there; no product or price language.
- It reads at the buyer's altitude.
