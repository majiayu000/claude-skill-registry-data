# draft-message

## First, establish which of these it is

| Kind | Sent? | Written for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Unsent letter** | Never | Getting it out. Written to the person, not for them. |
| **Message** | Yes | To be read by someone who will react. |
| **Script** | Spoken | A conversation they're dreading — openings, and what to do if it goes sideways. |
| **Formal** | Yes, on the record | HR, a landlord, a resignation, a complaint. Different register entirely. |

Ask. The same content in the wrong mode does real damage — an unsent letter that gets sent is
the classic failure here, and it's worth saying so once when drafting one.

## Procedure

1. **What outcome do they want?** Not "what do you want to say" — what do they want to be true
   afterwards. If the honest answer is "I want them to feel bad", that's allowed; it just means
   the piece is probably an unsent letter, not a message.
2. **What can't go unsaid,** and what would they regret saying. Both lists.
3. **Draft.** Their register, not yours. If they write in short blunt sentences, so does the
   draft. Length matched to the medium: a WhatsApp message is not five paragraphs.
4. **Mark the risky parts.** Anything likely to escalate, be quoted back, or be read differently
   than intended gets flagged in a comment beneath the draft — not silently softened.
5. **Offer one alternative version** at a different temperature, and say what each is likely to
   get. Two options, not five.
6. **Sit on it.** For anything to be sent: recommend leaving it overnight and re-reading before
   sending. Save to `artefacts/drafts/YYMMDD-<slug>.md`, and note explicitly whether it was sent.

## Formal messages

Register changes completely: dated, factual, chronological, no interpretation of motive, no
emotional content, one ask stated plainly at the end. Pull the facts from
`artefacts/timeline.md` so dates are right and citable.

If it touches employment law, tenancy, family law or anything with a deadline, say once and
plainly that a person with local qualifications should see it before it goes. Then help with the
draft anyway — an unsent unwritten complaint helps nobody.

## Rules

- **Never send anything.** You draft; they send. No mail tools, no messaging tools, no exceptions,
  even if they ask — this is the one place where a mistake can't be taken back.
- **Never sand off the edges without saying so.** If the draft is materially softer than what
  they said, tell them that's what you did and why.
- **An unsent letter stays unsent.** Save it in `artefacts/drafts/` with `sent: never` in the
  frontmatter, and don't offer to adapt it into a real message in the same session.
- **Don't write threats, ultimatums or anything designed to hurt**, including on request. Offer
  the unsent-letter version instead, which is what that impulse usually actually wants.
- If they're drafting to someone who has been abusive or coercive, slow down: ask what happens
  after it's sent, and whether they have somewhere to be if it goes badly.
