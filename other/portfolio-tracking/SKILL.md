# Portfolio Tracking & Attribution

You can't manage what you don't record — or learn from what you flatter. One ledger of truth, honest performance math, chosen-upfront benchmarks, and reports that never leak identity.

## When to use
- Recording trades, dividends, contributions/withdrawals.
- Monthly/quarterly performance review and attribution.
- Preparing reports (for yourself or advisors) — privacy-scrubbed.

## Method
1. **One ledger of truth**: date, instrument, quantity, price, fees, FX; every corporate action (splits, dividends, mergers) applied and logged; reconcile to broker statements monthly — discrepancies fixed at the source.
2. **Performance math, stated explicitly**: TWR for portfolio performance independent of cash-flow timing; IRR/XIRR for personal experience of money; pick, state which, and never mix. Show both when they tell different stories.
3. **Benchmarks chosen in advance**: appropriate index/blend per portfolio mandate; record the choice before results arrive; compare honestly (including fees and taxes where material).
4. **Attribution**: decompose what drove returns — allocation vs selection vs timing vs costs vs currency; write the one-paragraph truth including the unflattering parts.
5. **Tax lot awareness (e.g., Canadian ACB discipline)**: track adjusted cost base, acquisition dates, superficial-loss awareness; lot selection noted when selling — consult the accountant for filings, but keep the data clean for them.
6. **Reporting**: monthly/quarterly one-pager (value, return vs benchmark, attribution, actions); annual summary; archive everything.
7. **Privacy scrubbing**: reports never contain account numbers, institution logins, or raw statements; aggregate before sharing; financial documents never leave private storage (house rule).

## Quality gates
- [ ] Ledger reconciled to statements; corporate actions applied.
- [ ] Performance method (TWR/IRR) stated; benchmark documented upfront.
- [ ] Attribution written (honest, including costs).
- [ ] Reports scrubbed of identifiers before any sharing.

## Red lines
- Never leak account numbers or balances externally; no silent history edits — corrections annotated; no mixing return methods in one report.
