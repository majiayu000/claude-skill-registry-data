# ICAIRE Content Optimization

Turn evidence-labeled ICAIRE social findings into a focused, testable next-week content plan.

## Contract Checklist

- Read the canonical ICAIRE Brain page `protocol/icaire-content-optimization-interpretation` before interpreting findings.
- Accept only traceable findings produced under the social-performance analysis protocol.
- Preserve each finding's evidence label, platform scope, sample size, and limitations.
- Produce a report with exactly two main sections: `Analysis` and `Optimization`.
- Make next-week actions specific, feasible, review-gated, and tied to named evidence or hypotheses.
- Separate exploitation of stronger observed patterns from experiments designed to test weak signals.
- Specify what will be measured, the comparison, the decision rule, and the future review date.
- Do not draft final posts, publish, message, or modify account state.

## Workflow

1. Load the protocol and analysis handoff:
   - Read `protocol/icaire-content-optimization-interpretation` from the live ICAIRE Brain.
   - Verify the analysis artifact, source run IDs, evidence labels, platform scope, and limitations.
   - Stop if findings are not traceable or the protocol is unavailable.
   - Anti-patterns: optimizing from an aggregate PDF alone, using remembered protocol text, accepting unlabeled claims as findings
2. Restate the defensible analysis:
   - Build the `Analysis` section from validated findings only.
   - Lead with cross-cutting patterns when supported, then platform-specific signals, counterexamples, and important uncertainties.
   - Keep data-quality issues visible and distinguish observed patterns from weak signals.
   - Anti-patterns: hiding caveats, rewriting weak signals as conclusions, repeating every metric without interpretation
3. Translate findings into next-week choices:
   - Build the `Optimization` section as a prioritized set of content actions for the next seven-day cycle.
   - Tie each action to a finding identifier or label it as a new hypothesis.
   - Specify target platform, topic or message angle, language, timing window, format, visual treatment, owner, and review gate when known.
   - Keep the plan small enough to execute and learn from.
   - Anti-patterns: generic advice such as post more, unsupported prescriptions, changing several uncontrolled dimensions in every post
4. Design testable hypotheses:
   - Express each test as `If [controlled content choice], then [metric] will [direction] versus [comparison], because [evidence or rationale].`
   - Define the primary metric, comparison or baseline, minimum observation window, confounders to hold stable, and decision rule.
   - Pre-register hypotheses before publishing and do not alter success criteria after seeing results.
   - Anti-patterns: unmeasurable hypotheses, outcome switching, tests without a comparison, causal certainty from observational history
5. Check portfolio balance and safeguards:
   - Balance exploitation of stronger patterns with a limited number of learning experiments.
   - Respect ICAIRE public-communications approval, confidentiality, accessibility, Arabic and English quality, and brand requirements.
   - Flag any action needing team confirmation or missing source evidence.
   - Anti-patterns: exposing internal-only work, optimizing only for vanity metrics, bypassing approval, sacrificing message quality for engagement
6. Save and hand off:
   - Save the two-section report and structured experiment backlog under the Audience Growth Engine.
   - Link every recommendation to its source finding or explicit hypothesis.
   - Report exact artifact paths, next review date, approvals required, and deliberate non-actions.
   - Anti-patterns: publishing directly, omitting the future review point, claiming optimization success before the next measurement cycle

## Report Design

### Analysis

Summarize scope, defensible cross-cutting patterns, platform-specific signals, counterexamples, uncertainties, and data gaps.

### Optimization

List prioritized next-week actions, testable hypotheses, measurement plans, owners, review gates, and the next review date.

## Anti-Patterns

- Treating optimization guidance as proven fact.
- Recommending actions that cannot be traced to evidence or a clearly labeled hypothesis.
- Optimizing only for total engagement without considering reach, audience quality, mission, or measurement comparability.
- Drafting or publishing content when the task is interpretation and planning.
- Producing a plan too broad to execute or evaluate in one cycle.

## Output

Produce a report with the two main sections `Analysis` and `Optimization`, plus a structured experiment backlog. Include source finding IDs, evidence strength, next-week actions, test definitions, approvals required, next review date, exact artifact paths, and a statement that no content was published.
