# ICAIRE Partnerships Lead: Research Visitor

Use this skill to prepare focused background research for a visitor, ambassador,
speaker, partner, or institution before ICAIRE engagement.

## Contract

- Start from the person or institution, visit context, meeting purpose, and
  audience for the brief.
- Use BigBrain or the ICAIRE MCP connector for ICAIRE facts, prior meetings,
  relationship history, initiatives, people, and open follow-ups.
- Use current public sources for titles, affiliations, institutional mandates,
  recent work, publications, speeches, partnerships, and public positions.
- Always check the person or institution's public X account, when one can be
  reliably identified, for recent priorities, public positioning, and active
  conversation themes.
- Separate confirmed facts, source-backed analysis, ICAIRE relevance,
  assumptions, and open questions.
- Keep the output practical for conversation preparation, not a generic
  biography.

## Workflow

1. Identify the visitor, ambassador, speaker, partner, or institution, plus the
   engagement date, format, hosts, and desired outcome if known.
2. Gather internal context from user material, BigBrain, or the ICAIRE MCP:
   prior links to ICAIRE, SDAIA, UNESCO, Saudi AI priorities, active
   initiatives, previous commitments, and relationship sensitivities.
3. Gather external background from reliable public sources when current facts
   matter.
4. Identify and review the relevant public X account. Prefer verified official
   profiles, profiles linked from official biographies, or accounts repeatedly
   referenced by the person's institution. Capture recent themes and useful
   signals, but treat X content as public positioning rather than confirmed
   institutional commitments unless supported elsewhere.
5. Build a concise profile covering role, institution, relevant work, public
   interests, and likely priorities.
6. Explain relevance to ICAIRE and identify useful overlaps, risks,
   sensitivities, and possible value propositions.
7. Draft conversation angles, briefing questions, and follow-up opportunities.
8. Verify names, titles, affiliations, dates, X handles, and prior links against source
   material or retrieved ICAIRE context.

## Guardrails

- Do not invent prior relationships, commitments, titles, institutional
  positions, or links to ICAIRE, SDAIA, or UNESCO.
- Do not rely on stale public profile details when a current title or role is
  important; verify with live or provided sources.
- Do not guess X handles. If the account cannot be reliably identified, say so
  and continue with other public sources.
- Do not treat reposts, likes, or informal X posts as formal commitments unless
  corroborated by official material.
- Do not overstate alignment or partnership opportunity when evidence is thin.
- Do not expose internal-only ICAIRE context in external-facing copy unless the
  user explicitly asks for it.
- Do not persist new facts to BigBrain unless the user explicitly asks for
  ingestion or profile updates.

## Output Expectations

Return a briefing-ready research note. When useful, include:

- profile
- current role and affiliation
- public X account signals
- institutional background
- relevance to ICAIRE, SDAIA, UNESCO, or active initiatives
- prior links or relationship history
- conversation angles
- briefing questions
- risks or sensitivities
- follow-up opportunities
- sources, assumptions, and missing information
