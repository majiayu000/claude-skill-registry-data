# Hook Formats

Ten hooks in one shape are one hook. Six formats give six real choices. Every format needs a contrast underneath it: what the viewer believes or does now, versus what this video shows.

## Step 1: Find the contrast

Write it in one line before any hook: "Viewers think [A]. This video shows [B]."
- Explicit contrast says both sides ("Not more practice. Slower practice.").
- Implied contrast says only B, and the viewer supplies A ("The one setting that fixed my blurry photos").
- If you can't find a contrast the video can prove, the idea is weak. Say so instead of dressing it up.

## Step 2: Build the core

A strong hook usually carries these jobs, not as fixed slots but as meanings: a **subject** (what or who), an **action** (what happened or what to do), an **objective** (the result the viewer wants), the **contrast**, and where the creator has them, **proof** (a number or a visible result) and **time** (how fast). Not every hook needs all six. The first four do most of the work.

## Step 3: The six formats

| Format | What it does | Shape | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fortune Teller** | Says what is coming or what will change for the viewer | "Soon / next time you [situation], [what will be different]" | A real, supportable change. No invented future |
| **Experimenter** | Shows a test the creator ran and its result | "I tried [x] for [time]. [Surprising part]" | The creator actually did it, in the footage |
| **Teacher** | Gives a lesson with a clear outcome | "How to [result] without [the thing they hate]" | A real method shown in the video |
| **Investigator** | Uncovers why something happens | "Why your [thing] [problem], and it's not [the usual guess]" | A real cause the video explains |
| **Contrarian** | Challenges a common belief | "Stop [common habit]. [What works instead]" | The creator can show the alternative works. No straw man |
| **Magician** | A visual surprise that stops the thumb. A layer on top of another format, not a hook on its own | Open on the most striking frame, a reveal, a before and after | The frame exists in the footage |

For each format, write: the spoken line, the on-screen text, and the first shot. Drop a format if it needs proof the creator doesn't have, and say why in one line.

## Step 4: Pick

Choose the top pick by: clearest topic, strongest true contrast, best match to the first shot, and the payoff closest to the start. Name the runner-up from a different format.

## Rules

- No fake experiments, no invented numbers, no "scientists say". Proof is theirs or a [placeholder].
- Contrarian must be fair. Don't attack a belief nobody holds.
- Questions as hooks are fine only if the viewer can't answer "no" and swipe. "Do your photos look blurry?" invites a no. "Why your phone photos look blurry at night" doesn't.
