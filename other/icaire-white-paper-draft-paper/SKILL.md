# ICAIRE White Paper: Draft Paper

Draft an ICAIRE white paper from an approved proposal, source material, and
known review constraints.

## Contract

- Keep scope to ICAIRE white papers. Scientific papers, journal submissions,
  formal academic manuscripts, and peer-review publication strategies are out
  of scope unless a separate workflow is created.
- Start from an approved or reviewable proposal. If approval status is unknown,
  label the draft as provisional.
- Use BigBrain or the ICAIRE MCP connector for ICAIRE context, source pages,
  initiatives, meetings, prior reports, stakeholder language, and constraints.
- Use provided source material first. Add citations or source notes without
  fabricating bibliographic details.
- Treat criteria, templates, citation style, Arabic support, grammar rules, and
  design standards as placeholders until provided.

## Workflow

1. Confirm the drafting frame.
   - Identify title, audience, objective, approved scope, expected length,
     language requirements, deadline, source set, and approval status.
2. Build the evidence map.
   - Map each proposed claim to provided sources, ICAIRE context, or a missing
     evidence placeholder.
   - Mark claims needing expert, policy, legal, Arabic-language, citation, or
     leadership review.
3. Structure the paper.
   - Prepare a white-paper outline with sections, section purposes, key claims,
     evidence, figures or diagrams, and review notes.
   - Include methodology, research workflow, or benchmarking sections only when
     useful for the white-paper argument.
4. Draft the content.
   - Write in a clear institutional voice for the intended audience.
   - Keep the abstract, executive summary, recommendations, and conclusion
     aligned with the approved scope.
   - Use citations or source notes in the requested style, or a simple
     placeholder style when no standard has been supplied.
5. Perform a drafting QA pass.
   - Check for unsupported claims, scope creep, duplicated arguments, undefined
     terms, missing citations, grammar issues, Arabic-language needs, and design
     or formatting dependencies.

## Output

Use the smallest artifact that satisfies the request. Common sections include:

- `Draft Status`
- `Working Abstract`
- `Outline`
- `Evidence Map`
- `Draft Sections`
- `Citation Placeholders`
- `Figures Or Diagrams Needed`
- `Arabic And Grammar Checks Needed`
- `Review Needs`
- `Open Questions`

## Guardrails

- Do not fabricate citations, statistics, external claims, or ICAIRE positions.
- Do not treat a provisional draft as approved.
- Do not use scientific-paper conventions as default white-paper structure.
- Do not bury missing evidence or review needs inside polished prose.
