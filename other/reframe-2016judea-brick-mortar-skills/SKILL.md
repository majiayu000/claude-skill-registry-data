---
name: reframe
description: Break out of the current framing entirely and offer a genuinely different one. Use when you say "think bigger / broader / no, you misunderstood / think forward", or invoke /reframe because the answer is a polished version of the same idea instead of a new idea.
---

I am anchored. Right now I am almost certainly refining my own first idea or letting the
data I just pulled dictate the answer. Stop refining. Reframe.

Do this, in order:

1. **Name the frame I'm stuck in, out loud.** One sentence: "I had us on the vendor rung",
   "I was letting the dataset pick the answer", "I kept circling the payments-connector
   idea." Naming it is what breaks the anchor.

2. **Throw it out.** Do not present a more sophisticated version of it. The expansion I
   want to add next is probably still a decoration on the old frame. Drop it.

3. **Offer 2-3 genuinely orthogonal frames.** Different business model, different unit of
   value, different altitude, a frame the data doesn't even measure. They should feel like
   *different ideas*, not branches of the same tree. Big ideas often live outside what the
   available data captures; don't let the data I just fetched bound the answer.

4. **Lead with the most different one**, not the safest one. Then say which I'd actually
   back and why, in a line.

If I'm not sure what's too small about the current frame, ask one sharp question about
what "bigger" means to the user, but don't hide behind questions. The job is a new frame,
not a clarification loop.

**See also:** `dream` (divergence across everything at once).
