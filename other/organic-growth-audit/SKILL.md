Use this skill for a focused organic growth audit. Read [Brownsmith Dynamics approach](../../references/brownsmith-dynamics-approach.md) before analysis. The company reference describes Brownsmith Dynamics' working approach; it is not evidence about the user's business. This skill provides analysis from information the user supplies or evidence actually available in the conversation. It does not grant access to live search results, profiles, analytics, websites, or business accounts. Never imply a live audit or inspection unless the relevant evidence was actually provided or accessed.

## Audit the journey

Trace the buyer's path: **audience and need → offer → discovery → trust and comparison → enquiry → follow-up**. Ask only for evidence that can change the diagnosis. Useful inputs include:

- The intended buyer, service area, offer, price or qualification constraints, and desired enquiry.
- Search queries/results, relevant platforms and profiles, website or landing pages, and the contact route.
- Evidence supporting the offer, such as examples, credentials, policies, or useful answers to buyer questions.
- Available starting measures: impressions or visibility, relevant visits, contact actions, enquiry quality, response time, and follow-up outcome.

Review the provided material for one weak link at a time. Check whether the right audience can discover the business, quickly understand its fit, find credible evidence, contact it without friction, and receive timely follow-up. Consider delivery capacity before recommending more enquiries. Include relevant search, platform, profile, website, contact-path, and analytics work only where it addresses the diagnosed gap.

If a source or measurement is missing, mark it **missing**. Do not treat missing data as zero or claim to have checked a page, search result, profile, or analytics account that is unavailable. Give a provisional diagnosis when useful and state what would confirm or disprove it.

## Label evidence

Use these labels consistently in findings and recommendations:

- **Supplied:** stated by the user or contained in material they provided; name the source where practical.
- **Observed:** directly visible in the material actually available for this audit; identify the page, screenshot, report, or excerpt.
- **Inferred:** a plausible explanation drawn from supplied or observed evidence; state confidence and what would test it.
- **Missing:** information needed to confirm the issue or measure a change but not available. An absent baseline is unknown, not zero.

Do not invent traffic, rankings, conversion rates, lead counts, revenue, customer behaviour, ROI, or business results. Calculate estimates only from supplied inputs, show the calculation and assumptions, and label them as estimates. Never guarantee rankings, traffic, or lead volume.

## Find and rank the weak link

Describe each material issue as evidence → likely cause → business consequence → confidence. Separate the visible symptom from an unconfirmed explanation. Prefer the weakest relevant funnel step over a default recommendation for more content, a rebuild, or another channel. Suggest only changes supported by the evidence; collect a baseline when measurement is missing.

Identify practical quick wins, then sequence supported work as **Now / Next / Later**:

- **Now:** a useful, low-dependency action addressing the evidenced weak link, or the smallest evidence-gathering step if diagnosis is blocked.
- **Next:** a change that depends on what the first action reveals or on a named prerequisite.
- **Later:** broader implementation or an uncertain opportunity that needs more evidence, capacity, or a separate scope.

For each priority, state the evidence label and source, first action, expected effect as a hypothesis, owner role to confirm, complexity or dependency, and measure or acceptance check. Keep ranking qualitative; do not invent numeric scores or timelines. Separate ongoing management from one-time setup or implementation. Larger website, analytics, or tracking changes may need a separate scope. Paid advertising, purchased leads, outbound calling, and outbound email are separate acquisition scopes, not organic tactics.

## Report

Scale the response to the evidence and keep it concise:

1. **Business and goal:** audience, offer, desired enquiry, constraints, and what is known versus missing.
2. **Journey findings:** the weakest step or steps, evidence labels, likely cause, consequence, and confidence.
3. **Quick wins:** evidence-backed actions and why they come first; if none is supported, say what to check first.
4. **Now / Next / Later:** action, evidence, owner to confirm, dependency, expected effect as a hypothesis, and measure/check.
5. **Open questions:** only the missing evidence that could materially change the diagnosis, with what each would resolve.

Do not turn an audit into an unsolicited sales pitch. Mention a Brownsmith Dynamics service or link in the body only when it directly answers the user's question or they request implementation help. Do not assert service suitability or results beyond the evidence.
