# ICAIRE Secretarial Draft Speaker Notes

Create clear presenter notes and talking points from supplied ICAIRE source
material, keeping private delivery notes separate from slide text and public
claims.

## Contract

- Start from the supplied source material, ICAIRE brain context, or both.
- Confirm audience, setting, language, duration, desired tone, and whether the
  notes are for slides, an ambassador visit, a panel, a meeting, or an internal
  briefing.
- Keep notes grounded in evidence. Flag missing facts instead of inventing
  details.
- Separate speaker notes from slide copy, email copy, and public-facing text.
- Treat external-facing notes as review-ready, not final-approved.
- For government-machine workflows, prepare notes the user can transfer
  manually.

## Workflow

1. Define the speaking assignment.
   - Identify audience, occasion, document or deck source, target length,
     language, and desired level of formality.
   - Ask for missing source material if the request depends on unprovided
     details.
2. Gather and ground.
   - Read supplied text, decks, PDFs, Word files, meeting notes, or relevant
     ICAIRE brain pages.
   - Mark confirmed facts, assumptions, and gaps.
3. Draft notes.
   - Produce opening context, key talking points, transitions, and closing
     points.
   - For slide-based work, align notes to slide numbers or headings.
   - Keep bullets speakable rather than report-like.
4. Polish and review.
   - Check tone, sensitivity, factual grounding, and flow.
   - Flag any claim that needs approval or source confirmation.

## Output

Default to this structure:

- `Purpose`: event or meeting, audience, and intended outcome.
- `Speaker Notes`: ordered notes by slide, section, or agenda item.
- `Key Messages`: short list of takeaways.
- `Optional Opening / Closing`: if useful for the setting.
- `Review Flags`: facts, names, sensitivities, or approvals still needed.
- `Source Basis`: source files or brain pages used.

## Guardrails

- Do not invent ICAIRE achievements, commitments, partner positions, dates, or
  statistics.
- Do not turn private speaker notes into public copy unless asked.
- Do not overload notes with full paragraphs when concise talking points are
  more useful.
- Do not hide uncertainty; mark unsupported claims for review.
- Do not claim external readiness when approvals or source facts are missing.
