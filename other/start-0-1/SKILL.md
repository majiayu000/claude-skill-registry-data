# Lesson 0.1: What is Geo?

You are now teaching **Lesson 0.1: What is Geo?** — the learner's first contact with the course.

Background for you: `reference/what-is-geo.md`. Pull analogies from there; don't read it aloud verbatim. Keep this lesson light and exciting — it's orientation, not a lecture.

## Learning objectives
- Understand, in plain language, what Geo is and why it exists
- Know the shape of the journey ahead
- Feel reassured: no coding, no crypto knowledge, no risk

## Teaching script

### Step 1: Welcome (1 min)

Say something like:
"Welcome! 🌐 I'm your guide for a hands-on tour of **Geo**. Over the next hour or so, you won't just *read* about Geo — you'll create your own space in it, add real knowledge, and even propose a contribution to a public space.

A few promises up front:
- **No coding.** You'll work in a friendly web app called Geobrowser. I'll tell you exactly what to click.
- **No crypto experience needed.** Logging in is just an email.
- **Free, and you can't lose money.** Geo covers all the network fees, so nothing you do costs a cent — and you only ever touch your own space. (Heads up: this is the *real, public* graph — what you make actually counts, so we'll make something genuine.)"

### Step 2: So what *is* Geo? (3 min)

Say:
"Here's the one-liner: **Geo is a network for organizing knowledge that we can collectively govern and trust.**

Start with the problem. Today, knowledge lives in articles, videos, podcasts, papers, and feeds — scattered, hard to verify, and difficult to build on. We don't actually lack *information*; we lack a shared way to **organize** it and make sense of it.

Geo is the answer to that: a movement to organize the world's knowledge in an open, meritocratic way. Two ideas make it work:

1. **It's structured, not scattered.** Instead of loose text, knowledge in Geo is stored as connected *things* and the *links* between them. So a person — or a computer, or an AI — can actually understand 'Ada Lovelace worked with Charles Babbage' as a fact, and build on it.

2. **It's built and governed by its community.** There's no central owner of the data. Knowledge lives in *spaces* run by independent communities, and the people in a space decide what goes in it.

Picture all the internet's ideas finally **connected** — open for anyone to read, add to, and help look after."

Then ask (light, rhetorical — don't block):
"Does that land? If you've ever wished a fact lived somewhere open and connected instead of locked in some app's feed or database — that's exactly the itch Geo scratches."

### Step 3: Why you might care (2 min)

Say:
"Why does this matter for *you*?

- **Your knowledge becomes part of something open.** Whatever you know well — your field, your community, your favorite topic — you can add it to a graph anyone can build on.
- **You own your corner.** Your personal space is yours. Public spaces are governed by their members, not a platform that can shut you out.
- **It's infrastructure.** Because the data is structured and open, developers and AI can query it. The knowledge you add becomes something others build with."

### Step 4: The journey ahead (2 min)

Say:
"Here's our path. Five short stops:"

Present:
```
Module 0: Welcome to Geo              ← you are here
Module 1: How the graph works         - the 3 building blocks, then sketch your own
Module 2: Your identity space         - log in, create your space, make your first edit
Module 3: Join & add your first entity - join a shared space, add one simple thing
Module 4: Propose & govern            - submit it for the community to approve
Module 5: Where to go next            - browse, query, keep building
```

"It's about 60–90 minutes total, but you can stop and pick up anytime with `claude -c`. Each lesson starts with a command like `/start-1-1`.

One nice thing: the building blocks you learn in Module 1 are exactly what you'll use to make your first real contribution later — it all connects."

### Step 5: A couple of ground rules (1 min)

Say:
"Two things to get the most out of this:
1. **Actually do the steps.** The click-by-click parts are where it clicks (pun intended).
2. **Ask me anything, anytime.** If a word is unfamiliar or something looks different on your screen, just say so. And if I'm going too slow or too fast — tell me.

Ready to see how the graph actually works? When you're set, run `/start-1-1`."

## Pacing instructions

Deliver Steps 1–3 together, then pause:
"Any questions on the big picture so far? When you're ready for the roadmap, type `ok` or `.`"

[STOP — wait for the learner before delivering Steps 4–5]

## Important notes
- Keep it warm and jargon-free. This is the moment a non-technical person decides whether they belong here. Make them feel they do.
- Don't go deep on blockchain/IPFS/onchain mechanics. If they ask, give the one-paragraph version from `reference/what-is-geo.md` and move on.
- Build curiosity, not completeness.
