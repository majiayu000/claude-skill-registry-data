# Tractor Spec Options

Tractor spec choices made at purchase drive 5–7 years of operating cost. A bad spec costs ~$15K over the trade cycle. A good spec recovers more residual at trade.

## Engine choice

Three major heavy-duty diesel platforms:

| Engine | OEM | Power range | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cummins X15 | Cummins (sold to all OEMs) | 400–605 hp | Most widely serviced, parts everywhere |
| Detroit DD15 / DD16 | Daimler (Freightliner, Western Star) | 400–600 hp | Strong in DTNA fleets, lower-end TCO |
| PACCAR MX-13 | Kenworth / Peterbilt | 405–510 hp | Lighter weight, smoother |
| International A26 | International (Navistar) | 400–475 hp | Comeback platform, lower hp ceiling |

**For most line-haul:** 425–500 hp paired with 1,650 lb-ft torque is plenty. Higher hp burns more fuel and rarely needed unless heavy-haul or steep grades.

**Aftermarket / parts availability:** X15 wins for owner-operators who may end up far from home — every truck stop and dealer carries common parts.

## Transmission

| Type | Cost vs manual | Trade-in retention | Driver preference |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10-speed manual (Eaton Fuller) | Baseline | Decreasing — fewer drivers can run | Older drivers prefer |
| Eaton Endurant / Cummins Endurant AMT | +$5,000 | Improving | Most new hires prefer |
| Detroit DT12 AMT | +$5,000 | Improving | Strong in DTNA fleets |
| Volvo I-Shift | Included in Volvo | High | All Volvo |
| Mack mDRIVE | Included in Mack | High | All Mack |

**The case for AMT (now ~85% of new tractors):**
- New drivers don't have manual transmission CDL endorsement (restriction L)
- AMT shifts more efficiently in heavy traffic / mountainous terrain
- Fewer clutch warranty claims
- Fuel economy advantage 0.2–0.4 MPG over manual driven by average driver

## Axle ratio

Rear axle ratio determines RPM at cruise speed. Lower ratio = lower RPM = better fuel economy.

| Ratio | RPM @ 65 mph (typical) | Use case |
|---|---|---|
| 2.47 | 1,300 | Premium fuel economy, light-load line-haul |
| 2.64 | 1,360 | Most common line-haul |
| 2.79 | 1,400 | General purpose |
| 3.08 | 1,500 | Heavy-haul, mountainous |
| 3.36 | 1,650 | LTL pickup-and-delivery |

Lower ratios are "fast" axles for fuel economy; higher ratios are "slow" axles for pulling power. Match to lane:
- Flat Midwest line-haul: 2.47–2.64
- Mountain (CO, PA, WV): 2.79–3.08
- Heavy haul / vocational: 3.08–3.55

## Sleeper size

| Size | Use case | Cost premium |
|---|---|---|
| Day cab | Local / regional, no overnight | Baseline |
| 48" sleeper | Regional teams, occasional overnight | +$10K |
| 60" sleeper | Solo OTR, basic | +$15K |
| 72" mid-roof | Solo OTR comfort | +$20K |
| 76"–84" high-roof | Team driving, long-haul | +$25K–$35K |

Sleeper size affects driver recruiting — modern solo OTR drivers expect 72"+ mid-roof minimum with refrigerator, microwave, inverter, and APU. Skimping costs in driver retention.

## Fuel tanks

| Configuration | Capacity | Range @ 6.5 MPG |
|---|---|---|
| Single 100-gal | 100 gal | 650 mi |
| Dual 100-gal | 200 gal | 1,300 mi |
| Dual 120-gal | 240 gal | 1,560 mi |
| Dual 150-gal | 300 gal | 1,950 mi |

Larger tanks = more flexibility on fuel-stop strategy, more weight. 240 gal is the sweet spot for OTR.

## Aerodynamic options

| Option | Cost | MPG gain |
|---|---|---|
| Full chassis fairings | Often standard on aero models | Built in |
| Side skirts (under fuel tank) | $800 | 0.1–0.2 |
| Cab roof fairing matched to trailer | Often standard | 0.2–0.3 |
| Wheel covers | $400/set | 0.1 |
| LED headlights / clearance lights | $400 | (0) — but fewer replacements |

A "fully spec'd aero" tractor (Cascadia, Pete 579, Volvo VNL 740) returns 0.5–1.0 MPG over a "vocational spec" tractor.

## Electronic features

| Feature | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Lane keeping assist | Often bundled | Reduces lane-departure crashes |
| Collision mitigation (radar) | $2K | Insurance discount available |
| Active steering | $2K | Newer feature |
| Predictive cruise control | $3K | Uses GPS terrain for shifting/cruising — 1–2% MPG |
| Electronic stability control | Standard on new | Required since 2017 |
| Driver-facing camera | $500–$1,000 + monthly | Major insurance discount; driver acceptance varies |

## Resale value drivers

Tractors that command top resale at trade-in (3–5 years):
- High-volume, common platform (Cascadia, Pete 579, KW T680, Volvo VNL)
- Major engine (X15 first, then DD15/DD16)
- Auto transmission
- Common color (white preferred)
- Lower miles (under 500K)
- Documented maintenance history

Resale discounts ($5K–$15K):
- Manual transmission
- Niche engine (International A26 weak in some markets)
- Bright / custom paint
- 700K+ miles
- Wreck history

## Warranty

| Component | Standard | Extended typical |
|---|---|---|
| Engine | 2 yr / 250K | 5 yr / 750K extended |
| Transmission | 2 yr / 250K | 5 yr / 750K extended |
| Drive axle | 3 yr / 350K | 5 yr / 500K extended |
| Cab structure | 5 yr | Often included |
| Aftertreatment (DPF/SCR) | 5 yr / 100K (EPA mandated) | Extended available |

Aftertreatment failures are the most expensive non-warranty repair ($3K–$8K). Extended aftertreatment warranty is the best ROI of any extension.

## Common mistakes

- Spec'ing too much horsepower because driver wants it — fuel economy penalty
- Wrong axle ratio for the lane — costs 0.5 MPG forever
- Tiny sleeper to save $10K — costs $30K in driver turnover
- Buying a "value" engine outside the X15/DD15/MX-13 trio — parts headaches on the road
- Skipping driver-facing camera — insurance premium 10–20% higher
- Custom paint to "personalize the fleet" — kills resale
- Manual transmission today — no new drivers can run it
