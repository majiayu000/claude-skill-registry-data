# Baking Structure

## Core Workflow

1. Identify the baked good, desired crumb or structure, formula type, and current defect or adaptation goal.
2. Map ingredient roles: flour or starch, water, sugar, fat, eggs or binders, leavening, salt, acids, inclusions, and tenderizers.
3. Explain the likely structure mechanism: gluten development, starch gelatinization, hydration, leavening gas retention, egg setting, fat plasticity, or bake-through.
4. Recommend measured changes to formula, mixing, rest, pan size, oven temperature, bake time, or doneness checks.
5. Provide sensory and measured cues for batter or dough state and final doneness.

## Baking Guidance

For dense, gummy, crumbly, dry, collapsed, tunneled, greasy, or under-set results, avoid a single-cause answer unless the evidence is clear. Separate formulation problems from process problems and oven or pan problems.

For substitutions and dietary adaptations, preserve structure before flavor. Explain what replaces binding, hydration, tenderness, lift, or set, and identify tradeoffs in texture and shelf life.

Route to `food-safety` for allergen-critical claims, celiac-sensitive work, raw flour or raw egg handling, custards, cream fillings, long holding, or vulnerable diners.

## Concision Guidance

Default to the most likely causes and the next batch's highest-leverage changes. Avoid listing every baking variable when the prompt only supports two or three likely mechanisms. Put cues and measured changes ahead of background chemistry.

## Output Contract

- Baked good type and target structure
- Ingredient role map
- Current defect or adaptation risk
- Likely mechanisms and confidence
- Formula and process adjustments
- Mixing, pan, oven, timing, and rest guidance
- Doneness and texture verification cues
- Safety or allergen gate when relevant
