# Bid/No-Bid Decision

## When To Use
A request for proposal arrives from a client you have never spoken to, or an email says you are one of three firms on a list. Being invited feels like progress, and when you work alone the response will eat the week. Run this before you open a blank document, to decide against criteria you wrote in a calmer moment.

## When Not To Use
For a warm lead with no formal request, MEDDIC Qualification is the lighter check. Once the answer is yes, AI-Written Brief Review reads what the document says; this skill only settles whether to respond.

## Inputs
- The request, invitation or email, and what you know about how it reached you
- Your go or no-go criteria, written before this arrived or reused from last time
- Your estimate of the hours a response would take, and any direct cost
If you have none of this, I start from a short list of common criteria for you to edit and the invitation itself, and mark the record as a first draft.

## Approach
A bid or no-bid gate is a long-standing habit among people who write proposals for a living, described here generically: a named person decides against criteria fixed in advance, so the flattery of being asked does not decide instead. Buyers can now assemble a list of firms with an AI tool before speaking to any of them, so a place on a shortlist no longer proves anyone has a view of you. The failure it prevents is ten days of work to be the third price on someone else's spreadsheet.

## Workflow
1. Ask, in one go: are your criteria written down (if not, we write them now, before reading the request closely)? What is the real submission deadline? Have you had any contact with the person who decides?
2. Set two tiers. Pass or fail: fit to your practice brief, capacity in the delivery window, any mandatory requirement you cannot meet. Then the judgment criteria: access to the decider, whether you helped shape the requirement, risk, the chance of being more than a comparison. One fail ends it.
3. Against each criterion, write the fact, its source and met, not met or unknown. You set how many unknowns you can live with; I fill none of them in.
4. Run the shortlist check as questions for the client: how did you find us? Was the requirement written before you spoke to any supplier? Can we talk to the decider before we submit? How many firms are being asked? Process only, never speculation about named rivals.
5. Write the cost of pursuit in your hours and costs exactly as you gave them, next to what else that time would have gone to.
6. Draft the record: bid, bid with a condition (for instance a conversation with the decider first), or no-bid. For a no-bid, draft a short, warm reply that leaves the door open, for you to edit and send. Tender or contract terms that worry you: check with a qualified adviser.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Go or No-Go Record
Opportunity: [title] · Received: [date] · Deadline: [date] · Decides: [your name]
## Pass or fail
| Criterion (written before the facts) | Fact (source) | Met / not met / unknown |
|---|---|---|
| Fit to my practice brief | [fact] ([section / email]) | [status] |
## Judgment criteria
| Criterion | Fact (source) | Met / not met / unknown |
|---|---|---|
| Access to the decider | [fact] | [status] |
## Shortlist check
| Question for the client | Answer (source) or "to ask" |
|---|---|
| How did you find us? | [answer] |
## Cost of pursuit
[Your hours] hours and [your costs], instead of [what that time was for]
## Decision
[Your name] decides bid / bid if [condition] / no-bid on [date], because [reason]. The reply is sent by [your name].
```

## Done When
- The criteria existed before the facts were filled in
- Each fact carries its source, or the cell reads "unknown"
- The shortlist check holds questions about process, with no guess about other firms
- The record names you, a date and a reason

## Quality Bar
- A failed pass or fail criterion ends the bid, however good the invitation feels
- The cost of pursuit is in your figures and nobody else's
- A no-bid reply is short and gracious, and it is yours to send
- A named person decides against written criteria; I supply no fact you did not give me and send nothing.
