# LinkedIn Post Engine

Create LinkedIn posts that feel human, useful, and credible: proven hook frameworks, clear narrative structures, proof-first writing, and practical CTA design.

**Voice calibration**: when the context is Oded's voice or senior engineering leadership topics, read [references/oded-voice.md](references/oded-voice.md) and apply it — modes, formatting, Hebrew register, and judging criteria all live there.

## Process Workflow

### Phase 1: Audience + Positioning (CRITICAL)

Before writing, define:

1. **Audience** — Who is this for? (founders, recruiters, engineers, operators, marketers, sales leaders, etc.)
2. **Goal** — Reach, authority, leads, hiring, trust, replies, profile visits?
3. **Core insight** — What is the one thing worth remembering?
4. **Proof** — What makes this believable (numbers, before/after, constraints, mistakes, outcomes)?

If proof is missing, use placeholders like `[X%]`, `[Y hours]` and request exact values.

When the user gives only a raw idea, ask/fill: Audience, Goal, Topic, Proof points, Tone, CTA preference.

### Phase 2: Structure + Hook Selection

Choose a format first (Story, Framework, Contrarian, Case study, Teardown, Build-in-public), then select 2-3 hooks and finalize one.

### Phase 3: Draft + Polish

- Keep paragraphs short (1-2 lines)
- Front-load specifics
- Remove generic filler; key claims and advice include specific reasoning or mechanisms, not surface statements
- End with one clear CTA
- Add 3-6 hashtags max

## LinkedIn Feed Dynamics (Practical)

What usually performs best:

1. **Strong first two lines** (stops the scroll)
2. **Specificity** (numbers > adjectives)
3. **Authentic pragmatism** — willingness to discuss real constraints, tradeoffs, and what actually broke at scale (avoid classic "vulnerability" framing and over-used formulas)
4. **Clear structure** (easy to skim on mobile)
5. **Conversation CTA** (quality comments over empty likes)

Avoid: buzzword soup, generic motivation posts, overlong hashtag blocks, fake certainty without evidence, over-used LinkedIn tropes and boilerplate phrasing.

## Hook Formulas

**Important**: Avoid generic/boilerplate hooks and over-used formulas (dramatic "everyone is doing X while they should be doing Y", "you won't believe...", overly polished contrarian openers). Prioritize hooks that sound like a real, pragmatic, experienced practitioner — leverage, constraints, clarity, systems-level observations.

### The Contrarian / Leverage Hook (preferred style)
- "The highest-leverage move most orgs are still avoiding..."
- "Most teams optimize for local [metric]. The real constraint is almost always [system-level thing]."
- "We improved this by removing options and forcing clarity, not by adding process or tools."

### The Specific Result Hook
- "In [timeframe], we improved [metric] by [number]. Here's how."
- "We cut [cost/time] by [X%]. Not with a new tool—by changing this one workflow."

### The Mistake / Reality Hook (use sparingly, make personal)
- "I made this [role]-mistake for months. It cost us [outcome]."
- "We shipped the wrong thing fast. Here's what actually fixed it at scale."

### The Framework Hook
- "The [3-step/4-step] framework I use for [outcome]."
- "If I had to restart as [role], I'd follow these 5 rules."

### The Question Hook
- "Would you let your team do [X] without [Y]?"
- "What's your biggest bottleneck in [domain] right now?"

## High-Performing Post Formats

### 1) "This runs now" (Operational Story)
Best for real systems, workflows, and automations.

Template: bold claim → "Not as a demo. As an actual [responsibility/workflow]." → "Here's what it does:" with 4-6 concrete bullets → measurable result → perspective line + CTA question.

### 2) Case Study
Template: starting problem → constraints → intervention → before/after metrics → key lesson → optional "comment TEMPLATE" CTA.

### 3) Contrarian Opinion (Hybrid with Framework elements)
Best for posts that challenge dominant narratives while providing structured insight.

Template:
- Open with dominant narrative + mild critique ("The dominant AI narratives right now are depressingly predictable...")
- Pivot sentence to what deserves more attention
- Personal grounding ("I've been using the staff archetypes framework from Will Larson's book for years...")
- Breakdown of implications (by archetype/role or key dimensions)
- Synthesis: overall observation on incentives, tradeoffs, or long-term effects + forward-looking implication
- Understated close + peer question CTA ("What are you seeing in your org?")

### 4) Framework Post
Template: name framework → 3-5 steps → one mistake to avoid → one practical "do this today" action.

Bullets should reflect genuine insight density. If a point is thin or generic, deepen the reasoning or remove it — do not pad for length. When the post also has a contrarian angle, blend in the Contrarian Opinion flow above.

### 5) Build-in-Public Update
Template: what shipped this week → what worked → what broke → what changed next → ask for informed feedback.

More format skeletons: [references/post-formats.md](references/post-formats.md).

## Persuasion Principles

- **Specificity**: "Saved 3.2 hours/week" beats "saved time."
- **Mechanism**: Explain how, not just outcomes.
- **Credibility**: Mention tradeoffs, not only wins.
- **Relevance**: Tie insight to audience reality.
- **Clarity**: One post = one core idea.
- **Earned Insight**: Advice and observations must feel earned — explain *why* something works (or fails), surface real tradeoffs or second-order effects, use specific language over platitudes. Applies to substance regardless of post length or format.

## Writing Rules

- Hook in first 1-2 lines
- 1-2 sentence paragraphs
- Plain language over hype
- Emojis sparingly, as section markers at most
- Keep claims realistic
- Never invent outcomes, clients, or credentials
- Hashtags: 3-6 max, niche + function + audience mix, at the very end

## Output Contract

By default, provide:

1. 3 hook options
2. 1 full post
3. 1 spicier variant
4. 3 first-comment ideas (to deepen discussion)

Optional on request: Hebrew / English / mixed variants, X/Twitter adaptation, short + long versions, carousel text outline.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- Generic "AI changed everything" without concrete examples
- No proof signals
- Too many ideas in one post
- CTA mismatch (asking for leads on a pure thought-leadership post)
- Over-formatting with noisy symbols
- AI/org transformation posts that ignore role-specific downsides or long-term incentive effects on talent

## Execution Checklist

Before finalizing:

- [ ] Hook is strong and specific
- [ ] Core claim is clear
- [ ] Includes at least one proof signal
- [ ] Easy to skim on mobile
- [ ] CTA invites real conversation
- [ ] Hashtags are relevant and limited
- [ ] Tone feels authentic to author (voice-calibrated posts: run the judging criteria in [references/oded-voice.md](references/oded-voice.md))
