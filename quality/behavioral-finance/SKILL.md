# Behavioral Finance Discipline

The market isn't the enemy; your reflexes are. Build a process that assumes you will feel fear, greed, and FOMO — and makes acting on them harder than following the plan.

## When to use
- Preparing decisions (bias-proof the process before the heat).
- Feeling urgency, euphoria, or dread about a position.
- Reviewing past decisions (honest scorekeeping).

## Method
1. **Bias playbook with counters**:
   - Loss aversion → pre-committed exits; re-underwrite instead of "waiting to get even".
   - Anchoring → write your own valuation BEFORE reading the market's price targets.
   - Recency → base rates and long windows; one year is not a trend.
   - FOMO/chasing → 48h cooling-off; no entry without a written thesis.
   - Overconfidence → sizing caps; track your hit rate honestly (journal).
   - Confirmation → mandatory disconfirming-evidence section in the thesis.
   - Sunk cost → "would I buy this today at this price?" test.
2. **Pre-commitment**: decisions get made when calm and enforced when not — written rules, journal, and stop levels beat willpower.
3. **Journals**: every decision + expected outcome + later actuals; review monthly; patterns surface (did FOMO entries underperform? — probably).
4. **Watch conditions**: no continuous price-watching; scheduled reviews instead; notifications off; headlines are not data.
5. **Committee tricks for solo investors**: assign yourself a devil's advocate round for big decisions; sleep on material changes; for major moves, discuss with the advisor set (documented, not vibes).
6. **Temptation architecture**: remove one-click trading paths for the accounts that matter; if it's easy to YOLO, it will be.

## Quality gates
- [ ] Cooling-off periods honored for impulsive ideas.
- [ ] Disconfirming evidence present in every thesis.
- [ ] Journal current with outcomes + lessons.
- [ ] Review cadence held (not continuous watching).

## Red lines
- No decisions driven by headlines, streaks, or moods; no skipped journals (the process IS the edge).
