# Media Pitch

Use when an announcement, data point, expert perspective, customer story, or trend has a credible editorial reason for a journalist or publication to care.

## Procedure
1. Identify the actual news value: new event, timely data, meaningful change, conflict, consequence, expert access, or audience-specific insight.
2. Define the publication and journalist audience and review their recent coverage before choosing the angle.
3. Write the core pitch in one or two sentences focused on why the story matters to their readers now.
4. Support the angle with verifiable facts, dates, data, customer or expert access, and links to primary materials where useful.
5. State what is available: interview, demo, embargoed material, data, images, customer source, or background briefing.
6. Personalize only around genuine relevance to the journalist's beat or prior work; avoid fake familiarity.
7. Keep product descriptions subordinate to the story and remove unsupported superlatives.
8. Track outreach, response, requests, corrections, and outcome so future media work learns which angles were actually useful.

## Decision rules
- A product launch is not automatically newsworthy to every publication.
- Journalists are not an extension of the marketing channel.
- Claims and data need sourceable evidence.
- Follow-up should add useful context, not simply repeat the original email.

## Quality gate
The pitch is ready when the editorial angle is clear without promotional padding, the target journalist has a credible reason to care, claims are verifiable, source or asset access is real, and the message can be understood quickly without opening a press release.
