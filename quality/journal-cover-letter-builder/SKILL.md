# Journal Cover Letter Builder

## Purpose

Create concise, accurate, and professional journal cover letters or presubmission inquiry letters. This skill helps authors explain what the manuscript contributes, why it may interest the journal audience, and what ethical or administrative statements are needed.

## Typical Inputs

- Manuscript title, abstract, highlights, or a short study summary.
- Target journal name and article type, if known.
- Key novelty points and main findings.
- Ethical statements, conflict-of-interest statements, funding information, or prior-submission notes.
- Editor comments or journal-specific cover-letter requirements.

## Core Rules

- Do not fabricate editor names, journal requirements, impact factors, submission policies, references, data, ethics approvals, conflicts, funding, or prior-review history.
- Do not claim that a journal is a perfect fit unless the user provides a basis or the fit is phrased cautiously.
- Do not guarantee acceptance, publication, editorial interest, or priority.
- Preserve scientific claim strength and distinguish association from causation.
- Keep confidential manuscript details only in the user's local context; do not add unnecessary sensitive information.
- Recommend final human review before submission.

## Workflow

1. Identify letter type:
   - standard cover letter
   - presubmission inquiry
   - revision cover letter
   - transfer submission note
2. Extract the manuscript essentials:
   - title
   - article type
   - study design
   - primary finding
   - novelty
   - target audience
   - key limitations or claim boundaries
3. Draft a concise editor-facing letter:
   - opening submission sentence
   - one-paragraph study summary
   - one-paragraph novelty and journal-audience fit
   - compliance statements if supplied
   - polite closing
4. If journal requirements are not provided, use generic wording and mark journal-specific items for author verification.
5. Remove hype and unsupported claims.

## Output Modes

Default output:

- A polished cover letter ready for author review.

When information is missing:

- Draft the letter with neutral placeholders such as `[journal name]` only if necessary.
- Add a short "Author to verify" checklist.

For presubmission inquiries:

- Keep the letter shorter.
- Emphasize research question, novelty, article type, and why the journal audience may care.

## Quality Checklist

- No journal policy, editor name, impact factor, or acceptance likelihood was invented.
- The novelty statement is specific and not exaggerated.
- Claims match the provided manuscript summary.
- Required ethics, conflict, funding, and prior-submission statements are included only if provided.
- The final letter requires human review before submission.
