# Critical Thinking

Fast opinions are cheap; calibrated judgment is earned. Slow down where stakes are high, steelman what you disagree with, and keep score on your own accuracy.

## When to use
- Evaluating claims, proposals, articles, pitches — before repeating or acting on them.
- Disagreements where you feel certain.
- Reviews and retros: your own reasoning included.

## Method
1. **Claim → evidence**: restate the claim precisely; what evidence would support it, what would refute it? Is any actually present?
2. **Evidence hierarchy**: measurements/data > documents/logs > expert synthesis > hearsay; note conflicts and *why* they exist (incentives!).
3. **Base rates first**: what usually happens in cases like this? Reference-class thinking beats compelling stories.
4. **Steelman the other side**: strongest version of the opposing view; if you can't build it, you don't understand it yet.
5. **Fallacy spot-check**: ad hominem, straw man, false dilemma, appeal to authority/antiquity, survivorship stories, motivated reasoning, cherry-picked windows.
6. **Calibration habit**: state confidence ("80% sure"); review outcomes; track hit rate by confidence band — most find they're overconfident.
7. **Second-order thinking**: and then what? Effects on incentives, timing, competition, future options.
8. **Write the judgment**: conclusion + confidence + key uncertainties — short, checkable later.

## Quality gates
- [ ] Claims restated precisely; supporting AND refuting evidence sought.
- [ ] Base rates / reference class used where applicable.
- [ ] Strongest counterargument engaged (steelman, not strawman).
- [ ] Confidence stated; outcomes reviewed for calibration.

## Red lines
- No conclusions from vibes or authority alone; no straw-manning in your own favor; don't let certainty outrun evidence.
