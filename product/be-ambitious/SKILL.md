# Be Ambitious

Find a better destination, not just a better way to reach the current one. Ambition means greater value, not necessarily a larger system.

Inspired by Felix Rieseberg’s [“How to be more ambitious”](references/felix-rieseberg-article.md). Read the supplied article for background only when useful.

## Find the opening

Ground the discussion in what the user cares about, what already exists, and what new capabilities or connections make possible. Distinguish observed assets from assumptions.

Look beyond the immediate ticket: if this succeeds, what becomes possible next? What important problem remains? Is the current artifact the right destination? Bring a substantive possibility rather than asking the user to supply the ambitious idea.

## Change the frame

Use whichever lenses reveal an opportunity:

- **Altitude:** From artifact to outcome, then beyond. What new decision, capability, experience, or discovery could this enable?
- **Depth:** What becomes possible with primary evidence, expert instruments, or a radically stronger target? Test apparent limits rather than accepting them.
- **Range:** Describe the activity with verbs; borrow a specific method from another field. Apply its reasoning, not its persona.
- **Speed:** If the work became dramatically cheaper or faster, what previously impractical goal would become worthwhile?

Consider a few distinct directions and recommend the strongest. Seek a genuine step change, not a feature backlog or incremental cleanup. More scale, tools, agents, or novelty are not inherently more valuable.

## Make the leap credible

Connect the recommendation to:

- **Value:** What new goal is worth pursuing, and for whom?
- **Leverage:** Why is it plausible now? What exists, and what is missing?
- **Uncertainty:** Which assumption would make or break it?
- **First proof:** What small experiment tests that assumption? What observable result would justify continuing, stopping, or changing direction?

Keep the larger destination visible; the first proof tests the ambition rather than replacing it. Adapt the response to the conversation—these are thinking prompts, not mandatory headings or a demand for a formal project plan.

## Follow through appropriately

For exploration, discuss and recommend. For authorized execution, carry out the first proof and continue as evidence warrants. Do not turn brainstorming into implementation or stop at a plan when action was requested.

Broaden the solution space, not the permission boundary: no implicit publication, spending, deployment, or upstream contact. Document unresolved redistribution rights during private experiments; respect access, privacy, and confidentiality constraints.

Treat stretch targets as hypotheses, not promises. Validate claims with measurements, tests, held-out evidence, or independent judgment; never invent data, results, or validation. State what remains speculative.

## Examples

- **Football stats → player-value research:** Go beyond dashboards. Distinguish sporting contribution from transfer price; check data coverage and labels before proposing a baseline with temporal validation.
- **Reading-group schedules → shared inquiry:** Reconsider the group’s purpose or experience, not just meeting frequency. An app or business is not the default destination.
- **Behavior-preserving parser refactor:** Use deepen-task to strengthen methods within the assignment; do not substitute a product vision.
