# Commercial Due Diligence Sprint

Use this skill to turn a target, market, or investment question into a structured commercial diligence sprint.

## Reference Loading

Read `references/diligence-workplan.md` when the user needs an interview plan, research plan, diligence workplan, or investment committee output.

Read `references/red-flags.md` when the user asks for risks, deal breakers, or what could invalidate the investment thesis.

## First Frame

Start every diligence with four questions:

1. What decision is being made: invest, pass, price, partner, enter, or prioritize?
2. What is the target and what is the value thesis?
3. What are the 3-5 diligence questions that could change the decision?
4. What evidence is available, missing, or unreliable?

Do not start with a generic market overview. Start with the decision and the critical uncertainties.

## Diligence Issue Tree

Use this base tree and tailor it to the sector:

1. Market attractiveness
   - Market size and growth
   - Profit pool and margin structure
   - Cycle, regulation, technology, and channel shifts
   - Segment-level attractiveness

2. Target competitiveness
   - Customer value proposition
   - Differentiation and switching costs
   - Win/loss drivers
   - Pricing power
   - Sales productivity and channel quality

3. Quality of growth
   - Retention, expansion, churn, and cohort health
   - New customer acquisition efficiency
   - Revenue concentration
   - Recurring vs. one-time revenue
   - Dependency on one channel, vendor, geography, or product

4. Competitive and substitution risk
   - Direct competitors
   - Adjacent substitutes
   - Buyer power
   - Supplier or platform dependency
   - Technology disruption

5. Value creation path
   - Pricing
   - Sales coverage
   - Product roadmap
   - Geographic expansion
   - Channel partnerships
   - Cost-to-serve improvement

6. Investment risks
   - Market risk
   - Execution risk
   - Customer risk
   - Regulatory risk
   - Data-quality risk
   - Integration risk

## Sprint Workflow

1. Build the decision memo skeleton before research.
   - Draft the investment thesis.
   - List confirm / deny hypotheses.
   - Identify kill criteria.

2. Create a fact base.
   - Public market data.
   - Company materials.
   - Customer and expert interviews.
   - Competitor evidence.
   - Operational and commercial metrics.

3. Run hypothesis tests.
   - State the hypothesis.
   - Identify the minimum evidence required.
   - Mark evidence as confirmed, directional, conflicting, or missing.

4. Synthesize the answer.
   - Separate investment merits from diligence gaps.
   - Put risks in decision language, not generic caveats.
   - State what would change valuation, terms, or integration plan.

5. Produce the output.
   - Executive answer.
   - Market and target assessment.
   - Risk register.
   - Value creation plan.
   - Evidence gaps and next diligence requests.

## Guardrails

- Do not confuse a large market with an attractive market.
- Do not treat management projections as evidence without triangulation.
- Do not bury kill criteria at the end; surface them early.
- Use confidence levels. If evidence is directional, say so.
- If the user asks for financial, legal, tax, or investment advice, frame the output as analytical support and note what must be verified by qualified professionals.
