# Driver Pay Models

How a fleet pays its drivers affects retention, recruitment, and unit economics. There's no single "right" model — different fleets pick different structures based on operation type + driver expectations.

## The 5 main pay models

### 1. Cents Per Mile (CPM)

Driver paid per loaded mile (sometimes also per empty mile).

**Typical CPM rates (as of 2026):**
- Entry-level driver: $0.45 - $0.55/mile
- Experienced driver: $0.55 - $0.70/mile
- Owner-operator (paid by load): variable
- Team drivers (sleeper team): $0.65 - $0.90/mile combined

**Pros:**
- Simple to calculate
- Drivers like predictability
- Easy to communicate at recruitment

**Cons:**
- Doesn't reflect time spent (detention, delays)
- Empty miles vs loaded distinction matters
- Drivers may push for more loaded miles vs deadhead
- Pay drops in slow weeks

### 2. Percentage of Revenue (POR)

Driver paid a % of the gross revenue of the loads they haul.

**Typical %:**
- Lease-purchase operator: 25% - 30% (lease cost absorbed in deduction)
- Company driver (less common): 22% - 28%
- Owner-operator (common): 70% - 90%

**Pros:**
- Drivers benefit when freight rates are high
- Skin in the game — fleet + driver aligned on rate
- Owner-operators love it

**Cons:**
- Drivers lose when freight rates drop
- Settlement statements complicated
- Disputes over what's "gross" (deductions confusing)

### 3. Hourly

Driver paid by clock-time, regardless of miles.

**Typical hourly rates:**
- Entry-level: $18-22/hour
- Experienced: $22-30/hour
- Specialty (hazmat, oversize, tanker): $25-40/hour
- Sleeper-team night driver: shift differential added

**Pros:**
- Compensates for detention time, delays, traffic
- Driver doesn't feel pressure to speed for more miles
- Compliance-friendly (drivers don't push HOS limits for pay)

**Cons:**
- Bookkeeping complexity (FLSA tracking)
- Drivers may run slower; lower revenue per truck
- Less alignment with delivery efficiency

### 4. Salary

Fixed weekly/monthly pay regardless of miles or hours.

**Typical salaries:**
- Entry-level CDL driver: $45,000 - $60,000/year
- Experienced regional: $60,000 - $85,000/year
- Long-haul OTR: $70,000 - $100,000/year
- Specialized (chemicals, oversize, military hauling): $90,000+/year

**Pros:**
- Predictable income for driver
- Simple administration
- Good for short-haul local drivers
- Common for dispatchers + safety mgrs (non-driving)

**Cons:**
- Less motivation to drive more
- Doesn't reflect actual productivity
- High retention cost if performance varies

### 5. Hybrid / Tiered

Common variations:
- **Base + bonus** — salary or hourly with quarterly safety bonus
- **CPM + safety bonus** — CPM with bonus for clean inspections, no claims
- **Percentage + minimum guarantee** — POR with floor that protects driver in slow weeks
- **Sliding-scale CPM** — higher CPM for difficult lanes (mountains, weather)

## Detention pay

Time spent at shipper/receiver beyond the included free time. Most carriers pay detention starting at:
- **2 hours grace**: detention starts after 2 hours
- **Rate**: $10-25/hour after grace
- **Daily cap**: typically $200/day

Detention is increasingly tracked + billed back to shippers when applicable. Carriers should pay drivers detention they're getting paid for.

## Bonus structures

Common driver bonuses:
- **Safety bonus** — $200-500/month for clean inspections + no claims
- **Retention bonus** — $1,000-$10,000 paid at hire, 1-year, 2-year anniversaries
- **Recruitment bonus** — $1,000-$5,000 for referring new drivers
- **Fuel mileage bonus** — for drivers achieving target MPG
- **Holiday pay** — premium rate for working holidays
- **Stop pay** — $30-50 per stop beyond first 2 in a multi-stop load

## FLSA & state wage law

Under FLSA, certain commercial driving operations have **exemptions**:

- **Motor Carrier Act Exemption (MCA)** — drivers operating in interstate commerce + > 26,001 lb GVWR are exempt from FLSA overtime
- This means CPM, POR, hourly, salary all OK without overtime requirements (federally)
- BUT state law varies significantly:

| State | Driver Wage Law |
|---|---|
| California | Different — drivers ARE covered by CA wage/hour; overtime applies |
| Washington | Some restrictions; overtime applies in some configurations |
| Most states | MCA exemption applies; no overtime requirement |

California specifically: drivers may need:
- Itemized wage statements
- Meal + rest breaks (with paid time for breaks)
- Overtime for hours > 8/day OR > 40/week

US carriers operating into California with California-domiciled drivers must comply with CA wage law.

## Common driver-pay mistakes

1. **Treating CPM drivers as exempt without verifying MCA eligibility.** Drivers operating in intrastate + small vehicle = NOT exempt; must follow FLSA.
2. **Not paying detention** even when shipper paid for it.
3. **Deductions making net pay less than minimum wage.** Federally + state-protected. Carriers can't deduct so much that net pay falls below minimum.
4. **Bonus structures that effectively pay below CPM rate.** "Bonus" that converts to monthly check looks like base pay; could trigger overtime requirements.
5. **California operations without CA-compliant wage structure.** Class action lawsuits possible.

## Recruitment & retention

Driver-pay competitiveness varies by:
- Region (CA + WA pay more than rural states)
- Lane (long-haul OTR vs regional vs local)
- Commodity (oversize + hazmat command premiums)
- Schedule (home daily vs home weekly vs OTR)
- Equipment (newer truck = retention factor)

A 2-3 cent CPM gap to competitor = significant retention risk. A 10% retention bonus paid quarterly often beats a 1 cent CPM raise on overall cost.

## Owner-operator vs company driver economics

| | Owner-Operator | Company Driver |
|---|---|---|
| Truck ownership | Driver owns | Carrier owns |
| Pay model | POR (typically) | CPM/Salary/Hourly |
| Insurance | Driver pays | Carrier pays |
| Fuel | Driver pays | Carrier pays |
| Maintenance | Driver pays | Carrier pays |
| 401k/Benefits | None typically | Yes typically |
| Risk | Driver bears | Carrier bears |
| Take-home | Higher per load | Lower per load, stable |
